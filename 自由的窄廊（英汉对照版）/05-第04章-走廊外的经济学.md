# Economics Outside the Corridor · 第4章　走廊外的经济学

## The Ghost in the Granary · 谷仓里的鬼魂

In 1972, the anthropologist Elizabeth Colson was in the midst of her fieldwork among the Gwembe Tonga, a people of southern Zambia who had been stateless prior to the conquest of the region by the British (see Map 5 in the previous chapter). She was at a homestead collecting information when a woman entered and asked the resident housewife for grain. Both women were members of the same clan, but they lived quite far apart and only knew each other vaguely. In response to the request the housewife went to her granary and filled the visitor’s basket until it was overflowing and the visitor departed satisfied.

> 一九七二年，人类学家伊莉莎白．柯尔森（Elizabeth Colson）正在格温贝东加人（Gwembe Tonga）中进行田野调查。格温贝东加人住在东非尚比亚南部，在英国人征服这个区域前，一直是属于无国家状态（参见前一章的地图五）。她在一座农场里搜集资讯时，一位妇女走进来，向住在农场里的家庭主妇索取粮食。两位妇女属于同一个部族，住所却距离很远，彼此不大熟悉。这位家庭主妇听到请求后，走去谷仓，把客人的篮子装到满出来，客人满意地离开了。

This type of generous sharing of food within clans, kin, or other types of groups is common among many stateless societies. It is interpreted by most anthropologists and many economists as a sign of deep-rooted customs and norms of cooperation and reciprocity. It also has a clear economic logic: today you help somebody from your clan, and tomorrow when you need it, they will help you. This is exactly how Colson initially interpreted this ostentatious generosity.

> 在没有国家机器的社会中，部族、亲属或其他形态的团体之间，这样慷慨地分享食物很常见。大部分人类学家和很多经济学家把这种事，解释为代表根深柢固的习俗和互惠合作的规范。这样做也有一种清楚的经济逻辑：今天你帮忙同一部族的人，明天你有需要时，他们会帮忙你。柯尔森最初正是这样解释这种仿佛炫耀式的慷慨。

It was only later, when she observed a young man in the village receiving a disturbing letter from home, that Colson came to understand that the reality was rather different. She recorded in her field notes, “One evening, lights had been seen about his granary and his wives and brother had later found evidence that ghosts had urinated over the grain, an act which Tonga believe ghosts carry out only if sent by a sorcerer.”

> 一直到后来，柯尔森看到村里的一位年轻人，收到家里一封令人不安的信后，才知道实际情况大不相同。她在田野调查笔记中写道：「某一天晚上，他的谷仓透出灯光，他的妻子和弟弟后来发现鬼在谷物上撒尿的证据。东加人认为只有巫师派出的鬼，才会做这种事情。」

The likely aim of the sorcerer was to kill the man and his family. He lamented that “his ambition of the previous year—which had led him to work early and late in the large field he had planted—was now bringing him only a harvest of hate.” The same housewife, who was present, immediately grasped what was going on. Colson went on: “She thought someone must have visited his homestead and seen all the granaries, and she asked if he could remember anyone having stood to gaze at them or asking for food or making any comment about all that food.” The housewife concluded:

> 巫师的目标可能是要杀死这个男人和他家人，男主人感叹地说：「我前一年早出晚归、耕种大片土地的野心，现在收获的却是仇恨。」在场的那位家庭主妇立刻了解怎么回事，柯尔森继续记道：「她认为一定有人来过他们的农场，看过所有谷仓。她还问男主人，是否记得有人曾经站在谷仓前面凝视，或是索取食物，或对食物说过什么话。」这位家庭主妇的结论是：

It is not safe to deny them. You saw me give grain to that woman who came the other day. How could I refuse when she asked me for grain? Perhaps she would do nothing, but I could not tell. The only thing to do is to give. Not giving risked sorcery and violence. It was fear, fear from retribution and violence that would follow if she broke the norm, not some abstract notion of generosity, that made her give.

> 拒绝他们并不安全。你看到我送谷子给那天来的那位妇女，她索讨谷子时，我怎么可能拒绝？她可能什么事情都没有做，但是我无法分辨，我唯一该做的事情就是赠予。不赠予会有招来巫术和暴力对付的风险。促使她赠予的原因，不是某种乐善好施的抽象观念，而是恐惧，是如果她违反规范，会遭到报复和暴力对待的恐惧。

This type of threat was endemic in Tonga society and was even embodied in the structure of its clans. The neighboring Plateau Tonga, for example, have fourteen clans, each of which has associated “totems”—animals that shouldn’t be eaten. The totems of the Bayamba clan are the hyena, rhinoceros, pig, ant, and fish. Members of this clan cannot eat any of these animals. The Batenda clan has the elephant, sheep, and hippopotamus as its totems.

> 这种威胁在东加社会很流行，甚至表现在部族结构上。例如，附近的高原东加人有十四个部族，每个部族都有「图腾」，象征不该吃掉的动物。贝延巴（Bayamba）部族的图腾是土狼、犀牛、猪、蚂蚁和鱼，表示该部族成员不能吃这些动物；巴腾达（Batenda）部族的图腾则是大象、绵羊和海马。

Other forbidden animals include leopard (the Bansaka clan), frog (Bafumu clan), and even the white vulture (Bantanga clan). The origins of these food prohibitions, according to Tonga legend, is that long ago some groups of people were eating leopard, and other people became envious of the plentiful food they had and cursed them, making them subsequently allergic to the specific food. The descendants of the cursed people became the clan.

> 其他禁止食用的动物包括班沙卡部族（Bansaka）禁吃豹子，巴夫姆部族（Bafumu）禁吃青蛙，班坦加部（Bantanga）禁吃白秃鹰。根据东加人的传说，这些食物禁忌的来源是很久以前，有些群体的人吃豹子，其他群体的人嫉妒他们有这么丰盛的食物，并且诅咒他们，害他们后来对这种特定食物过敏。受到诅咒的人后代就变成了这个特定部族。

So the norms of hospitality and generosity among the Tonga had emerged and were obeyed not so much because of some moral imperative or because people saw economic benefits stemming from them, but because they feared violence and sorcery, not to mention the social ostracism and other less violent retributions that would be exacted on those deviating from the norms. They lived in a society without a state, so there were no police or government officials who could protect them or resolve disputes. Nevertheless, because of the norms preventing and containing conflict, they didn’t suffer endemic violence. The visitor came, the housewife was generous, and any potential conflict was avoided.

> 因此，东加人会出现和遵守好客与慷慨的规范，倒不完全是因为道德上的强制规定，或是因为人们看出其中会产生经济利益，而是因为大家害怕暴力和巫术，更不用说还害怕社会的排斥，也害怕施加在背离规范的人身上、暴力程度比较低的其他报复。他们活在没有国家的社会中，因此没有警察或政府官员，可以保护他们或解决纠纷。他们依靠社会规范来防止和遏止冲突，才免于暴力肆虐。宾客光临，家庭主妇慷慨招待，以避免任何可能的冲突。

## No Place for Industry · 产业没有地位

Hobbes thought that without centralized authority societies would find themselves in a condition of Warre. He also anticipated, as we saw in Chapter 1, that Warre would destroy economic incentives, writing “in such condition there is no place for industry, because the fruit thereof is uncertain.” Or put in the language of modern economics, conflict and uncertainty mean that individuals do not have secure property rights on the fruits of their investments and on what they produce, gather, or hunt, and this discourages economic activity.

> 霍布斯认为，没有中央权威的社会将会处在战争状态的不利状况中。他也像我们在第一章中看到的一样，预期战争状态会摧毁经济诱因，他写道：「在这种情况下，产业会没有地位，因为其中没有成果。」用现代经济学的语言来说，冲突和不确定性表示，个人的投资成果不会获得稳固的私有财产权，人们生产、搜集、捕猎的成果也是如此。这种情况会阻挠经济活动。

To get a sense of the economic consequences of Warre, let’s return briefly to the Democratic Republic of Congo. At the start of Chapter 1 we mentioned that the east of the country, particularly the Kivus, is plagued by militias and armed groups. Indeed, in the Kivus, Warre isn’t just between individuals, as Hobbes often seems to suggest, but between groups.

> 为了了解战争状态对经济的影响，我们要回头谈谈刚果民主共和国。我们在第一章开始时，提到这个国家的东部，尤其是南基伍省和北基伍省〔合称基伍地区（Kivus）〕受到多种民兵组织和武装团体的侵扰。事实上，基伍地区的战争状态似乎像霍布斯经常暗示的那样，不仅存在于个人与个人之间而已，也存在于不同团体之间。

One of these in eastern Congo is the RCD-Goma, based in the city of Goma. This group was a splinter faction of the Congolese Rally for Democracy (in French, Rassemblement Congolais pour la Démocratie, hence the abbreviation RCD), and had its roots in the First and Second Congo Wars, sometimes called the Great War of Africa, between 1996 and 2003. When a peace agreement was finally signed, the RCD-Goma, along with many others, kept fighting and terrorizing local residents.

> 其中一个团体叫做「刚果民主联盟—戈马派」（RCD-Goma），这个团体的基地设在戈马市，是从刚果民主联盟分裂出来的一个支派，起源可以追溯到第一次与第二次刚果战争（偶尔有人把发生在一九九六年至二○○三年之间的这场战争，叫做「非洲大战」）。和平协定最后签署时，刚果民主联盟—戈马派和很多其他派系继续交战，并且用恐怖手段对付当地居民。

In December 2004, RCD-Goma units approached the town of Nyabiondo in North Kivu. The town was protected by members of another militia, the Mai-Mai. The RCD attacked on December 19. Mai-Mai fighters who were caught were tied up and burned alive. Initially civilian casualties were low, as under the cover of early-morning mist people managed to flee to the fields and forest. But the RCD hunted them down. Within a few days 191 people had been murdered in cold blood. Willy, aged just fifteen, told a researcher from Amnesty International:

> 二○○四年十二月，戈马派中的多个单位前进北基伍省的尼亚比安杜市（Nyabiondo），该市由称为「马伊马伊派」（Mai-Mai）的另一个民兵组织保护。十二月十九日，戈马派发动攻击，被捕的马伊马伊派战士被人绑起来，活生生烧死。起初平民的伤亡人数很低，因为居民利用清晨雾色的掩护，设法逃到田野和森林中，但戈马派却搜捕他们，几天之内，就有一百九十一位平民，遭到冷血残杀。只有十五岁的威利告诉国际特赦组织的一位研究员说：

The soldiers came in vehicles and on foot, killing and pillaging. Some were in uniform but others wore civilian clothes . . . The population fled straight to the forest. I was in a group of fifteen, with my mother, neighbours and other relatives. The soldiers found us and made us lie on the ground, where we were beaten with rifle butts. Baroki, the [local chief] was with us. The soldiers came and took him away, I saw that. Then I saw his body afterwards, a week later, on 25 December. He had taken a bullet in the head. He had been tied up and whipped. The body lay on the ground.

> 士兵坐车和徒步进来屠杀、抢掠，有的穿着制服，有的穿着便服……民众直接逃进森林里。我跟妈妈、邻居和其他亲戚，跟着一群十五岁的人一起逃。士兵发现我们，叫我们躺在地上，用枪托打我们，地方上的领袖巴洛奇跟我们在一起，士兵过来把他带走，我看到了这一幕。然后，过了一星期后，到了十二月二十五日，我看到了他的尸体，他的头部吃了一颗子弹；他被人绑着，被人鞭打过，尸体躺在地上。

Girls as young as eight were raped and 25,000 people were ultimately displaced. Their belongings were destroyed and their houses burned to the ground. Nyabiondo was systematically looted with even the tiles being stolen from rooftops. This is Warre with obvious and wrenching human consequences. The economic consequences are just as evident. The economy of the Kivus is devastated and the same is true for much of the rest of the Congo. The result is abject poverty.

> 才八岁的小女孩就遭到强奸。最后有二万五千人流离失所，他们的财产遭到摧毁，屋子被烧成平地。尼亚比安杜遭到有系统的掠夺，连屋顶上的瓦片都被偷走。这就是「战争状态」，明显具有令人痛苦的人道影响。经济影响同样明确，基伍地区的经济遭到摧毁，刚果的其他大部分地区也一样，其结果就是赤贫。

Income per capita in the Congo is about 40 percent of the level it was in 1960 when the country became independent. At about $400, less than 1 percent of the U.S. level, it’s as poor as any country in the world. Life expectancy is twenty years less than it is in the United States. In the Democratic Republic of the Congo, Hobbes was right. The life of men is indeed “poore, nasty, brutish, and short.” It is even worse for women.

> 刚果的人均所得大约为四百美元，大约只有一九六○年独立时的百分之四十，不到美国人均所得水准的百分之一，是世界上最贫穷的国家之一。刚果人平均寿命大约比美国少二十岁。以刚果民主共和国的情况来说，霍布斯说对了。男人的生活「孤独、贫困、难受、残酷、匮乏」，女人更惨。

Yet the Tonga don’t look anything like the Kivus or like the type of stateless, warring peoples Hobbes portrayed. They resemble the kind of society that would support claims that we are a “cooperative species,” with norms that support cooperation, hospitality, and generosity. But as we have already seen in Chapter 1, the price that people pay for this is often being locked into the cage of norms. Yet now we come to understand that the cage constrains not only their social choices, but also their economic lives.

> 但是，东加人看来一点也不像基伍地区的人，或霍布斯所描绘的那种没有国家、互相交战的人；他们具有支持合作、好客和慷慨的规范，比较像可以支持我们是「互相合作物种」说法的那种社会。但就像我们在第一章看到的那样，人民也为这种状况支付代价，经常被固锁在规范牢笼里。我们现在知道牢笼不但限制他们的选择，也会限制他们的经济生活。

This is clear for the young man who had worked hard only to see his granaries attacked by ghosts and sorcerers, because people were jealous of his success. The adverse effects of these norms for economic incentives are not confined to such attacks. They also impede property rights, even if this takes a less obvious form than that which Hobbes anticipated would plague the economy of stateless societies. Suppose, for example, you invest and increase your production. With secure property rights, you would get to enjoy the greater output you produce, and you may do what you please with it. If you really enjoy being generous, that’s part of the reward.

> 对于努力工作、却因为别人嫉妒自己的成功，看到自己的谷仓遭到鬼魂或巫术攻击的人来说，情况很清楚。这种社会规范对经济诱因的不利影响，不仅止于害怕遭到于这种攻击而已，也会妨碍财产权，即使这种妨碍不如霍布斯所预期、会侵害无国家社会的经济那么明显，仍然会有所妨碍。例如，假设你投资增产，在拥有安全财产权的情况下，你会享受你所生产的较大产出，你也可以随心所欲地支配你的产出，如果你确实乐善好施，这就是报酬的一部分。

But in Tonga society, as in many other economies shaped by the cage of norms, you give that output away not because you enjoy it, but because you are afraid of the social retribution, and even the violence, you may suffer if you fall afoul of the norms. That effectively means you again do not have secure property rights, since the additional income you generate will be taken away from you, even if the taking looks voluntary, couched in customs of generosity. The consequence would be little different from what Hobbes observed; there would be “no place for industry.”

> 但是，在东加社会中，你会像很多由规范牢笼所塑造的其他经济体那样，你交出产出，不是因为你喜欢这样做，而是因为你害怕社会的报复，甚至担心暴力的报复；你担心如果自己抵触规范，你就可能受害。这表示你在实际上并没有可靠的财产权，因为你创造的额外所得会被人拿走，即使拿走的行为是以乐善好施的习俗来表现，情况还是一样，结果跟霍布斯的说法几乎没有什么不同：「产业没有地位。」

The obvious way that this manifested itself in Tonga society was in the constant presence of hunger. Hunger and begging. Colson noted how close people lived to the edge: When a household has exhausted its own food supplies or is near to doing so, it attempts first to obtain food from kinsmen or friends who live nearby. Children and disabled people are sent to kinsmen in areas which still have food.

> 这造成一个明显的结果，就是让东加社会长年处在挨饿状态。饥饿和乞讨持续存在，柯尔森指出人民多么濒临这种险境：家庭粮食供应告罄或即将竭尽时，他们首先会设法从住在附近的亲戚朋友处，取得食物；并把孩子和残障的家人送到还有食物供应地区的亲戚那里。

Men go out to work to leave more food for those who must stay at home, but they take little comfort in the thought that they will be fed while at home there will be hunger. When local supplies are exhausted, people walk many miles to tap kinsmen living at a distance. Often they go to the Plateau where their appearance is looked upon with much the same enthusiasm as the settling of a swarm of locusts.

> 男性会外出工作，好把比较多的食物，留给必须留在家里的人，但是想到自己会有东西吃、留在家里的人却会挨饿，让他们根本高兴不起来。本地供应告罄时，人们会走很远的路，去向住在远处的亲戚求助，他们经常会去高原东加人住的地方，那里的人看到他们时，不会比看到一群蝗虫来定居还高兴多少。

The founding father of economics, Adam Smith, emphasized the propensity of humans to “truck, barter and exchange.” In Tongaland, begging was more prevalent than trucking, bartering, or exchanging. Colson pointed out how “the Valley had no middlemen or markets to organize internal trade. It also had no universally accepted medium of exchange.” There was trade of sorts, but

> 经济学之父亚当．斯密强调人类有「交易、易货和交换」的倾向。在东加，乞讨比交易、易货和交换盛行。柯尔森指出：「谷地没有中间商或市场推动内部交易，也没有人们普遍接受的交易媒介。」某些形式的交易的确存在，但是：

much of the exchange is obligatory, consequent upon institutionalized relationships existing between the parties to the transaction: one has the right to receive and the other the obligation to give. The result was a society trapped into subsistence agriculture, vulnerable to every sort of economic setback and adversity. Technology was stagnant and backward. Precolonial Tonga society did not use the wheel, either for pottery or transport.

> 大部分是义务性的交易，随着交易双方之间既有的制度性关系而来：一方有权接受，另一方有权给予。社会因此困在自给自足的农业中，容易受到各式各样的经济衰退和困境伤害，科技落伍，又停滞不前。殖民前的东加社会没有利用轮子，不论制陶还是运输都是。

Farming, the economic mainstay in the area, was unproductive, not because the land was infertile, but because it was worked using digging sticks rather than plows. As we have already seen, the origins of the Tonga cage of norms is not unrelated to Hobbes’s observations. Part of the reason why such norms have developed in many places is that egalitarianism has a clear political logic. Norms of egalitarianism maintain the status quo. When such norms are weak or nonexistent, hierarchy emerges, the slippery slope kicks in, and statelessness ends.

> 农业是这个地区的经济支柱，但生产力低落，原因不是土地不肥沃，而是耕作时采用木棍松土，而不是用犁犁田。我们已经看到，东加规范牢笼的起源跟霍布斯的说法无关。这种规范会在很多地方发展，原因之一是平等主义具有明确的政治逻辑，平等主义的规范会维持现状；当这种规范虚弱无力或不存在时，阶级就会出现，滑坡跟着出现，结束没有国家的状态。

The surviving stateless societies thus tend to be those where norms of egalitarianism are strong and ingrained. These norms also help control conflict. If conflict risks erupting into violence and feuds, it’s better to follow a closely scripted economic playbook. With new economic activities, new opportunities, and new inequalities, there will be new conflicts, which are much harder for existing norms to handle. Better not to risk tipping into Warre. Better to stay with the status quo.

> 因此，幸存的无国家社会通常都是平等主义规范强而有力、根深柢固的地方。这些规范也有助于控制冲突，如果冲突有爆发成暴力和争执的风险，人们最好遵循慎重编定的经济规章。新的经济活动、新机会和新的不平等会带来新的冲突，是现有规范非常难以处理的东西，人们最好不要冒险进入战争状态中，最好留在现状中。

## The Caged Economy · 鸟笼经济

This is of course very familiar from the Tiv. As you’ll recall from Chapter 2, the Tiv developed a set of norms to make sure they never got onto the slippery slope. Anyone who tried to accumulate power to start exerting authority over others was put back in his place by witchcraft accusations. It turns out that their norms and the cage they created also did the same economically, effectively forging a “caged” economy.

> 蒂夫族想必非常熟悉这种情况。读者可以回想一下第二章，蒂夫族发展出一套规范，确保自己永远不会走上滑坡。凡是想增加权力、开始对别人发号施令者都会遭到施展巫术的指控，被打回原形。实际上，他们的规范和创造的牢笼，在经济上也有同样的效果，有效形成一种「鸟笼」经济。

The Tiv were a society based on kin and descent. As we have seen, the Tiv word for a territory occupied by the descendants of a single ancestor is tar, and within a tar it was the elders who exercised what little authority there was in traditional Tiv society. They allocated sufficient land to people within the tar to provide a livelihood for their family. Sufficient, but not much more than that. As anthropologists Paul and Laura Bohannan noted, if a man “wants to plant many more yams than his wives and children need, so that he can sell them to get more money and goods than others in the compound, he is likely to be refused.”

> 蒂夫族是以亲属和血统关系为基础的社会。我们已经看到，蒂夫族语言中，描述单一祖先后代所占领领土的字是「塔尔」（tar）。在一块「塔尔」里，发挥传统蒂夫族社会仅存的极少权力的人是各位长老，他们分配足够的土地，给这块「塔尔」里的人民，好让他们刚好维持家人温饱。人类学家布哈南夫妇指出，如果一个男人「想种出比妻子儿女所需还多很多的山药，以便出售，得到比住这一区的其他人还多的钱和物品，那他可能会遭到排挤」。

There was also no market in Tivland where labor could be bought and sold, and no land or capital markets either. The labor of the family or the tar was the only thing available for agricultural production. Both men and women farmed, but they each farmed specific crops and only women grew the main staple, yams. Husbands were required to provide land for their wives to farm, but had no automatic claim on what they produced. There were markets for some of these produced goods, but the Bohannans remarked: Perhaps the most characteristic feature of the Tiv market is that it is extraordinarily constrained and shows little tendency to invade the other institutions of society.

> 蒂夫社会里没有可供买卖劳动力的市场，也没有土地或资本市场。家人的劳动或「塔尔」是唯一能够用来生产农产品的工具，男人和女人都要耕种，但彼此只能种植特定的作物，只有女人可以种山药这种主食，丈夫必须提供妻子土地，却不能自动宣称拥有妻子所生产的东西，这些农产品有一些市场，但布哈南夫妇指出：或许蒂夫族市场最特别的地方，是受到极为严格的限制，几乎没有显示要侵入其他社会制度的倾向。

Indeed, the Tiv didn’t have free markets, they had markets that were caged—structured not to facilitate trade but to avoid the slippery slope. Perhaps the most obvious instance of this is with respect to notions of the exchange of goods, which was strictly limited. The economy was separated into different spheres. One could trade within a sphere but not across spheres. The most flexible sphere was the market for foodstuffs and subsistence goods that included chickens, goats, sheep, household utensils, and other craft products (mortars, grindstones, calabashes, pots, and baskets). Raw materials used to produce any of these items would also be included here. These were traded on local markets that opened periodically, and prices were flexible and subject to haggling.

> 的确如此，蒂夫族没有自由市场，他们的市场都关在牢笼里──不是为了促进交易，而是为了避免滑坡。这一点最明显的例子，可能是跟商品交换受到严格限制有关的观念，他们的经济体分成不同的领域，你可以在一个领域中交易，却不能在另一个领域中交易，最有弹性的市场是食物和日常用品市场，交易标的包括鸡、山羊、绵羊、家庭用品，和其他手工艺品（臼、磨石、葫芦、罐子和篮子）用来生产上述任何产品的原料也会包括在内，这些东西都在定期开放的本地市场中销售，价格具有弹性，可以讨价还价。

Trading in such markets had already adapted to the availability of money. Nevertheless, this sphere remained completely separate from prestige goods, which could not be exchanged in a market. Prestige goods included cattle, horses, a special type of white cloth known as tugundu, medicines, magical items, and brass rods. Formerly slaves were also included in this category of goods. Money was not used in this sphere, but there were equivalences between different goods. For example, historically the price of slaves was quoted in cows and brass rods, the price of cattle in terms of brass rods and tugundu cloth.

> 这种市场的交易已经适应货币的供应，却跟声望市场完全不同，声望商品不能在市场上交易，声望商品包括牛、马、一种称为「图根杜」（tugundu）的特殊类型白布、药品、法术用品和铜棒。以前奴隶也列在这一类的商品中，这种领域里不利用货币，不同的商品之间，却有等价关系，例如，历史上，奴隶的价格是用牛只和铜棒计算，牛只的价格是用铜棒和图根杜白布的价格计算。

Akiga, the Tiv elder we encountered in Chapter 2, mentions some specific ways in which various prestige items could be traded: “You could buy one iron bar for a tugundu cloth. In those days five tugundu cloths were equivalent to a bull. A cow was worth ten tugundu. One brass rod was worth about the same as one tugundu cloth; thus five brass rods were worth a bull.” To the extent that something like modern-looking exchange happened, the terms of trade were strictly fixed and unchanging. Although these prestige items might be exchanged in this way, this did not mean they were bought or sold, or as the Bohannans put it, “Tiv will not buy cows or horses in a market.”

> 我们在第二章里看到的蒂夫族长老阿奇佳，提到有一些特殊方法，可以交易各种声望商品：「你可以用一块图根杜白布，买一支铁棒，当年五块图根杜白布等于一头公牛，一头母牛等于十块图根杜白布，一支铜棒的价值大约等于一块图根杜白布；因此，五支铜棒的价值等于一头公牛。」从物品多少有点像在现代式交易所中交易的角度来看，蒂夫族的交易条件十分严格，而且固定不变，虽然这些声望商品或许可以用这种方式交易，却不表示这种产品可以买卖，或者如同布哈南夫妇所言：「蒂夫族不会在市场上购买母牛或马匹。」

How do you acquire prestige goods then? Moving from subsistence goods to prestige goods was a process that the Bohannans dubbed “conversion.” The Tiv recognized that acquiring subsistence goods could be the fruit of hard work, but not so for prestige goods. It took “more than hard work—it takes a ‘strong heart.’” Conversion upward may be possible when someone who has to get rid of his existing prestige goods is therefore willing to convert down. But “they try to keep a man from making conversions,” because such a man “is both feared and respected. If he is strong enough to resist excessive demands of his kinsmen . . . he is feared as a man of special, potentially evil, talents (tsav).”

> 这样的话，你要怎么得到声望商品呢？从谋生用品跨进声望商品，是布哈南夫妇所说的「转换」过程。蒂夫族认为，获得谋生用品可能是勤奋工作的成果，声望商品却不是这样，获得声望商品「不仅需要勤奋工作而已，还需要『强大的心意』」。有人必须把现有声望商品脱手，因此乐于向下转换时，向上转换才可能出现。但是，「他们会设法阻止某一个人从事转换」，因为这种人「是大家既害怕又尊敬的人，如果他够坚强，能够抗拒亲属的过分要求……大家怕他可能是特别邪恶又能力高强的人（萨夫）」。

There we have it, back to tsav! The Tiv norms caged the economy, eradicated factor markets, and made the lineage the main agent of production. In return, they achieved a balance between the different kin groups, avoided the slippery slope, and made their status quo much more enduring. Like the enforced generosity of the Tonga, the caged economy of the Tiv had obvious adverse consequences. Markets are critical for an efficient organization of the economy and for prosperity. But they weren’t permitted to function among the Tiv. To the extent that trade happened, relative prices were often fixed.

> 我们又碰到萨夫，又回到萨夫上了！蒂夫族的规范拘限了经济，消除了要素市场，还把血统变成主要的生产代理人，得到的好处是在不同的亲属团体之间获得平衡，避免了滑坡，使现状变得更能持久。蒂夫族的鸟笼经济像东加人强制性的慷慨一样，产生了明显的不利影响，市场攸关经济组织的效能和繁荣，蒂夫族却不容许市场发挥作用，就交易发生时的状况而言，相对价格经常是固定的，蒂夫族得到的结果跟东加人一样，都是赤贫。

The result in Tivland, just as for the Tonga, was dire poverty. The institutions of Tiv society created little incentive for capital accumulation, other than in the form of simple instruments such as digging sticks and devices for processing food. Indeed, even saving could lead one to be accused of having the wrong sort of tsav, so the fear of retribution made accumulation too dangerous. As a result, at the time of the conquest of Tivland by the British, people were living at close to subsistence levels of income with life expectancy of around thirty years.

> 蒂夫族的社会制度几乎不曾为资本的累积，创造过任何激励诱因，只有松土棍和食品加工器材等简单工具是例外。事实上，连储蓄都可能导致个人遭到具有不当萨夫精神的指控，害怕受到惩罚的心理使累积财富变得太危险，因此，英国人征服蒂夫兰时，蒂夫族的所得勉强可以维持生计，平均寿命大约为三十岁。

## Ibn Khaldun and the Cycle of Despotism · 赫勒敦和专制周期

Our discussion of the Tonga and the Tiv suggests that Hobbes’s analysis of the economic consequences of statelessness wasn’t exactly right. These societies weren’t mired in continual violence and conflict destroying all economic incentives, even if the Kivus in the Congo remind us that there are plenty of examples of societies without centralized authority locked into such conflicts. All the same, Hobbes’s conclusion turned out to be not completely off the mark, because the norms that these societies fashioned to control conflict ended up creating highly distorted incentives.

> 我们针对东加人和蒂夫族的讨论显示，霍布斯就无国家状态对经济影响的分析并不完全正确。即使刚果的蒂夫族提醒我们，有很多没有中央权威的社会深陷在这种冲突中，但这些社会并没有陷入持续不断的暴力，或是陷入摧毁所有经济诱因的冲突中。同时霍布斯的结论并非完全错误，因为这些社会为了控制冲突所塑造的规范，最后会创造出高度扭曲的诱因。

Was Hobbes also right that the Despotic Leviathan would be better for economic incentives because it would create security, predictability, and order? Hobbes was partly—and only partly—right, here too, and there is no better place to start understanding the double-edged nature of the economy that the Despotic Leviathan creates than the work of the great Arab scholar Ibn Khaldun. Born in Tunis in 1332 CE, Khaldun traced his ancestry back to Muhammad via Yemen. Khaldun had a remarkable life which included meeting the Mongol conqueror Tamerlane.

> 霍布斯说过：就经济诱因而言，专制国家会比较好，因为专制国家会带来安全、可预测性和秩序，这种说法正确吗？答案也是部分正确──只有部分正确而已。专制国家对经济发展是一把双面刃，若要了解这一点，最好要先看伟大阿拉伯学者伊本．赫勒敦（Ibn Khaldun）的著作。赫勒敦一三三二年生于突尼斯，先祖可以透过叶门，追溯到穆罕默德时代的贵族。赫勒敦的一生多采多姿，包括曾经和帖木儿汗国开国大汗帖木儿见面。

His most renowned scholarly work was the Kitab al-Ibar, or “Book of Lessons,” whose first volume, known as the Muqaddimah, or “Introduction,” is particularly useful for understanding the economic consequences of a despotic state. Khaldun’s “Introduction” is rich in ideas. In addition to tracing the economic consequences of the emergence of a state in the Arabian Peninsula, it presents a theory of the dynamics of political institutions based on what he viewed as two fundamental conflicts in society. The first is between the desert, with its nomadic life, and sedentary urbanized society. The second is between the rulers and the ruled.

> 他最著名的学术著作《世界通史》（Kitab al Ibar ），其中的第一卷《历史绪论》（Muqaddimah ）在了解专制国家对经济的影响方面，特别有用。《历史绪论》富含创意，除了追踪阿拉伯半岛出现一个国家对经济的影响之外，也根据他认定的社会两大根本冲突，提出政治制度动态理论：第一种冲突是沙漠游牧生活和久坐不动都市化社会之间的冲突，第二种是统治者和被统治者之间的冲突。

Khaldun argued that the odds are stacked in favor of the desert people in the first conflict due to the type of society that the harshness and marginal nature of desert life creates. Their society was characterized by what he called asabiyyah, which translates as social solidarity or group feeling. Asabiyyah should be a familiar concept by now. It was part of the cage of norms of a stateless society, but Khaldun brings in a new angle on these norms. From our perspective so far, asabiyyah was something that helped regulate conflicts and preserve the political equality in nomadic societies. Khaldun pointed out that it also makes such a society very good at subjugating neighboring sedentary peoples.

> 赫勒敦主张在第一种冲突中，因为沙漠严酷与边缘生活特性所创造社会形态的缘故，导致情势对沙漠人民有利，他们的社会具有他所说的「阿拉伯部族主义」（asabiyyah）特征，意思是社会团结或族群意识。现在人们对部族主义的观念应该已经很熟悉，部族主义应该是无国家社会规范牢笼的一部分，但赫勒敦对这些规范提出了新的角度。从我们到目前为止的看法来说，部族主义是协助游牧社会管制冲突、维护政治平等的东西，赫勒敦却指出，部族主义也使这种社会非常善于征服邻近的定居人民。

We saw in the previous chapter how Islam provided an edge to Muhammad in his state-building efforts. The Bedouin tribes that Muhammad relied on in this process had a lot of asabiyyah, and this gave him and his followers a second edge to expand the Islamic Empire into a massive empire. In Khaldun’s account, this edge was created not only by the economic hardship of the desert, but also by the dense networks of kinship that had evolved to provide mutual help in the tough desert landscape.

> 我们在前一章里，看到伊斯兰教在穆罕默德努力建国时，提供了很大的优势，穆罕默德在这种过程中仰赖的贝多因人（Bedouin）部落，具有浓厚的部族主义，让他和信徒在扩张伊斯兰帝国为巨大帝国时，掌握到第二种优势。根据赫勒敦的说法，这种优势不但是靠沙漠的经济艰困创造出来的，也是靠深厚亲属关系在艰困沙漠环境中促发的互助精神创造出来。

The desert was always destined to overcome the sedentary world and form new states and dynasties. Khaldun, however, argued that though asabiyyah helped the desert people to conquer “civilized lands” and set themselves up in power, the intrinsic dynamics of such authority inevitably led to the decay of asabiyyah and the ultimate collapse of a state that groups like the Bedouin founded. Then the whole cycle started over again with a new group from the desert replacing the collapsing state.

> 沙漠总是注定会征服久坐不动的世界，建立新的国家和王朝。然而，赫勒敦认为，虽然部族主义帮助沙漠居民征服「文明世界」，执掌大权，这种权威固有的力量势必将导致部族主义崩坏，最后造成贝多因人等族群建立的国家崩溃；然后，整个循环会重新开始，从沙漠出身的新族群会取代崩溃中的国家。

In Khaldun’s words: The duration of the life of a dynasty does not as a rule extend beyond three generations. The first generation retains the desert qualities, desert toughness, and desert savagery . . . the strength of group feeling continues to be preserved among them . . . the second generation changes from the desert attitude to sedentary culture . . . from a state in which everybody shared in the glory to one in which one man claims all the glory for himself . . . People become used to lowliness and obedience . . . The third generation . . . has completely forgotten the period of desert life . . . They have lost . . . group feeling, because they are dominated by force . . . When someone comes and demands something from them, they cannot repel him.

> 下文是赫勒敦的说法：一个王朝的生命存续期间通常不超过三代，第一代保留沙漠的特质、沙漠的韧性和沙漠的残忍无情……他们继续维护族群意识的力量……第二代从沙漠的态度变成久坐不动的文化……从每一个人共享荣耀的状态，变成一个人把所有荣光包揽在自己身上的状态……人民习于卑下和服从……第三代……已经完全忘记沙漠生活时期……丧失族群意识，因为他们受到武力宰割……有人来要胁他们时，他们无法把他击退。

Khaldun’s analysis also insightfully illustrates the role of conflict between the ruler and the ruled. After coming in from the desert, “one man claims all the glory for himself,” while the majority of people “become used to lowliness and obedience.” Khaldun gave a new dynasty about 120 years. Before delving into these political dynamics and their economic consequences in more detail, it’s worth picking up the history where we left off in the previous chapter and following what happened after the death of Muhammad.

> 赫勒敦的分析也深入说明了统治者和被统治者之间的冲突角色，从沙漠出来后，「一个人把所有荣光包揽在自己身上」，同时大部分人民「变成习于卑下和服从」。他估计新王朝的寿命大约有一百二十年。我们在更详细研究这些政治动态及其经济影响前，值得重拾前一章中中断的历史，探查穆罕默德经济扩张发生的事情。

The Arab conquests set in motion by Muhammad were initially sustained by four leaders, known as the Caliphs, who derived their authority from their close personal relationships with him. These were Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman, and Ali, Muhammad’s cousin and son-in-law, who was assassinated in 661. Ali’s murder came at the end of a period of contention over how the newly emerging state was to be governed. Uthman had tried aggressively to increase central control over the nascent state and had been murdered by rebellious soldiers. Ali’s succession was contested by Muawiya, Uthman’s cousin and the governor of Syria. This led to a protracted civil war, which ended with Ali’s death and Muawiya’s being declared caliph.

> 一开始时，穆罕默德率先推动的阿拉伯征服行动，由四位号称哈里发的领袖继续维持，他们的权威来自跟穆罕默德的密切关系，四位哈里发是阿布．巴克尔（Abu Bakr）、欧玛尔．宾．哈塔卜（Umar）、奥斯曼．宾．阿凡（Uthman）和穆罕默德的姪儿兼女婿、西元六六一年遭到暗杀的阿里（Ali）。阿里遭到暗杀时，正好这个新出现国家应该如何治理的争论期间结束。奥斯曼曾经设法大力提高中央对这个新国家的控制，却遭到叛变的士兵杀害；阿里的继承遭到奥斯曼的姪儿、叙利亚总督穆阿维亚一世（Muawiya）的质疑，这件事引发冗长的内战，到阿里死亡、穆阿维亚一世接任哈里发时，内战才结束。

He founded the Umayyad dynasty, which ruled for almost one hundred years until it was replaced in 750 by the Abbasid dynasty, named after Abbas, an uncle of Muhammad. By the time Umayyad rule emerged, Iran, Iraq, Syria, and Egypt had been conquered, and the annexation of North Africa, which was finally completed by 711, was well under way. By the middle of the eighth century, much of Spain was conquered and large swaths of inner Asia were added to the empire.

> 穆阿维亚一世建立了伍麦亚王朝（Umayyad），统治了将近一百年，才由根据穆罕默德叔叔阿巴斯（Abbas）名字命名的阿拔斯王朝（Abbasid）取代。到伍麦亚王朝的统治出现时，伊朗、伊拉克、叙利亚和埃及已经遭到征服，北非的兼并已经大力展开，到西元七一一年，终于完成并吞事功。到八世纪中叶，西班牙大部分领土遭到征服，亚洲内陆的大片「内亚」领土也并入这个帝国。

In the conquered lands, the Umayyads initially superimposed the rule of the Arab warrior class on top of the preexisting institutions of the Byzantine (in Syria, Palestine, Israel, Egypt) and Sassanid (in Iraq and Iran) Empires that they had replaced. It was only when Caliph Abd al-Malik assumed power in 685 that the Umayyads began to build a more distinct state structure based in their new capital in Damascus. But the Umayyads were never able to construct a truly effective centralized state, and neither were their successors, the Abbasids. Though their armies had proved to be hugely effective and occupied a large area, transforming this occupation into a real system of government and gaining the loyalty of the occupied people turned out to be a lot harder.

> 伍麦亚王朝在征服的土地上，开始把阿拉伯战士阶级的统治，加在他们取代的拜占庭帝国（统治的叙利亚、巴勒斯坦、以色列、埃及）以及萨珊王朝〔（Sassanid）治下的伊拉克和伊朗〕的旧制度上，一直到西元六八五年，阿卜杜勒．马利克（Abd al-Malik）哈里发掌权后，伍麦亚王朝才开始以新首都大马士革为基础，建立比较明显的国家结构。但是，伍麦亚王朝从来不曾能够建构真正有效的中央集权式国家，继承他们的阿拔斯王朝也一样，虽然他们的军队极其有效率，占领了广大的疆域，但要把占领区变成真正的政府制度，获得占领区人民的效忠，却难得多。

The Umayyads and Abbasids then ended up relying more and more on local elites to govern the provinces of their empire, raise taxes, and keep order. To buy the support of these elites, they engaged in “tax farming,” wherein they sold the right to raise taxes for a fixed sum of money. Once you had the right to tax granted to you by Damascus, and subsequently Baghdad when the Abbasids came to power, you had carte blanche to levy whatever taxes you wanted on local communities. This seems to have been a recipe for punitively high rates of taxation and the accumulation of land by elites since they took over the lands of people who could not afford to pay the taxes they levied.

> 伍麦亚王朝和阿拔斯王朝最后都必须愈来愈依赖当地菁英，统治他们帝国辖下的省份、课税和维持秩序。为了买通这些菁英的支持，他们采用「包税」制度，把课税的权利用一定的金额售出，一旦你取得由大马士革授予，以及后来阿拔斯王朝掌权后由巴格达授予的课税权，你就拿到了一张空白支票，可以对本地社区随心所欲地爱课多少、就课多少税，这种做法看来就是采用惩罚性高税率的起因，也是菁英搜刮土地的法门，因为纳税人如果无法缴纳他们所课的高额税负，菁英就会没收他们的土地。

This political structure of empire was ultimately self-defeating. Local elites demanded to be named as hereditary governors and recruited their own armies to provide order. Soon Baghdad had no control over them. The empire came apart at the seams and finally collapsed in 945. None of this was surprising to Khaldun. He was a firm believer in the will to power, noting that human beings need someone to act as a restraining influence and mediator in every social organization, in order to keep the members from fighting with each other. That person must, by necessity, have superiority over the others in the matter of group feeling. If not, his power to exercise a restraining influence could not materialize.

> 这种帝国的政治结构最后会自取灭亡，本地菁英要求取得世袭总督的头衔，可以召募私人武力，以便维持秩序，巴格达很快地就无法控制他们，帝国开始分崩离析，终于在西元九四五年瓦解。赫勒敦对这种事情一点也不觉得惊讶，坚决相信权力意志的他指出：人类的每一个社会组织，都需要有人，担任限制性的影响力量和仲裁人，以便阻止成员互相争斗，这种人必须在族群意识上，拥有高于其他人的权威，否则他发挥限制性影响力的权力不可能落实。

Once such a person was recognized, as Muhammad had been in Medina, he became a leader, and “leadership means being a chieftain, and the leader is obeyed, but he has no power to force others to accept his rulings.” But Khaldun understood that the mere existence of such a leader was likely to move society swiftly along the slippery slope. Indeed, “When a person sharing in the group feeling had reached the rank of chieftain and commands obedience, and when he then finds the way open toward superiority and the use of force, he follows that way.”

> 一旦这种人像穆罕默德在麦地那的情况那样得到承认，就会变成领袖，「领导代表成为酋长，大家服从这种领袖，但他没有权力强迫别人接受他的裁决」。但是，赫勒敦深知光是领袖的存在，可能推动社会快速的沿着滑坡移动。事实上，「分享族群意识的个人升到酋长的高阶，能够要求大家服从，然后发现通往权威和动用武力的大门洞开时，他一定会踏上这条路」。

Thus, because group feeling was not endowed equally on all people, it tended to inexorably lead to royal authority, which “means superiority and power by rule of force.” However, once in power the rulers of the new dynasty “will not need much group feeling to maintain their power. It is as if obedience to the government were a divinely revealed book that cannot be changed or opposed.” So, in line with his generational theory, Khaldun argued that the rulers of a dynasty started to distance themselves from those who had helped them into power and to forge relationships with new groups within their empire.

> 因此，因为族群意识并非平等的赋予所有人民，族群意识通常会无可阻挡地导向王者的权威，「意思就是变成利用武力统治的权威和权力」。然而，新王朝的统治者一旦掌权，「就不需要太多的族群意识来维持权力，服从政府会变成不能改变或反对的天启」。因此，赫勒敦根据他的三代理论，认为一个王朝的统治者会开始远离帮助他掌权的人，开始跟帝国内部的新团体建立关系。

This was an intrinsic part of creating an empire once you had conquered new lands. These lands were already occupied and often controlled by local elites and notables, so new dynasties had to come to some sort of agreement with them and obtain their loyalty or face constant rebellion. As the dynasty changed its nature and asabiyyah eroded, despotism emerged, or in Khaldun’s words, “With the disappearance of Arab group feeling and the annihilation of the Arab race and complete destruction of Arabism the Caliphate lost its identity. The form of government remained royal authority pure and simple.” The consequences of this were simple too:

> 这是你一旦征服新领土、创立帝国时固有的一部分历程，这些土地已经占领，而且经常受到当地菁英和贵族控制，因此新王朝必须跟他们达成某种协议，得到他们的效忠，否则就会面临不断的叛乱。随着这个王朝的性质改变，部族主义遭到侵蚀，专制开始出现，或者用赫勒敦的话来说：「阿拉伯族群意识消失，阿拉伯种族毁灭，阿拉伯主义遭到完全摧毁后，哈里发国丧失了自己的身分认同，政府的形式仍然维持纯粹且彻底的王权。」这种事情的后果也很单纯：

The restraining influence of religion had weakened. The restraining influence of government and group was needed. . . . Royal authority requires superiority and force . . . The decisions of the ruler will therefore, as a rule, deviate from what is right. They will be ruinous to the worldly affairs of the people under his control, since, as a rule, he forces them to execute his intentions and desires, which it may be beyond their ability to do. . . . Disobedience makes itself noticeable and leads to trouble and bloodshed.

> 宗教的限制性影响力削弱后，需要政府和团体的限制性影响力……王权需要威权和武力……因此统治者的决定通常会背离正确的道路，对他所控制人民的世俗事务会具有毁灭性，因为他通常会迫使人民超越本身的能力，执行他的意图和设计……不服从会自行表现出来，造成麻烦和流血。

Here Khaldun hints at some of the economic implications of the creation of a new dynasty. Early on with the power of group feeling and the “restraining influence of religion,” prosperity was a potential. But later, when “royal authority” consolidated itself, economic policies would be “ruinous to the worldly affairs of the people.” Nowhere were the economic implications of Khaldun’s generational theory more evident than in his discussion of taxation, which we turn to next.

> 赫勒敦的话暗示创造新王朝会具有某些经济意义，初期王朝笼罩在族群意识和「宗教限制性影响力」下，繁荣还有可能出现，但到了「王权」巩固后，经济政策应该会「对人民的世俗事务具有毁灭性」。赫勒敦贵不过三代的理论对经济的影响，表现最清楚的地方，莫过于他对下一节我们着墨的税收。

## Ibn Khaldun Discovers the Laffer Curve · 赫勒敦发现拉弗尔曲线

It should be known that at the beginning of the dynasty, taxation yields a large revenue from small assessments. At the end of the dynasty, taxation yields a small revenue from large assessments. With this statement Khaldun anticipated Reaganomics, the economic doctrines that were promulgated by the American president Ronald Reagan in the early 1980s. One of the plinths of Reaganomics was first sketched out on a napkin in the Two Continents Restaurant in Washington, D.C., by the economist Arthur Laffer.

> 大家应该知道的是，王朝开始时，课税会从少少的估价中，征得大笔税收，王朝结束时，会从大笔的估价中，征得少少的税收。赫勒敦以这种说法，预测到一九八○年代初期美国总统雷根发布的雷根经济学。雷根经济学的基础之一最先是在华盛顿两大洲餐厅（Two Continents Restaurant）的一张餐巾纸上，由经济学家亚瑟．拉弗尔（Arthur Laffer）画出来的。

Laffer was trying to explain to Republican rising stars Donald Rumsfeld and Dick Cheney and the journalist Jude Wanniski what he regarded as a basic principle of fiscal policy: the hump-shaped relationship between the tax rate levied by the government and the amount of tax revenues. When tax rates are low, an increase in the rate tends to increase tax revenues for the simple reason that the government takes a greater share of everyone’s incomes. However, as the tax rate starts to get too high, it chokes off incentives to work hard, exert effort, and invest because the gains created by all these activities are being taken away by the government.

> 拉弗尔试图向共和党政治新星唐纳．伦斯斐（Donald Rumsfeld）、狄克．钱尼（Dick Cheney）和新闻记者裘德．万尼斯基（Jude Wanniski），解释他心目中的财政政策基本原则，即税率和税收金额会呈现小丘状，税率低落时，提高税率通常会使税收增加；原因很简单，就是政府从每一个人所得中拿走的比率比较高。然而，税率升到太高时，就会扼杀大家努力工作、付出辛劳和投资的诱因，因为所有这些行动创造的利得，都会被政府拿走。

As a result, when tax rates get punitively high, not only economic activity but even tax revenues start falling. This is clear to see in the extreme cases where the tax rate approaches 100 percent, so that the government takes everything and very little incentive is left to generate income; despite the very high tax rates, there won’t be any tax revenues. Wanniski named this hump-shaped relationship the Laffer curve in honor of the man who had sketched it out. The exciting implication for Rumsfeld and Cheney was the prospect that you could reduce tax rates while increasing tax revenues as people responded to the more powerful incentives created by lower taxes—the biggest win-win situation of all time, which quickly came to be incorporated into President Reagan’s economic policy.

> 因此，税率高到吓人时，不但经济活动会开始降低，连税收也会开始减少；从税率接近百分之百的极端例子中，可以清楚地看出这种情形。因此，政府拿走一切，几乎没有留下什么创造所得的诱因；这时虽然税率非常高，却不会有任何税收。万尼斯基把这种小丘状的关系命名为「拉弗尔曲线」（Laffer curve），纪念画出这种曲线的拉弗尔。伦斯斐和钱尼从中得到令人兴奋的启示：你可以降低税率，同时可以增加税收，因为大家会对较低税率创造的比较有力诱因起反应──不久之后，这种有史以来最强大的双赢状况，就纳入雷根总统的经济政策中。

Needless to say, in a world where real tax rates are quite a bit lower than 100 percent, such as in the United States when Reagan became president, whether or not tax cuts actually increase tax revenues is open to doubt. Khaldun’s analysis of the economic dynamics created in the Middle East was on firmer empirical grounds, and his idea of the Laffer curve was somewhat different from the one that Laffer explained to Rumsfeld and Cheney. It was based on his generational theory.

> 无庸置疑的是，雷根出任美国总统时，实质税率跟百分之百相去相当远，在这种情况下，减税是否真的能够增加税收，的确值得怀疑。赫勒敦对中东所创立经济的分析，立足在比较坚实的实证基础上。他的拉弗尔曲线概念是以「贵不过三代的理论」为基础，跟拉弗尔对伦斯斐和钱尼解释的略有不同。

To start with, a new dynasty, since it still has asabiyyah, “imposes only the taxes as are stipulated by religious law, such as charity taxes, the land tax, and the poll tax.” This had beneficial effects on the economy because “when tax assessments and imposts upon the subjects are low, the latter have the energy and desire to do things. Cultural enterprises grow and increase . . . When cultural enterprises grow . . . the tax revenue, which is the sum total of the individual assessments increases.”

> 首先，新王朝开始时，仍然保有部族主义，「只课征宗教法律规定的租税，如慈善税、土地税和人头税」。这样对经济有好处，因为「加在臣民身上的税收估价和税课低落时，臣民就有精力和意愿做事，文化企业会成长和增加……文化企业成长时……个人税务估价增加之和的税收就会增加。」

Here Khaldun points out that low taxes stimulate economic activity, what he calls “cultural enterprises,” and this leads, as in the Laffer curve, to buoyant tax returns. The evidence suggests that this is exactly what happened in the wake of the Arab conquests. The Umayyads brought together a large area under one language, one religion, and one system of government, with a common legal system that flowed from Muhammad’s teachings.

> 赫勒敦指出，低税负会刺激他称之为「文化企业」的经济活动，会像拉弗尔曲线所显示的一样，带来丰厚的税收。证据显示，阿拉伯征服后，正好就是出现这种情形，伍麦亚王朝为广大地区带来相同的语言、宗教和政府制度，实施从穆罕默德教诲传承而来的共同司法制度。

The first and most obvious economic impact of this megastate was an expansion of trade and mercantile activities. Muhammad, after all, had started life as a trader. The geographer al-Muqaddasi compiled a tenth-century list of the items exchanged between Baghdad and Khorasan-Transoxania, in what is now northeastern Iran. The list starts with “11 different items of clothing and garments, including veils and turbans, all made of expensive cloth, sometime silk, sometime plain cloth as well as bracelets, clothing of hair of superior yarn, iron” from Naysabur, and “cloth, silk brocade of inferior quality, taffeta, raisins, syrup, steel, pistachios and confections” from Harat, and goes on for pages until “silver-coloured fabrics [simgun], and Samarqandi stuffs, large copper vessels, artistic goblets, tents, stirrups, bridle-heads and straps” from Samarqand.

> 这种超大国家为经济带来的最明显、最重要影响是交易和商业活动增加，穆罕默德毕竟是贸易商出身。地理学家穆卡达西（al-Muqaddasi）编辑了一份十世纪时，巴格达和今天伊朗东北部呼罗珊—河中地区（Khorasan-Transoxania）之间的贸易商品清单，这张清单开头就是「十一种不同品项的衣服和成衣，包括面纱和头巾，全都用昂贵的布料制成，偶尔是丝制品，偶尔是平纹布制品，加上手镯、高级纱线织成的头巾、内沙布尔（Naysabur）的铁，以及较低品质的布料、织锦、塔夫绸、葡萄干、糖浆、钢铁、开心果和赫拉特（Harat）的糕点」，产品列了非常多页，一直列到「银饰彩色纺织物（simgun）、撒马尔罕的产品、大型铜制器皿、艺术高脚杯、营帐、马镫、缰绳头和皮带。

This trade was accompanied by a vast amount of travel, the most powerful image of which was perhaps the annual pilgrimage to Mecca, the hadj, which brought hundreds of thousands of believers together from all across the empire, and provided a huge opportunity not just for piety, but also for trading. Another piece of evidence for the economic benefits of the rise of the Caliphate was an agricultural revolution. This was to some extent a consequence of the rise in trade, which created a much larger market than had existed before.

> 这种贸易伴随着大量旅行，其中最强而有力的形像可能是每年一次的麦加朝圣，朝圣把几十万信徒徒从帝国各处聚集在一起，不但为宗教事功带来新机会，也为贸易带来新商机。伊斯兰帝国崛起在经济上带来好处的另一个证据是农业革命，这件事多少是贸易勃兴、创造了比既有市场大很多的空前庞大市场带来的结果。

The Arab conquests were followed by the diffusion throughout the region of a variety of crops that had previously not been grown there. These included rice, sorghum, hard wheat, sugar cane, cotton, watermelons, eggplants, spinach, artichokes, sour oranges, lemons, limes, bananas, plantains, mangos, and coconut palms. Many of these plants, indigenous to the tropics, were not easy to grow in the cooler and drier regions into which they were now sown, and necessitated a major reorganization of agriculture.

> 阿拉伯征服促使各种作物，传播到帝国全境过去从来没有种植过的地方，这些作物包括稻米、高粱、硬麦、甘蔗、棉花、西瓜、茄子、菠菜、朝鲜蓟、酸橙、柠檬、莱姆、香蕉、大蕉、芒果和椰子树。这些作物中，有很多种是热带原生种，不容易在现在比较凉爽的地方栽种，需要针对农业，进行大规模的改组。

Previously, the growing season had been in the winter, with crops harvested in the spring. During the hot summer months, the land lay fallow. But the new crops came from tropical areas and thrived in the summers, so production was restructured and intensified. Before the Arab conquests of the Middle East, Byzantine practice was to crop the ground once every two years. Now two crops a year were grown, for example, winter wheat followed by summer sorghum, cotton, or rice.

> 过去作物是在冬季种植、春季收成，炎热的夏季就休耕，但新作物来自热带地区，夏季会蓬勃生长，因此生产需要改革和强化。阿拉伯征服中东前，拜占庭帝国的做法是两年种地一次，现在一年两作，例如，在夏季种植高粱、棉花或稻米后，冬季种植冬麦。

All of these innovations were documented in Arab farming manuals that helped to spread best practices throughout the empire. This intensification of production required fertilizer and irrigation too. Various types of irrigation were already in place in the areas that the Arabs conquered, but those of the Byzantine and Persian Sassanid Empires were often in advanced states of decay. The Arabs repaired these and built a profusion of new public infrastructure, including new kinds of dams, underground canals that tapped groundwater and brought it over long distances, and a variety of wheels for lifting water out of rivers, canals, wells, and storage basins. This infrastructural investment was not based on new technology but involved the adoption and deployment of existing technological know-how. Nevertheless, it greatly increased the productive capacity of the economy.

> 所有这些创新都记录在阿拉伯农业手册中，有助于把最好的做法传播到帝国全境。这种集约化生产也需要肥料和灌溉，阿拉伯人占领的地区已经设有各种形态的灌溉设施，但拜占庭和波斯萨珊帝国的设施经常已经处在朽坏的前期状态中，阿拉伯人修复这些设施，还兴建极多新的公共基础建设，包括新型的水库、利用地下水、把地下水送到远处的地下运河，以及把河流、运河、水井和蓄水池中的水扬升出来的各种轮子，这种基础建设投资不是以新科技为基础，却涉及采用和运用现有的科技知识，因而大大提高了经济产能。

The state that emerged from Muhammad’s political leadership played a decisive role in building and sustaining these renovated irrigation systems. It also encouraged private individuals to make complementary investments. This was not just because of the relative political stability that emerged with the Caliphate. It was also because Medina was an oasis and Muhammad, in his role as the resolver of disputes, had had to deal with conflicts over water and irrigation and had outlined a series of precedents that formed the basis of a legal system that facilitated investments. Particularly significant was the fact that instead of vesting them collectively in tribes or clans, these rulings individualized water rights. This improved incentives and eased disputes between individuals. Other laws directly encouraged production, including one that gave outright private ownership of a piece of land brought into cultivation for the first time and restricted taxation on such land to one-tenth of its output.

> 在兴建和维护这些创新灌溉系统上，从穆罕默德政治领导中蜕变出来的哈里发国扮演了决定性的角色，也鼓励民间个人投入辅助性的投资，这种现象不只是基于哈里发国的政治相当稳定，也是因为麦地那是个绿洲，穆罕默德身为纷争的仲裁者，必须处理跟水和灌溉有关的冲突，还描画了一系列的先例，形成促进投资法律制度的基础。特别重要的是，这些裁决把水权授予个人，而不是授予集体的部落和部族。直接鼓励生产的其他法律，包括把初次开垦土地的所有权，完全授予私人，还把这种土地的税负限制为土地产出的十分之一。

But these early benefits soon evaporated along with the buoyant tax revenues that they generated. Khaldun is indeed clear that these early economic advances could not be sustained. When the dynasty continues in power and their rulers follow each other in succession . . . the Bedouin qualities of moderation and restraint disappear. Royal authority with its tyranny, and sedentary culture . . . make their appearance . . . individual imposts and assessments upon the subjects, agricultural laborers, farmers, and all the other taxpayers, increase . . . Customs duties are placed upon articles of commerce and levied at the city gates. . . . Eventually, the taxes weigh heavily upon the subjects and overburden them . . . The result is that the interest of the subjects in cultural enterprises disappears, since when they compare expenditures and taxes with their income and gain and see the little profit they make, they lose all hope. Therefore, many of them refrain from all cultural activity.

> 但是，随着这种土地的税收增加，这些早期的好处很快就消失一空，赫勒敦的确很清楚这种早期的经济优势不可能维持下去。王朝继续掌权，统治者不断继承下去时，贝多因人适度和克制的特性消失，王室权威的暴虐和久坐不动文化……出现……对臣民、农业劳工、农民和其他纳税人课征的个人税负与估价提高……开始在城门门口，对商业物品课征关税……最后，税负重担压在臣民头上，负担变得过重……结果臣民对从事文化企业的兴趣消失，因为他们比较支出、所得和获利的税负后，发现自己几乎无利可图，因而丧失所有希望，因此很多人拒绝从事所有文化行动，结果导致整体税收下降。

The result is that the total tax revenue goes down. With tax revenues falling, “the ruler must invent new kinds of taxes. He levies them on commerce. He imposes taxes of a certain amount on prices realized in the markets and on the various imported goods at the city gates . . . Business falls off . . . This situation becomes more and more aggravated, until the dynasty disintegrates. Much of this sort happened in the Eastern cities during the later days of the Abbasid and Ubaydid-Fatimid dynasties.” (The Fatimid dynasty is a third caliphate—taking its name from Muhammad’s daughter Fatima—which ruled North Africa from the early tenth to late twelfth centuries.) Existing evidence supports Khaldun’s account.

> 税收降低后，「统治者必须发明新的税目，对所有商业课税、对市场中实现的价格课征一定的税额，也在城门门口，对各种进口商品课税……商业衰退……情势变得愈来愈恶化，到王朝解体为止。在阿拔斯王朝和法提玛（Ubaydid-Fatimid）王朝（法提玛王朝是第三个哈里发王朝，名称源於穆罕默德的女儿法提玛，从十世纪初期至十二世纪末期统治北非）。现有的证据支持赫勒敦的说法。

While the tax rate on land seems to have risen inexorably after the Arab conquests, revenues fell. Revenues from Iraq, for example, decreased from 12.8 million dinars after the conquest to 8.3 million dinars at the end of the Umayyad dynasty, to 5 million by 819 and just over 3 million by 870. The data from Egypt and Mesopotamia tell the same story. One common response was that the ruler “himself may engage in commerce and agriculture, from desire to increase [his] revenues.”

> 虽然从阿拉伯人征服后，土地税税率似乎持续不断地提高，税收却减少，以伊拉克的土地税收为例，从阿拉伯人征服后的一千二百八十万迪纳（dinar），下降到伍麦亚王朝结束时的八百三十万迪纳，到八一九年，更降为五百万迪纳，到八七○年，更是降到只略高于三百万迪纳，埃及和美索不达米亚的资料也一样。常见的反应是统治者「自己可能从事商业和农业，希望提高（自己的）收入」。

But Khaldun saw that this could be very harmful to the people in the society because “when the ruler, who has so much more money than they, competes with them, scarcely a single one of them will any longer be able to obtain the things he wants, and everybody will be worried and unhappy. Furthermore, the ruler can appropriate much of the agricultural products and the available merchandise . . . He can do it by force, or by buying things up at the cheapest possible price. Further, there may be no one who would dare bid against him. Thus, he will be able to force the seller to lower his price.” On the other hand, when the ruler is selling, he forces everyone to pay high prices. Competition from the ruler thus generates a situation where “the farmer gives up agriculture and the merchant goes out of business.”

> 但是，赫勒敦认为，这样可能对社会上的人造成非常大的伤害，因为「比人民有钱得多的统治者与民争利时，几乎没有半个百姓能够再取得他想要的东西，每个人都会担心、都会不高兴。此外，统治者可以占有大部分的农产品，以及他想要的商品供应……他可以运用武力，或以最便宜的价格购买。此外，可能没有人敢跟他竞标，因此他可以强迫卖方降价。」另一方面，统治者出价时，会强迫每一个人都付出高价，来自统治者的竞争会造成「农民放弃农业、商人停业」的状况。

As the Umayyad and Abbasid states disintegrated, the impact of tax farming and their inability to construct effective bureaucratic management also led to a deterioration of infrastructure, and a fall in investment since farmers were at the mercy of local elites. Indeed, land seems to have been abandoned by ordinary farmers who saw no way to make ends meet and instead moved to towns and cities. The entry of elites into the economy had exactly the impact Khaldun identified.

> 随着伍麦亚王朝和阿拔斯王朝的国家解体，包税制的冲击显现，加上两个王朝无力建构有效的官僚管理，也导致基础建设恶化，农民受到本地菁英的宰割，导致投资减少。事实上，一般农民看不出如何维持生计，因而似乎持续不断地抛弃土地，搬迁到城镇地区，菁英涉入经济，的确造成了赫勒敦所看出来的冲击。

Khaldun explicitly noted how his theory explained the collapse of the Abbasids: The group feeling of the Arabs had been destroyed by the time of the reign of al-Mutasim and his son al-Wathiq. They tried to maintain their hold over the government thereafter with the help of Persian, Turkish, Daylam, Saljuq, and other clients. Then the Persians (non-Arabs) and clients gained power over the provinces of the realm. The influence of the dynasty grew smaller, and no longer extended beyond the environs of Baghdad.

> 赫勒敦明确指出，他的理论解释了阿拔斯王朝崩溃的原因：到默达西姆（Mutasim）和他儿子瓦提克（Wathiq）统治时，阿拉伯人的族群意识已经遭到摧毁，因此他们后来靠着波斯人、土耳其人、代兰姆人（Daylam）、塞尔柱人（Saljuq）和其他附庸民族的协助，设法维持对政府的控制。然后波斯人（非阿拉伯人）和附庸民族在帝国疆域的省份中得势，王朝的影响力式微，以至于令不出都城巴格达地区。

## Janus-Faced Despotic Growth · 专制成长的两面特质

Khaldun’s theory is a brilliant illustration of the impact of despotic state formation on the economy—not because the type of cycle that Khaldun hypothesized with the bad following the good is some “historical law,” but because it highlights both the good and the bad that an economy under despotism will always embody. A state can provide many benefits in terms of increased order, security, and peace. It can enforce laws, bringing clarity and predictability to conflicts that inevitably emerge in the process of economic transactions. It helps markets and trade expand.

> 专制国家形成会对经济造成什么影响？赫勒敦的理论对此解释得非常清楚──不是因为赫勒敦假设恶和善相随出现的循环形态是某种「历史法则」，而是因为赫勒敦强调：在专制主义式的经济体中，善与恶总是会具体表现出来。国家在增进秩序、安全和和平方面，可以提供很多好处，可以执行法律、为经济交易过程中一定会出现的冲突，带来明确性和可预测性，协助市场和贸易扩张。

The state, and the state builders controlling it, may also find it in their interest to enforce property rights for the same reasons as the Laffer curve: without property rights and predictability of the state’s policies, it’s as if everyone is facing close to 100 percent tax rates and will lack incentives to produce, work, trade, and invest—and the result would be little tax revenue for the state. Not a desirable outcome. So it’s better to keep taxes low. When that happens, economic activity can potentially flourish, with all the benefits that this brings to society as well as to the Despotic Leviathan.

> 国家和控制国家的建国者们可能发现，执行财产权可能对他们自己有利，原因就像拉弗尔曲线一样：没有财产权、国家的政策没有可预测性的话，就像每一个人都要面对百分之百的税率，没有生产、工作、贸易和投资的诱因一样──结果就是国家几乎没有什么税收，这样不是什么理想状况。因此，最好把税负压低，那么在这种情况下，经济活动可能勃兴，为社会和专制国家带来所有好处。

The same logic underlies the reason why state builders might find it in their interest to provide public services, infrastructure, and even education to increase productivity and economic activity. All of this implies that the Despotic Leviathan may create better economic opportunities and incentives than Warre or the cage of norms. It may even organize society, structure laws, and make investments so as to directly stimulate economic growth. This is the essence of what we call “despotic growth.”

> 同样的逻辑强调建国者们为什么可能发现：提供公共服务、基础建设，甚至提供教育，以便提高生产力和经济活动，对他们自己有利。所有这一切都暗示专制国家可能比战争状态或规范牢笼，更善于创造比较好的经济机会和诱因，专制国家甚至可能组织社会、建构法律、从事投资，直接刺激经济成长，这种情况是我们所说「专制式成长」的本质。

The history of the Islamic state illustrates this type of growth clearly. Compared to the warring clans that had previously dominated Medina, Muhammad’s ability to resolve disputes and enforce his laws spurred economic activity. Property rights became more secure for the Medinans as Muhammad’s state prevented disputes from escalating and after his unification of the Arabian tribes put a stop to raids. The same factors also facilitated trade. As we have just seen, this proto-state organized new public infrastructure investments, including dams, underground canals, and other irrigation facilities. Agricultural productivity increased greatly as a result. All in all a far cry from where Medina was heading before Muhammad’s arrival.

> 伊斯兰国的历史清楚说明这种成长形态。和先前主导麦地那的部族交战不休相比，穆罕默德解决纷争、执行他所制定法律的能力，刺激了经济活动。麦地那人的财产权得到更稳固的保障，因为穆罕默德的国家防止纷争升高，而且他统一阿拉伯部落后，互相袭击嘎然而止，同样的因素也促进了贸易。就像我们刚刚看到的那样，这个雏形国家推动新的公共基础建设投资，包括兴建水库、地下运河和其他灌溉设施，农业生产力因而大幅提高，一切的一切，都和穆罕默德来到麦地那之前不可同日而语。

Yet in the same way that the Leviathan is Janus-faced, so is despotic growth, and Khaldun understood this very perceptively as well. He recognized that the despotic state, lacking any popular control or mechanisms of accountability, is bound to concentrate more and more political power in its hands. With more power comes greater monopolization of economic benefits, greater temptation to violate the property rights that it was meant to protect, and a creeping slide along the Laffer curve toward the place where the tax rates and the risks of expropriation are so high that not only citizens’ livelihoods but even the state’s tax revenues start to suffer.

> 但是，专制式成长和国家巨灵一样，也具有两面性格，赫勒敦也非常清楚这一点，知道专制国家因为少了人民的控制，也没有负责机制，注定会把愈来愈多的权力集中在自己手里。权力愈多，垄断的经济利益愈大，国家原本应该保护的财产权，受到侵害的可能性大增；国家在不知不觉中，沿着拉弗尔曲线，落入税率和征收风险极高，不但陷入公民生活开始受到侵害、连国家税收都会开始受损的状态中。

Khaldun in fact saw this stage, where the state turns against society, as inevitable. This not only meant that the fruits of despotic growth would ultimately dry out, but also that the anticipation of the fearsome face of the Leviathan would undercut the benefits it generates even sooner than that. In Khaldun’s poetic language, this meant: Like the silkworm that spins and then, in turn

> 赫勒敦其实看到了这个阶段，看到国家不可避免地开始和社会为敌的阶段，这样不但表示专制式成长的果实最后会干枯，而且预期面目狰狞的国家，甚至更早就会开始侵害自己所创造的好处。赫勒敦用诗歌般的文字表示，这样代表：好比蚕儿吐丝一般，

Finds its end amidst the threads itself has spun. A second reason why despotic growth will be limited is equally fundamental. As we emphasized in our previous book, Why Nations Fail, sustained economic growth necessitates not just secure property rights, trade, and investment, but more critically, innovation and continual productivity improvements. These are much harder to usher in under the stern gaze of the Despotic Leviathan.

> 再在丝中吐尽结茧。专制式成长会受到限制的第二个原因同样重要。我们在前一本书《国家为什么会失败》里强调，经济成长不但需要稳固的财产权、贸易和投资，但更重要的是，需要创新和持续提升的生产力。在专制国家的严厉监视下，要引进这些东西会困难得多。

Innovation needs creativity and creativity needs liberty—individuals to act fearlessly, experiment, and chart their own paths with their own ideas, even if this is not what others would like to see. This is hard to sustain under despotism. Opportunities aren’t open to everybody when one group dominates the rest of society, nor is there much tolerance for different paths and experiments in a society without liberty.

> 创新需要创意，创意需要自由──个人要无畏无惧、勇于实验，根据自己的构想，擘画前途，不顾别人的喜恶。在专制制度下，这种事情很难长久维持。当一个群体宰制社会上的其他人时，机会之窗不会对每一个人开放；在没有自由的社会里，也不会太能容忍不同的路线和实验。

Indeed, these are the reasons why we argued in our earlier book that “extractive growth,” a close cousin of what we are calling despotic growth here, is limited and is highly unlikely to become the basis of sustained, long-term prosperity. We illustrated this limited nature of extractive growth with several examples, the simplest being the rise and fall of the Soviet growth miracle. The Soviets could organize the economy to pour resources and huge investments into manufacturing and subsequently into the space race and military technologies. Yet they could not generate sufficient innovation and productivity improvements to keep their economy from stagnating and then collapsing.

> 的确如此，我们在上一本书里，主张跟我们现在所说专制式成长类似的「榨取式成长」有其限制，而且很不可能成为长期持续繁荣的基础。我们用好几个例子，说明榨取式成长受到限制的这种性质。最浅显的例子是前苏联成长奇迹的兴衰，前苏联可以组织经济，倾注资源和巨额投资在制造业中，然后再倾注在太空竞赛和军事科技中，却无法创造足够的创新和生产力改善，避免经济陷入停滞和随后的崩溃。

This example underscores the fact that extractive growth results when a ruler who is constrained neither by institutions nor society finds it in his interest to support growth. But even when this is the case, the ruler will not be able to organize or command innovation. Nor will he be able to secure a broad-based distribution of opportunities to make best use of the creativity of the people. It’s the same with despotic growth called into existence by the Despotic Leviathan, without any popular control, active participation by society, or true liberty.

> 这个例子强调在统治者既不受制度约束，社会也不觉得支持经济成长对自己有利时，榨取式成长会有什么结果。就算这真的达成经济成长，统治者也会无法推动或下令创新，也无法确保机会能够普遍的分配，以便善尽利用人民的创意。专制国家地区的专制式成长也是这样，缺少了人民的控制、社会的积极参与或真正的自由。

## The Law of the Splintered Paddle · 碎桨法

The benefits of despotic growth for state builders were well understood by Kamehameha as he was unifying the Hawaiian islands. The first law he passed after his final conquest was the Law of the Splintered Paddle. This read: Oh my people, honor thy gods; respect alike [the rights of] men great and humble; see to it that our aged, our women and our children lie down to sleep by the roadside without fear of harm.

> 卡美哈梅哈统一夏威夷群岛时，十分清楚专制式成长对建国者们的好处，他完成最后征服后，通过的第一项法律就是《碎桨法》（Law of the Splintered Paddle），该法规定：全国人民啊，敬天法祖；尊敬所有人的权利，无论尊卑；确保老弱妇孺安睡路旁，无虞受到伤害。

Disobey and die. This law has been seen to be so significant in the history of Hawaii that it was incorporated into the 1978 State Constitution, Article IX, Section 10, of which reads: Public safety. The law of the splintered paddle, mamala-hoe kanawai, decreed by Kamehameha I—Let every elderly person, woman and child lie by the roadside in safety—shall be a unique and living symbol of the State’s concern for public safety. The State shall have the power to provide for the safety of the people from crimes against persons and property.

> 不服从此法者受死无碍。这项法律在夏威夷历史上极为重要，以至于纳入一九七八年的州宪第九条第十款中，条款中规定：公共安全──卡美哈梅哈一世颁布的《碎桨法》中、老弱妇孺安睡路旁无虞受到伤害的条文，是本州关心公共安全独一无二的鲜明象征。本州必定会保障人民人身与财产免于犯罪侵害。

The law’s original intention was to indicate that the new state would not tolerate unprovoked attacks on people or property. The name of the law referred to an incident in which Kamehameha, as a young warrior, was engaged in a raid on the Puna Coast, on the southeastern side of Hawaii Island (see Map 6 in the previous chapter), and decided to attack some fishermen to take their catch. Jumping out of his canoe onto the shore, Kamehameha got his foot caught in a crevice of lava, and seeing this, one of the fishermen had the courage to come up and hit him with a paddle that splintered on impact.

> 该法的初衷意在指出，这个新州绝不容忍对人身与财产的无端攻击，该法名称起源于卡美哈梅哈身为年轻战士时发生的一件事：当时他参与劫掠夏威夷岛东南部的普纳海岸（参见前一章的地图六），决定攻击若干渔民，夺取他们的渔获。卡美哈梅哈从独木舟跳上岸时，脚陷在岩浆形成的裂缝中。一位渔民勇气十足，跑过来用船桨打他，一打之下，船桨破裂。

The title of the law indicates that Kamehameha later realized that the attack had been wrong, and he signaled his intention to eliminate such behavior. He was worried about unprovoked attacks not just against the persons and property of indigenous Hawaiians, but also against foreigners. He realized that the prosperity of his new island kingdom rested on increased commercial relations with the outside world. During the unification of the islands an active trade in the provisioning of foreign ships had developed, yet this was continually threatened by hostile acts.

> 这项法律的名称显示，卡美哈梅哈后来知道，他们的攻击不对，他要借着这项法律，表明他希望消除这种行为的心愿。他不仅担心夏威夷原住民人身和财产遭到无端攻击，还担心外国人也遭到攻击；他知道他新统一的岛屿帝国能否繁荣发展，关键在于跟外在世界增进商业关系。在夏威夷群岛统一期间，夏威夷和外国船舶之间发展出积极的补给品贸易关系，但这种贸易持续受到敌对行为威胁。

Hawaiians were particularly fond of stealing the anchors of foreign vessels. In the previous chapter we saw that the chain of events that led to the death of Captain Cook had been initiated by the theft of a cutter from Cook’s flagship. As early as 1793, Kamehameha had declared to a Mr. Bell, a member of the explorer George Vancouver’s expedition to the islands, his most solemn determination never to molest or disturb the weakest vessel that comes to Kealakekua, or where he himself is, on the contrary to do everything he can to make their stay among them comfortable.

> 夏威夷人特别喜欢偷窃外国船舶的船锚，我们在前一章里，看到导致库克船长丧生的连锁事件，开端就是库克旗舰上的小艇失窃。早在一七九三年，卡美哈梅哈就对探险家乔治．温哥华（George Vancouver）率领、来到夏威夷群岛的远征船队成员贝尔先生宣布：他最庄严的决心──就是他绝对不会骚扰或打扰来到基亚拉凯库亚（Kealakekua）的最脆弱船舶，反之，他要尽其所能，让他们的停留舒适愉快。

Kamehameha was serious about this and about galvanizing despotic growth. Soon he managed to overcome the reticence of foreign traders to come to the islands. The potential economic benefits were substantial, and Kamehameha aptly took advantage of them. He monopolized foreign trade by introducing new kapu regulations to stop common people from engaging in trade with foreigners. He cornered the market so successfully that he was able to choose the terms of trade with foreigners, setting high prices for the supplies they needed to provision their ships. Such provisioning was lucrative, but Kamehameha soon realized that there were even better profits in the export of sandalwood.

> 卡美哈梅哈很注重这件事，也关心如何刺激专制式的成长，他很快地就设法克服了来到夏威夷群岛外国贸易商的沉默不语。潜在的经济利益十分庞大，卡美哈梅哈巧妙地加以利用，推出新的禁忌法规，阻止平民和外国人交易；他借此独占对外贸易，极为成功地垄断市场，以至于能够选择跟外国人贸易的条件，为外国人需要的船舶补给品订定高价，从中获得丰厚利润；但他很快地就知道檀香木的出口利润更高。

In 1812 he signed a contract with Boston ship captains the Winship brothers and W. H. Davis, under which he had the monopoly of sandalwood exports from Hawaii. The agreement was to last ten years and Kamehameha personally took one quarter of all profits. Ibn Khaldun would have noted that this arrangement wouldn’t bring prosperity for long. It didn’t, and in exactly the way Khaldun would have anticipated.

> 一八一二年，他跟波士顿的船长文希普（Winship）兄弟和戴维斯（W. H. Davis）签订合约，根据这个合约，他独占了夏威夷檀香木的出口；这项合约有效期间为十年，卡美哈梅哈个人取得所有利润的四分之一。赫勒敦应该会指出，这种安排不会带来长期繁荣。事实上也是这样，而且后续发展恰恰符合赫勒敦应有的预测。

## A Shark Going Inland · 进入内陆的鲨鱼

One of the great scholars of historical Hawaii was a Swede, Abraham Fornander, who arrived in the islands in 1838, learned the language, married a Hawaiian woman, and developed a passion for the society. He died in 1887, and his manuscripts were eventually published by the Bishop Museum in the 1920s. Fornander wrote down a chant: A shark going inland is my chief,

> 最伟大的夏威夷历史学家是瑞典人亚伯拉罕．傅南德（Abraham Fornander），他在一八三八年来到夏威夷群岛，学会当地语言，娶了一位夏威夷女性，培养出对夏威夷社会的热爱。一八八七年他去世后，手稿终于在一九二○年代，由主教博物馆出版。傅南德写过下面这首赞颂：我的酋长是进入内陆的鲨鱼，

A very strong shark able to devour all land; A shark of very red gills is the chief, He has a throat to swallow the island without choking. The chant compares the chiefs of Hawaii (before Kamehameha’s time) to “sharks going inland.” It was an apt, and predatory, analogy.

> 牠强壮到可以吞下所有土地；酋长是一只鱼腮鲜红的鲨鱼，喉咙可以顺利吞下这个岛屿。这篇赞颂把（卡美哈梅哈之前的）酋长，比喻为「进入内陆的鲨鱼」，是个具有掠夺性的恰当比喻。

Just like other instances of despotic growth, the creation of a Hawaiian state under Kamehameha soon followed in the ways of the previous chiefs—the shark went inland. This process was summarized by Samuel Kamakau, another one of the first generations of Hawaiians to emerge as historians of their society. Like David Malo, whose evidence we discussed in the previous chapter, Kamakau witnessed firsthand many of the events he recorded, or interviewed people who had. His description mentions the beneficial aspects of Kamehameha’s state building but is also blunt on the huge downside:

> 卡美哈梅哈创建的夏威夷国就像其他专制式成长的例子，很快就遵循过去酋长的样子，变身进入内陆的鲨鱼。另一位出身夏威夷、变成自己所属社会第一代历史学家的萨姆尔．卡玛考（Samuel Kamakau）总结说明了这个过程，他和马洛一样，亲眼见证了自己所记录的很多事件，或是亲访曾经目睹这些事件的人，他的描述提到卡美哈梅哈建国带来的好处，却也直言不讳其中的重大缺点：

The country as a whole benefitted by the uniting of the government under one head, but most of the chiefs and landlords under Kamehameha oppressed the commoners and took away their lands, thus forcing the people who had owned the land to become slaves . . . Taxes were laid upon all holdings whether large or small and were constantly being added to, for there were many landlords and under landlords who demanded tribute . . . The uniting of the land had brought about excessive taxation . . . “Even the smallest patches are taxed” . . . was a familiar saying. The progress of the shark—or sharks, since the chiefs below Kamehameha and his successors quickly got into the action—is vividly described in a surviving set of documents written in 1846, in the context of King Kamehameha III’s attempt to rationalize and redistribute land rights.

> 整个国家从一位领袖建立的一统政府中得到好处，但卡美哈梅哈统领的大部分酋长和地主都欺压良民，夺取人民的土地，因而迫使原来拥有土地的人民变成奴隶……所有土地不分大小，都遭到课税，而且因为地主和对地主进献的人很多，遭到课税的土地不断增加……国家的统一带来过高的税负……「连最小块的土地都要纳税」是大家熟悉的说法。鲨鱼或鲨鱼群──因为卡美哈梅哈和他的继承人辖下的众多酋长迅速采取行动──的进逼，在一八四六年所写、留存到现在的一套文件中，有着鲜明的描述。

A board of three people, one Hawaiian and two foreigners, established a set of “principles” by which property rights were to be formalized. These principles noted that “the King, representing the government, having formerly been the sole owner of the soil . . . must be considered to be so still.” Of course, as we already saw, it wasn’t the case that kings “owned” the land in the Western sense of the word. Nevertheless, the document went on to note:

> 当时卡美哈梅哈三世国王打算推动地权合理化和重分配，由一位夏威夷人和两位外国人组成的三人委员会，建立了推动财产权正式化的一套「原则」；这些原则指出：「代表政府的国王过去是土地的唯一拥有者……现在必须仍然如此认定。」我们已经知道，在西方的文字意义中，国王当然并未「拥有」土地，然而，这份文件仍然指出：

When the islands were conquered by Kamehameha I, he followed the example of his predecessors, and divided out the lands among his principal warrior chiefs, retaining, however, a portion in his hands, to be cultivated or managed by his own immediate servants or attendants. Each principal chief divided his lands, anew, and gave them out to an inferior order of chiefs, or persons of rank, by whom they were subdivided again and again; after passing through the hands of four, five or six persons, from the King down to the lowest class of tenants. All these persons were considered to have rights in the lands, or the productions of them.

> 卡美哈梅哈一世征服所有岛屿时，遵循先祖的范例，把土地分配给主要战士酋长，却保留一部分在手里，让自己的近侍或仆人种植或管理。每位主要酋长再把自己的土地分给下级酋长或属下，由他们继续一再分配，经过四、五、六手分配后，从国王分到最下级的佃农手中，大家认定这些人全都拥有土地或其上农作的权利。

All persons . . . owed and paid to the King not only a land tax, which he assessed at pleasure, but also, service which he called for at discretion, on all the grades from the highest down. They also owed and paid some portion of the productions of the land, in addition to the yearly taxes. They owed obedience at all times. This wording is significant. Not just the makaainanas, the ordinary people, owed tribute and labor now, everyone did—“all the grades from the highest down.”

> 所有的人……不只积欠国王土地税，必须缴纳国王随心所欲征收的这种税，从最高阶以下的所有人，也必须服他随意要求的劳役。大家除了缴纳年度税负外，也必须缴纳积欠国王的一部分土地生产的作物，而且随时必须服从效忠。上文中的措词很重要，现在不仅平民百姓要完税、劳动，「最高阶以下的所有人」，每一个人都必须这么做。

Also notable is the reference to “service which he called for at discretion.” Forced labor was extensively used on the king’s lands. F. I. Shemelin of the Russian-American Company, which had frequent interaction with the islands, recorded that “not only does he pay them nothing for their labor, he even declines to feed them.” Kamakau recorded that the sandalwood cutters were reduced to eating “herbs and fern trunks.”

> 同样值得注意的是，文中提到「必须服他随意要求的劳役」，国王的土地上大量利用强迫劳工。经常跟夏威夷群岛互动的俄罗斯美国公司（Russian-American Company）员工谢模林（F. I. Shemelin）写道：「他不只不付他们任何劳动薪酬，甚至还拒绝供养他们。」卡玛考记录说，檀香木伐木工人甚至沦落到吃「草根树皮」。

Forced labor became especially important as the demand for sandalwood expanded in the 1820s after Kamehameha’s death. The wood was usually far from people’s farms, growing on the slopes of more mountainous areas, and the king and chiefs began to organize massive missions of hundreds and even thousands of coerced men who would have to find, cut, and transport the wood to the coasts, a process which could take weeks. The English missionaries Tyerman and Bennet saw 2,000 men carrying sandalwood to the royal storehouse in Kailua, Hawaii, in 1822. They were neither paid nor fed, instead having to live off the land.

> 一八二○年代卡美哈梅哈去世后，檀香木需求扩大，强迫劳动变得特别重要，檀香木通常长在离农田很远的地方，长在比较山区地带的山坡上，国王和酋长开始组织几百、甚至几千人的强迫劳工伐木队伍，花上好多星期的时间，去寻找、砍伐、运输檀香木到海边。英国传教士邰尔曼（Tyerman）和班奈特（Bennet）见过两千人擡着檀香木，送到夏威夷凯鲁亚（Kailua）王家仓库去的景象，他们既没有薪资，也没有饮食，反而必须吃地上长的东西维生。

Coerced labor and the dislocation that it entailed soon led to a precipitous fall in agricultural output and to persistent near-famine conditions. One contemporary visitor recorded that the reasons why provisions are so scarce on this island is, that the people, for some months past, have been engaged in cutting sandalwood, and have of course neglected the cultivation of the land. The behavior of one chief in the north of O‘ahu, known as Cox, is particularly well documented. In the early 1820s he organized long campaigns to wrest sandalwood from the upland forest surrounding the Anahulu River valley in the north of the island.

> 强迫劳动和流离失所迅速造成农业产出剧烈减少，导致近乎持续性的饥荒状态。当时一位到访的游客记录说，这个岛上粮食这么稀少，原因在于人民过去几个月里，一直都在从事砍伐檀香木的工作，当然荒废了土地的耕种。有人特别清楚地记录欧胡岛北部一位柯克斯（Cox）酋长的行为，一八二○年代初期，他针对该岛北部安娜呼卢（Anahulu）河谷四周高地的檀香木，发动长期掠夺的行动。

A trader, Gilbert Mathison, witnessed the scale of this undertaking and the intensity of the coercion involved. He wrote: Cox had given orders to some hundreds of his people to repair to the woods by an appointed day to cut sandalwood. The whole obeyed, except one man, who had the folly and hardihood to refuse. Upon this his house was set fire to, and burnt to the ground on the very day; still he refused to go. The next process was to seize his possessions and turn his wife and family off the estate.

> 贸易商吉伯特．麦西森（Gilbert Mathison）亲眼看到这种行动的规模，也记下其中强迫劳动的程度，他写道：柯克斯下令几百个治下百姓，在约定的一天里，聚集在森林中，砍伐檀香木，所有的人都听命行事，只有一个人愚蠢和固执地拒绝听命，到了那一天，他的房子因此遭到纵火，烧成平地；但是，他仍然拒绝前往，下一个程序是没收他的财产，把他的妻子和家人扫地出门。

Mathison’s observations about the way Cox ran his territory show how extractive Kamehameha’s state became after he died. For example, an American sailor, who had been granted land by Cox, reported that he was afraid of making any sort of improvement on his land because it might attract Cox’s attention, who would then appropriate everything for himself. A local reported to the preacher James Ely in 1824 that

> 麦西森针对柯克斯治理自己领土的说明，显示卡美哈梅哈的国家在他去世后，变成极度压榨人民。例如，有一位获得柯克斯赠予土地的美国水手表示，他害怕对他的土地进行任何改善，因为这样可能会吸引柯克斯的注意，把一切占为己有。一八二四年，一位本地人告诉传教士詹姆斯．艾利（James Ely）：

we are sunk in discouragement. We have no inducement to labor, but many things to deter us from it. If we are enterprising, we are marked by the chiefs, and the property we obtain is taken by them. If we feed the heads of swine, or flocks of sheep, goats or fowl they are borne from us at the pleasure of the chiefs. If we sell produce, the money or property received in return is taken from us. The more enterprising, the more we are oppressed.

> 我们灰心绝望，毫无劳动诱因，但很多事情阻止我们劳动，如果我们勤劳努力，酋长会标定我们，我们得到的财产会被酋长们夺走。如果我们饲养猪只、羊群或家禽，酋长们会随心所欲地载走。如果我们出售农产品，酋长们会拿走我们收到的金钱和财产。我们愈勤劳努力，遭到的压迫愈重。

By the late 1820s the required work for the king increased from one day to three per week as coerced labor services for harvesting sandalwood were intensified. By the 1830s the sandalwood forests were exhausted, but now the king and chiefs started using coerced labor in agriculture. In the 1840s, the missionary William Roberts estimated that, in addition to all the required labor services, the average farmer was passing on a massive two-thirds of all of the output he produced to the king and the different chiefs.

> 到一八二○年代末期，因为砍伐檀香木的强迫劳动服务更趋密集，国王要求的劳动日数从每周一天，增加到每周三天。到一八三○年代，檀香木森林已经砍伐殆尽，但现在国王和酋长开始把强迫劳工用在农业上。传教士威廉．罗伯兹（William Roberts）估计，除了所有规定的劳动服务外，一般农民要把自己所有产出的三分之二之多，贡献给国王和不同的酋长。

This extractive system culminated in the Great Mahele of 1848, when King Kamehameha III decided on the radical distribution of lands we mentioned above. The outcome of this was that 24 percent of the islands’ lands were taken as private property by the king. A further 36 percent went to the government—again, in effect, to the king. A further 39 percent went to 252 chiefs, leaving less than 1 percent for the rest of the population. The sharks were well inland at this point, devouring the land.

> 一八四八年，这种榨取式制度攀升到最高峰时，卡美哈梅哈三世国王提出「大土改」政策（Great Mahele），决定激烈改革我们在上文中提到的土地分配。这个政策实施后，结果夏威夷群岛有百分之二十四的土地，变成国王的私有财产，另外有百分之三十六的土地落入政府手中──事实上还是落入国王手中。此外，还有百分之三十九的土地，落入二百五十二位酋长手里，留下不到百分之一的土地，分给其他人民。这时鲨鱼十分深入内陆，吞噬了所有的土地。

## The Bird That Devours Others · 吞噬其他人的鸟类

The economic implications of the Zulu state were similar. In the 1820s, the many small chieftaincies of the area that later became KwaZulu-Natal relied on maize, millet, and ranching. There was not a great deal of trade, and the international slave traders had never penetrated into this part of Africa. As in most precolonial African societies, families and sub-lineages had user rights to farm or graze their herds in certain areas. Both cows and crops were privately owned by families.

> 祖鲁国对经济也有类似的影响，一八二○年代内，后来变成夸祖鲁纳塔尔省的地区里，很多小酋长领地依赖玉米、小米和放牧为生，这里的贸易量一直都不大，国际奴隶买卖从来没有渗透到南非的这个地方。家族和亚系和非洲殖民前的大部分社会一样，有权耕种，也有权在某些地区放牧，乳牛和作物都由家族私人拥有。

Although the Zulu economy differed in many ways from that of Tivland, where cows were very scarce because of the presence of the tsetse fly, the evidence suggests that it was similarly caged. Cows, for instance, were a prestige good and could only be sold under very specific and unusual circumstances. In the process of his state-building efforts, Shaka reorganized the economy, breaking parts of the cage of norms that stood in the way. He declared that all the land belonged to him, a very radical departure from the initial status quo. As one oral history records:

> 虽然祖鲁经济在很多方面，跟采采蝇肆虐、乳牛非常稀少的蒂夫兰经济不同，证据却显示，祖鲁经济同样是关在牢笼里；例如，乳牛是一种声望商品，只能在非常特别的不寻常情况下出售。夏卡在建国过程中，改革经济，打破妨碍经济的部分规范牢笼。他宣布所有土地都属于他，跟最初的现状截然不同。有一段口述历史说道：

The land of Zululand belongs to Tshaka, he who unified all of it. Tshaka would take a fancy to a man and then, having conquered some chief’s land, would say this man might go and build at any spot he [Tshaka] might indicate. Men used to be given land by Tshaka, and a man might be given permission to occupy land even though other people might be living on it at the time.

> 祖鲁兰的土地属于统一全境的夏卡，夏卡可能喜欢一个人，然后在征服某位酋长的领土后，会说这个人可以去他（夏卡）指示的地方盖房子。过去土地通常是由夏卡赐予，他可能会准许某个人，占据当时可能有别人居住的地方。

Not just the land, but also the cattle belonged to Shaka. Nevertheless, this was still quite a simple economy. There was little manufacturing, though Shaka monopolized for himself the production of weapons, specifically spears and shields, which he gave to his troops. The anthropologist Max Gluckman underscored the limited extent to which inequality could manifest itself in such a society by observing: There is only so much maize porridge that a Zulu chief can eat.

> 不只是土地属于夏卡，牛群也属于夏卡。但是，这时的经济仍然相当单纯，几乎没有什么制造业，不过夏卡垄断了武器的生产，尤其是独占矛和盾的制造，再发给部队使用。人类学家梅克斯．葛拉克曼（Max Gluckman）强调在这种社会中、不平等彰显出来的程度可能相当有限时指出：一位祖鲁酋长吃得下的玉米粥就是这么多而已。

All the same, state formation under Shaka coincided with a huge increase in inequality, with Shaka, his relatives, and the core Zulu clan members who made up the royal family being the main beneficiaries. Even if he could eat only so much porridge, Shaka could and did monopolize power completely and establish unchallenged dominance over others. He not only managed to assert property rights over land and cows, he used his control over women and marriage to exert social control. He also monopolized the burgeoning trade with Europeans, who were perched on the coast. He ensured that all trade with Europeans went through his hands and cornered valuable supplies of ivory to sell to them.

> 夏卡建国时，不平等程度同时巨幅增加，夏卡自己、他的亲戚和构成皇家家族的祖鲁核心部族成员是主要的受益者，即使夏卡只能吃得下这么多玉米粥而已，他还是可以、而且确实完全独占权力，建立宰割别人、不容挑战的权威。他不仅设法主张土地和牛只的财产权而已，还利用他对妇女和婚姻的控制，发挥控制社会的力量。他也独占跟欧洲人之间欣欣向荣的贸易，并确保和住在海岸的欧洲人之间的所有贸易，都由他经手，还垄断卖给欧洲人的宝贵象牙供应。

But it wasn’t just extraction under Shaka. Once the warfare that created the Zulu state subsided, Shaka, like Kamehameha, set up a legal system and centralized institutions of conflict resolution that helped his people while also improving economic incentives. Oral history has it that Dingiswayo’s initial plan for territorial expansion was precipitated by a desire to stop the constant fighting and conflicts that broke out between small clans and chieftaincies.

> 但是，夏卡的统治并未止于肆意压榨。一旦创建祖鲁国的战火平息，夏卡也像卡美哈梅哈那样，制定司法制度，把解决冲突的机制集中到中央，帮助人民，同时也改善经济诱因。口述历史指出，丁吉斯瓦约最初的领土扩张计划能够加速完成，是因为人们希望阻止小型部族和酋长之间持续不断的战斗和冲突。

Indeed, once they were incorporated into Shaka’s state, there was a good deal of order, and the pax Shaka certainly protected people from the threat of raiding and attack from surrounding chieftaincies. Crime within the kingdom also appears to have greatly declined. Thefts of cattle that were quite common before Shaka’s rise largely disappeared because of the severe punishments that Shaka meted out against lawbreakers. As in our other examples, order in Zululand bred despotic growth, which benefited society to some degree, but then it greatly benefited Shaka and his entourage too.

> 事实上，一旦这些地方纳入夏卡的国家，相当良好的秩序就出现，夏卡统治下的和平的确保护了人民，让他们免于四周酋长领地劫掠和攻击的威胁。王国境内的犯罪似乎也大为降低，因为夏卡严惩犯罪行为，夏卡崛起前相当常见的偷牛案件大抵都消失了。祖鲁兰建立秩序后，正如同本书所提到的其他例子一样，孕育了专制式的成长，多少造福了社会，却也大大地造福夏卡和他的手下。

## The Economics of the Rose Revolution · 玫瑰革命中的经济学

In the freewheeling days after the collapse of Communism in Georgia, there was a bonanza in the private sector for transport services. For instance, there was a boom in marshrutkas, taxi buses that, compared to the previously tightly regulated system, proved hugely attractive and flexible. But the government of Eduard Shevardnadze, whose rise to power we narrated in Chapter 3, soon showed it could regulate too, and in spades. All marshrutka drivers had to have a medical exam every day to make sure they were not drunk and did not have high blood pressure. If a driver did not display his health certificate, he risked losing his license.

> 在乔治亚共产政权崩溃后百无禁忌的岁月里，运输服务的民间部门出现发财机遇，苏式计程小巴的荣景就是例子，和过去严格管制的制度相比，这种计程车式的小型公车非常具有弹性和吸引力，但我们在第三章中探讨过的谢瓦纳兹政府，很快就证明他们也可以肯定无疑的加以管制。所有苏式小巴司机每天都必须身体检查，确保他们没有喝酒、没有高血压，如果一位司机没有展示健康证明，驾照可能遭到撤销。

By the time Shevardnadze was in power there were hundreds, probably thousands, of marshrutkas ferrying people all over the capital city of Tbilisi. Shevardnadze’s government was detail-oriented not only when it came to taxi drivers. It decided that all of the stalls of petty streetside traders had to conform to a particular architectural design. Like marshrutka drivers, such traders had to renew their licenses twice a year. These regulations were only the tip of the iceberg. Gas stations, for example, had to be located at a specific distance from the street.

> 到谢瓦纳兹当政时，很可能有数百、甚至几千辆苏式小巴，在首都提弗利斯载客，谢瓦纳兹的政府不仅在计程小巴司机的管理上注重细节，也决定所有街道上的路边摊商必须符合特别的建筑设计，这些摊商必须像苏式小巴司机一样，一年要换证两次。这些规定只是冰山的一角，像加油站也必须设在离街道一定的距离之外。

Shevardnadze’s state must have accumulated a considerable amount of capacity to implement such measures. In a sense it did, but not in the obvious meaning of the word. In fact, these regulations, and thousands like them, were never intended to be implemented. Nobody really expected marshrutka drivers to have a daily medical exam, and they didn’t. But by creating such a rule, the Georgian state immediately created a pretext for prosecuting the entire fleet of marshrutka drivers. To avoid this, the drivers had to pay bribes. So did the petty traders. So did the gas stations.

> 谢瓦纳兹的国家一定累积了相当高明的能力，足以推动这些措施──从某个角度来看，的确是这样，但从文字的明显意义来看，却不是这样。事实上，这些规定和几千种类似的管制，从来就不打算实施，没有人真的期望苏式小巴司机每天去身体检查，他们也没有这样做。但是，乔治亚政府借着制定这种规则，立刻创造了起诉整个苏式小巴司机队伍的借口，计程车司机为了避免遭殃，就必须贿赂，小摊商也是这样，加油站也一样。

There is something in Shevardnadze’s naked actions to extract resources and bribes from Georgians that is a little different from Khaldun’s generational theory, which foresaw that despotism would first fuel some growth and then start intensifying its extraction—a pattern that seems to fit what happened in the Caliphate, in Hawaii, and even in Zululand. Shevardnadze’s state skipped the first step and dived right into thievery. Why was that? To answer this question we have to first recognize that the treatment of the marshrutka drivers was part of a more systematic policy (if it could be called that), one that was dictated not by economics but by a political logic. It was: Remain in power by creating economic disorder.

> 谢瓦纳兹政府借着赤裸裸的行动，从乔治亚人身上榨取资源和贿赂的做法，跟赫勒敦所说贵不过三代的理论有一点不同的地方，赫勒敦预测专制主义起先会促进若干成长，然后会加强榨取，这种形态符合哈里发国、夏威夷、甚至符合祖鲁兰发生的事情，谢瓦纳兹的国家机器跳过第一步，立刻跳进偷窃的阶段，为什么会这样？要回答这个问题，我们首先必须承认苏式小巴司机的处理，是一种比较有系统政策（如果可以这样说的话）的一部分，这种政策不是由经济学主导，而是由政治逻辑主导，就是创造经济混乱，以便继续掌权。

Shevardnadze behaved in this way in large part because he was in a much weaker position than the other state builders we have encountered in this and the previous chapter. Even after he outsmarted the warlords he was faced with strong regional powers within Georgia. He was grasping on to power rather than building a capable state, and he tried to do this by placating powerful interests by co-opting them with riches (or at the very least with bribes). Corruption in developing countries is common, so marshrutka drivers’ bribing state officials isn’t that unusual. But what went on in Georgia was a little different from this type of corruption.

> 谢瓦纳兹会这样做，主要原因是跟本章和前一章谈到的建国英豪相比，谢瓦纳兹的地位脆弱得多，即使在他智胜众军阀后，还是要面对乔治亚国内强大的地区势力，他不是建立能力高强国家的豪杰，而是靠着财富（或至少可以说是配合贿赂）的吸引，安抚强大的权力团体，奋力爬上权力顶峰的。在开发中国家里，贪腐很常见，因此苏式小巴司机贿赂国家官员并没有那么不寻常，但乔治亚发生的事情跟这种贪腐有点不同。

Shevardnadze set up the system so that the drivers were bound to break the law, and this provided low-hanging fruit for the police. He made lawbreaking inevitable and created a system that encouraged corruption. The main reason for this was to control society, which was now continually guilty of breaking the law. You could avoid implementation of the law by paying a bribe today, but the state could come after you at any time. Yet this scheme also controlled officials in the state, another potentially powerful group—accepting bribes was illegal, so the state could go after them too if it wanted to.

> 谢瓦纳兹制定的制度注定要让驾驶违法犯纪，同时提供警察可以轻易达成的目标，谢瓦纳兹把违法变成无可避免的事情，也创造了鼓励贪腐的制度。这样做的主因是为了要控制已经变成违法常业犯的社会，你今天可以借着贿赂，避免法律的执行，但国家随时可以咬住你。不过，这种阴谋也控制了另一个潜在的有力团体，也就是控制了国家官员──接受贿赂是违法行为，因此如果国家有意，也一样随时可以咬住这些官员。

Shevardnadze combined what we might call this “low corruption” with an equally labyrinthine system of “high corruption.” High-ranking elites, members of the parliament, and senior civil servants were sucked into similar schemes. They were also given a stake in Shevardnadze’s regime because he shared with them the incomes that flowed into his government, mostly from international donors. But they would only enjoy these incomes if Shevardnadze stayed in power. So they had to hitch their horses to his wagon.

> 谢瓦纳兹把我们可以称之为「低度贪腐」的做法，和同样如同迷宫般的「高度贪腐」的制度结合起来。高阶菁英、国会议员和高级文官都陷入类似的阴谋中，因为他们却也从谢瓦纳兹的政权中，分得一杯羹，因为谢瓦纳兹跟他们分享流入政府的所得──这些所得大部分来自国际捐赠者。但是，前提是谢瓦纳兹要继续掌权，他们才能享受这些所得，因此他们必须把马匹系在谢瓦纳兹的马车上。

Shevardnadze used multiple methods to do this and he had a huge advantage thanks to the country’s Communist history: the Georgian government owned most of the productive sectors of the economy. Though there had been token attempts at privatization, this had not really happened prior to his assumption of power. Shevardnadze then immersed himself in Russian-style privatization—selling selected assets for cheap to powerful people or people he wanted to co-opt (we’ll see how this privatization worked in Russia in Chapter 9).

> 谢瓦纳兹利用很多种方法，做到这一点，而且他要感谢乔治亚的共产党历史，让他拥有庞大的优势：乔治亚政府拥有整个经济体的大部分生产部门。虽然过去推动过象征性的民营化计划，但在谢瓦纳兹上台前，民营化并未真正开始。因此，谢瓦纳兹把全副精神，放在俄罗斯式的民营化计划上──廉价出售经过选择的资产，给有权有势的人或他希望吸纳的人（我们在第九章里，会看到俄罗斯怎么推动这种民营化）。

Often, to seal the deal, he made them ministers in charge of regulating the firms they owned. In this way he managed to create a series of monopolies. Just as the regulations at the bottom were part of his political strategy, so were those at the top. So, for example, the government passed a law prescribing that every car had to carry a specific type of fire extinguisher—one that was imported exclusively by a relative of the minister of the interior.

> 为了敲定交易，他经常任命这些人，担任主管他们所拥有企业的部会首长。他用这种方法，创造了一系列的独占事业。就像底层的管制是他的政治策略的一环，高层的做法也是其中一环。因此，举例来说，政府通过一项法律，规定每一辆汽车都必须携带特定款式的灭火器，这种灭火器却是由内政部长的亲戚独家进口。

Shevardnadze’s family got in on the action too. While most of the population suffered repeated power cuts, two commercial firms owned by the president’s family sold government-produced electricity on the side for a healthy profit of around $30 million a year. There were many regulations of imports and exports, so smuggling was very profitable and happened on a huge scale. In 2003 a parliamentary commission calculated that 90 percent of flour, 40 percent of gasoline, and 40 percent of cigarettes consumed in the country were smuggled.

> 谢瓦纳兹的家人也凑上一脚。当大部分人口苦于一再停电之际，总统家人拥有的两家产业公司却兼营副业，销售政府所发的电力，每年赚到大约三千万美元的稳当利润。政府订定很多进出口规定，所以走私利润非常丰厚，规模十分庞大。国会一个委员会计算，二○○三年内，国内消费的九成面粉、四成汽油和四成香烟，都靠走私进口。

This generated a huge stream of bribes, but since the elites, many in the government, were involved in the illegal trade, it also provided ample ammunition for the state to go after them if it wanted to, just as with the traffic police and the health checks on the marshrutka drivers. The encouragement of illegality was all part of the strategy. An indication of the extent to which the membership of the cabinet was used as a tool for co-option and corruption is that it was only in 2000, after he had been in power for eight years, that Shevardnadze finally appointed someone from his own political party as a minister. Significantly, that man was Mikheil Saakashvili, who became minister of justice. Saakashvili refused to be co-opted and was quickly fired, but then became one of the leaders of the Rose Revolution of November 2003, which forced Shevardnadze from power. Shevardnadze’s economic impact was decidedly negative.

> 这种情形造成金额庞大的贿赂流量，但是因为菁英、许多政府官员参与这种非法贸易，因此在政府想要追究时，也提供国家足量的军火去追查，就像交通警察针对苏式小巴司机进行健康检查一样，这种策略的整体目标就是鼓励非法。有一个指标可以显示内阁部长职位拿来当成吸收工具的程度，就是一直到二○○三年，谢瓦纳兹掌权八年后，才终于任命自己所属政党的成员，担任部长，这位名叫米哈伊尔．萨卡希维利（Mikheil Saakashvili）出任司法部长的人很重要，他拒绝谢瓦纳兹的吸收，很快就遭到撤职，后来却变成二○○三年十一月玫瑰革命的领袖之一，这场革命迫使谢瓦纳兹黯然下台。

This wasn’t simply because all the monopolies and regulations undermined the ability of markets to create incentives and opportunities for productive activity. It was also because Shevardnadze managed everything with a great deal of discretion and unpredictability. The aim of this economic disorder was to keep everyone off balance. You might be a minister today with a nice monopoly, and tomorrow Shevardnadze might change his mind and take it all away. The idea was to make people so dependent on the president that they became totally loyal. This worked so well that Shevardnadze managed to stay in power for a full decade. But the ambiguity and unpredictability created tremendous disincentives for investment. As a direct consequence, economic growth, even in its despotic form, did not materialize in Georgia.

> 谢瓦纳兹确实对经济造成了不利的影响，原因不仅在于所有独占事业和管制条规，有害市场创造生产活动诱因和机会的能力，也因为谢瓦纳兹管理一切时，发挥极大的自由裁量和难以预测的精神；这种经济混乱的目的是要让每个人慌慌张张，你今天可能是拥有相当大独占权的部长，明天谢瓦纳兹可能改变主意，夺走一切，这样做的目的是要让大家极为依赖总统，变成彻底效忠。这种做法极为有效，以至于谢瓦纳兹整整掌握大权十年之久。但是，其中的模棱两可和难以预测对投资极为不利，直接的结果是连专制式的经济成长，都没有在乔治亚出现。

The political strategy Shevardnadze quickly developed and perfected is not a Georgian aberration. As we have seen, despotism means silencing and sidelining society’s participation in political, social, and economic decisions, which in turn enables the exercise of despotic power. It doesn’t necessarily mean that the despot will be secure in his position, however, because others may be tempted by the political and economic benefits of controlling a powerful, unrestrained state. The threat of losing power may then push the ruler to structure the economy not for efficiency, but for co-opting willing rivals and cutting down unwilling ones. This is what Shevardnadze achieved in such a short time.

> 谢瓦纳兹迅速发展完成的政治策略并非乔治亚特有的异常现象，我们已经看到，专制主义表示抹煞和排除社会参与政治、社会和经济决策，从而使专制权力得以发挥。然而，这样不见得表示暴君可以安稳在位，因为其他人可能受诱惑，想要到掌控强大却不受限制国家的政经利益，暴君碰到失去权力的威胁时，可能推动经济重整；重整的目的不是为了追求效率，而是为了吸收有心的竞争对手，排除无心竞争的人。这就是谢瓦纳兹在这么短的时间内创造的重大成就。

With Shevardnadze, we are thus witnessing the worst facet of despotic growth. Nevertheless, it is important to understand the commonalities linking this episode to other instances. The fragility of despotic growth emanates partly from the fact that it will go on only so long as it is in the interest of the ruler and his entourage. The problem in Georgia was that from the get-go, growth was not Shevardnadze’s priority. He was too focused on weakening society, creating corruption, and buying off other powerful actors in Georgia, all with predictably awful consequences for prosperity.

> 因此，我们在谢瓦纳兹的例子里，看到了专制式成长中最糟糕的一面。然而，重要的是，我们必须了解这件事和其他例子之间的共通关系。专制式成长会这么脆弱，原因之一是这种成长只对统治者和他的党羽有利，因此进展有限。乔治亚的问题是从一开始，成长就不是谢瓦纳兹的优先目标，他太重视削弱社会、创造贪腐、收买乔治亚的权贵，所有这一切都注定对繁荣会有不利影响。

## Caged and Despotic Economics · 鸟笼经济与专制经济

The scorecard for the economy outside the corridor is decidedly mixed. If you live without the Leviathan, the situation is dire. For one, you may end up with ceaseless conflict “of every man, against every man” as Hobbes foresaw, with dismal incentives and thus “no place for industry.” If your society manages to mobilize its norms and customs to put a lid on conflict and contain violence, then this tends to create a caged economy, constricted by norms and full of distorted economic incentives that do nothing to end poverty.

> 自由窄廊之外的经济表现的确参差不齐。如果你活在没有国家的社会中，情况会很可怕；你可能像霍布斯所预测的那样，碰到「人人互相为战」、冲突无休无止的局面，经济诱因很少，勤奋「毫无用武之地」。如果你的社会致力动用规范和习俗，来压制冲突、遏止暴力，那么通常会创造出受到规范限制的鸟笼经济，充满完全无助于消除贫穷的扭曲经济诱因。

Despotism may improve on these outcomes, or so thought Hobbes. Compared to Warre or the caged economy, the Despotic Leviathan has clear advantages. Despotic though it may be, the state can prevent fighting, resolve conflicts, impose laws that help economic transactions, invest in public infrastructure, and help generate economic activity. It can even benefit the economy by relaxing the norm-based restrictions on economic activities.

> 霍布斯认为，专制主义或许可以改善这种结果。比起战争状态或鸟笼经济，专制国家据有明显的优势：国家虽然专制，却可以防止战斗，解决冲突，实施有助于经济交易的法律，从事公共基础建设投资，协助激发经济活动，甚至可以借着放宽以规范为基础的经济活动限制，造福整体经济。

The Caliphate shows how it can unleash huge economic potential because of the order it imposes and the productivity-enhancing investments it undertakes or encourages. This is despotic growth at its best. But it is inherently fragile and limited. It is fragile because, as Khaldun anticipated, the Despotic Leviathan will be continually tempted to extract more revenues from society, monopolize more of the valuable resources, and act in more wanton ways.

> 伊斯兰王国证明国家可以借着建立秩序，推动或鼓励加强生产力的投资，把惊人的经济潜力释放出来，创造最好的专制式成长。但是，专制国家天生就比较脆弱、天生就受到限制，像赫勒敦所预测的那样；专制国家本质脆弱，是因为国家会持续受到诱惑，希望从社会上榨取更多收入，希望垄断更多有价值的资源，希望更肆无忌惮地行事。

It is also fragile because the power of the state can be used, as Shevardnadze did, to create a hugely inefficient system just to avoid or cripple challenges to the despot’s position. It is limited too when it comes to undergirding sustained economic growth, because it does not activate and nurture the most productive aspects of society—its ability to freely function, generate broad-based opportunities and incentives for economic activity, and bring forth investment, experimentation, and innovation. For that, we have to wait for the emergence of liberty and the Shackled Leviathan.

> 专制国家比较脆弱还有另一个原因，就是可能有像谢瓦纳兹那样的人，利用国家权力，创造极为没有效率的制度，以便避免或削弱别人对暴君地位的挑战。专制国家也会比较容易受到限制，是因为专制国家对经济的持续成长，只能提供有限的支持，无法激发和培养社会中最有生产力的一面，也就是无法促进社会自由运作，为经济活动创造无限的机会与诱因，激发投资、实验与创新。这一切都必须等到自由和受到制约的国家巨灵兴起。
