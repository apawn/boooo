# Honesty and surrender · 诚实与臣服

A few random shifts came my way after Ruth’s departure. At shift handover I met other carers. This was the only brief time of socialising with other staff. Throughout the long twelve-hour shifts, there was no team banter or laughter as handover was the only time we ever saw each other. The client, family and medical professionals who dropped in became the only contact.

> 鲁思去世后，又有几份看护轮班工作机会找到了我。在轮班的交接中，我认识了其他的看护员。这是仅有的与其他同行的简短社交接触。在整个的12小时轮班当中，没有同事间的玩笑话或者说笑声，只有当大家在交接班的时候才相互打个招呼。其他时间，顾客、家庭成员及医务专业人员的造访成为了唯一能接触的人。

This made the relationships even more personal. It also gave me time to occasionally read, write, continue my meditation practice, or do some yoga. Many of the carers went crazy with too much time to themselves and it was not unusual to arrive at a house and find the television on before breakfast. Grateful that I loved my own company, the long hours of silence suited me quite well. Even if there were people around, with a dying person in the home they were usually peaceful environments.

> 这使得人际关系更为单纯化，也偶尔给了我时间去阅读、写作、继续我的冥想练习或者做一些瑜伽运动。许多看护人员很过分地经常迟到，早餐之前到达工作地点和为病人找到要看的电视频道都是少见的事。感谢上帝，我爱自己的客户，长时间的安静工作环境特别适合我。通常与临终的人在一起的环境很安静、很平和，即使有其他人时不时在周围出现。

Walking into Stella’s home in the tree-lined suburb, this was definitely the case. It wasn’t just the fact that she was dying. These were peaceful, gentle people. Stella had long, straight, white hair. Graceful was the first word that came to mind when we met, despite the fact she was lying ill in bed. Her husband George was a beautiful man and welcomed me naturally.

> 然而，走进斯特拉位于树木林立的郊区的家，却绝对是不同于往常的特殊状况。事实上，绝不仅是斯特拉临近死亡。这个家里的所有人都很平和、善良。斯特拉有着长长的白色直发。尽管她生病躺在床上，但当我们见面的时候，浮现在我脑海里的第一个词仍是优雅。她的丈夫乔治是一个美男子，很亲切地欢迎了我。

Having to accept a family member is dying is an enormous life changing time in itself. When that person reaches the stage of needing twenty-four hour care however, everything about the life these people knew has disappeared. Their privacy and the special moments of just the two of them in the home are gone forever.

> 接受一名家庭成员即将死去这本身就是一种巨大的生活突变。然而，当临终的人需要24小时看护的时候，人们所熟悉的往日生活的一切已经一去不复返了。他们的隐私生活以及两个人在自己家里的那些特殊时光将永远消失。

Carers came and went, changing shifts morning and night. Some were regular but some only came once, in between their own regular clients. So there were new faces to deal with, new personalities and varying work ethics. Before long though, I became the regular day carer for Stella. A community nurse would also visit, as would the palliative doctor. He was a man I saw briefly with many clients over the following years and what a special, delightful, kindhearted person he was.

> 看护人员们来来去去，早晚的交接班。一些人是定期的过来，有些则在自己的固定客户之间偶尔来一次。因此，这里有新的面孔去应对，有新的人格特质和不同的职业道德观。我很快成为了斯特拉的定时白天看护人员。一名社区护士和姑息治疗“医生也会来看望病人。接下来的几年里我在许多客户那里时而也见过那位姑息治疗医生，他是一位很特别、令人愉快和仁慈的人。

After the experiences with Ruth, my employer said I’d handled it beautifully and offered me more training in palliative care, if I would like to go down that road. I accepted this offer, as I felt that life was calling me in this direction for now. The time and learning with Ruth had had a profound effect on me, leaving a desire to grow and experience more in this field.

> 有了在鲁思那里的工作经验之后，我的老板认我会很漂亮地处理好事情，如果我愿意在这条职业道路上走下去，他们将为我提供更多的姑息治疗方面的培训。我接受了老板的提议，因力我感觉到现在生活正召唤着我往这个方向走下去。在照顾鲁思的那些日子里我所学到的东西对我产生了深远的影响，让我有意愿在这个领域里成长和拥有更多的经验。

My training involved two workshops. One of these was to show myself and other carers how to wash our hands properly. The other was a very brief display in lifting procedures. That was pretty much it, my full extent of formal training. Then sending me off to work with Stella, my employer told me not to tell them I’d only ever had one palliative client. She believed I could do the job, as did I.

> 我的培训包含了两周的工作坊。这些课程之一是培训人员向我和其他看护人员展示如何彻底地洗手。其他的课程是在提升过程中一个很简短的示范展现。那些就是我所接受到的很好的全部正规训练。然后，我的老板派我到斯特拉这里工作，她告诉我不要对他们讲我以前只有一次的姑息照顾客人的经验。她相信我能像以前那样做好这份工作。

Honesty had always been a significant part of my personality. But when questions came up from the family about my experience, I found myself lying, all because I needed the work. New laws were also coming in about staff qualifications, of which I had none.

> 诚实总是我个性中的重要组成部分。但是，当斯特拉的家人问我有关这方面的工作经验时，我发现自己在撒谎，一切都是①姑息治疗医学是对那些对治愈性治疗不反应的病人完全的主动的护理。控制疼痛及有关症状，并对心理、社会和精神问题予以重视。其目的是为病人和家属赢得最好的生活质量。因为我需要这份工作。新的法律已经出台了规定，但我没有资格证书。

Even though I couldn’t prove my skills by speaking of previous experience however, I did also want Stella’s family to feel at ease with me. I knew in my heart I could do the job well, as it was about gentleness and intuition more than anything. So I went along with the lies, saying I had nursed more people than I had when they asked. Lying felt so uncomfortable for me though, I could never do it with another client.

> 即使我不能靠口述以往的经验来证明我的工作技能，我也要让斯特拉的家人感觉到对我有信心。我心里明白，我可以做好这个工作，因这份工作所需的善良与直觉胜过任何技能。所以，我继续我的谎言，并告诉他们我有护理过很多病人的经验。撒谎让我感觉很不爽，我绝不可以这样对另外的客户。

Stella was very much into hygiene and wanted clean sheets on the bed every day. But she was also a lady of style and insisted on wearing a matching nightdress to go with the colour or pattern of the sheets. George laughed to me one day that he had found himself in trouble for choosing the wrong sheets for the nightdress she wanted to wear. I said to him laughing, as I ended up saying to the families of almost all of my future clients, “Whatever makes her happy”.

> 斯特拉非常爱卫生，要求每天更换床单。而她也是一个有品味的女人，坚持睡衣的颜色跟床单的色彩相匹配。有一天，乔治对着我笑，因为他自己发现出了麻烦，所选的床单颜色没有跟她要穿的睡衣颜色一致。我笑着最后对他说“无论什么，让她开心就行”，对所有以后的类似客户我都这样说。

And so it was that this tall graceful woman lay dying in her choice of sheets and nightdress when she asked me about my life. “Do you meditate?” she asked. “Yes I do,” I happily replied. It was not a question I had expected. Stella continued. “What path do you follow?” I told her and she nodded in understanding.

> 当斯特拉问起有关我的生活时，这个高大而优雅的女人就是这样在病入音肓之际还躺在那里选择她的床单和睡衣。“你有做冥想吗？”“是的，”我开心的回答她。我没有想到她会问这个问题。她又问我：“你是遵照哪种方法？”我告诉了她，她会意地点点头。

“Do you do yoga?” she asked. “Yes I do,” I again replied, “but not as much as I’d like to”. “Do you meditate daily?” I said, “Yes. Twice a day.” I couldn’t help but smile, when after a moment she replied with a gentle voice, “Oh thank God. I have been waiting for you for ages. I can die now”.

> “你做瑜伽吗？”她继续问我。“是的，”我再次回答了她。“但是并没有像我希望的那样经常去做。”“你每天都做冥想吗？”我说，“嗯，一天两次。”停顿片刻之后，当她以温柔的声音跟我说，“噢，感谢上帝。我一直期待着有你这样的朋友很长时间了。现在我可以瞑目了。“我忍不住笑了。

Stella had been a yoga instructor for forty years, long before yoga had become an everyday thing in Western culture. Back then it was something strange from the East. She had been to India several times and was very devoted to her path. Originally, because it was too out-there for the world they lived in, Stella had said she was an exercise instructor when asked, rather than a yoga teacher. As society thankfully evolved over time and yoga became more mainstream, she came out of her shell and taught many students the arts and wisdom of this path.

> 在西方，在瑜伽成为了人们日常生活中一件寻常事的很久之前，斯特拉就已经是一名瑜伽导师并且持续了有四十年了。当时人们认为来自东方的瑜伽是一种稀奇古怪的东西。她曾经去过印度几次，并十分投入她的职业。当初，因为他们所居住的地区太不理解瑜伽，当有人问及她的职业，她宁愿说自己是一名健身教练，而不是瑜伽老师。随着社会的发展和时间的推移，瑜伽成了主流，她终于脱去伪装，正大光明地在瑜伽这个行业里向许多学员传授艺术和智慧。

Her husband was a retired professional who still did a little work from home. He pottered about peacefully and I enjoyed his presence. The home library was full of spiritual classics. Many I had read, but many I had wanted to and as yet hadn’t. It was a reader’s dreams come true, particularly for one so interested in philosophy, psychology, and spirituality.

> 她的丈夫是一位退休教授，在家里仍然做一些少量工作。我很喜欢他在家里平和且逍遥自在的样子。这个家里的书房充满了经典灵性的气息。许多的书我有读过，还有许多书我没有读过但很想去读。尤其对一个在哲学、心理学以及精神灵性方面有极大兴趣的读者来说，这里是一个美梦成真的地方。

I devoured them as much as possible. Stella would stir from her sleep, ask what book I was reading, where I was up to in the book, and pass comment on it. She knew them all. When she was alert enough for long conversations, which wasn’t often enough, it was always about philosophy. We shared many theories and found our thinking not terribly different from each other.

> 我尽可能多地如饥似渴地阅读那些书。斯特拉会放弃睡觉，打起精神问我正在读什么书，在哪里拿出这本书，并给予评论意见。她知道书房里的所有书。当她清醒得有足够时间进行长时间对话时，她总是谈论有关哲学的话题，不过，因为她的身体状况欠佳，我们不经常有这样的长谈机会。我们分享了许多哲学理论，并发现我们的想法差异不大。

My yoga practice also improved greatly. I didn’t feel I had to hide what I was doing, or go off into another room. The door to Stella’s bedroom was never closed, so fresh air blew through unhindered at all times. It was a lovely space to work in. Stella’s peaceful white cat called Yogi would lie on the end of her bed and watch me. As the afternoons in the neighbourhood were particularly peaceful, I used that time the most to stretch and breathe.

> 我的瑜伽练习进步很大。我没有觉得我必须隐瞒我正在做什么，或者离开到另外一个房间去练习。进入斯特拉卧房的门从未关闭过，这样新鲜空气可随时顺畅地穿入房间。这是一个可爱的地方。斯特拉那只安静的白色猫叫约吉，它会躺在斯特拉的床尾，望着她。尤其是随着傍晚左邻右舍安静下来，我习惯利用这个时间做一些伸展运动和深呼吸。

Thinking Stella was asleep, I would be delighted when she would pass a comment on something I was doing and how to improve that posture or to try another similar one, perhaps more dynamic and challenging, before she dozed off again. At the time, I had been doing yoga for about five years. It had begun in Fremantle, a suburb of Perth, whilst living over in Western Australia. Twice a week I would jump on my pushbike and ride down to Fremantle from a couple of suburbs away. Kale was the teacher. He truly was a wonderful introduction for me to yoga.

> 想到斯特拉要睡着了，在她又打瞌睡之前，当她把我正在做的事进行一番评论，并告诉我如何改善那个姿势或者让我试着做一个也许力度更大和更具有挑战性的相似动作时，我会很高兴地接受。那时，我已经进行了大约5年时间的瑜伽练习。我在帕斯的郊区弗里曼特尔开始学瑜伽的，居住到澳大利亚西部的时候也都在做练习。

He hadn’t found his own way to it until late in life. Back injury drew him to it. Obviously life had big plans for him and he did find his calling, much to the benefit of his many devoted students. When we had left Perth, life became unsettled for some time. But yoga continued to call. Wherever I was living, I would search for a new class and sometimes join one briefly. But finding a class I could connect with as much as I had Kale’s was in vain. It was not to be found.

> 当我离开了帕斯后，生活有些时候变得不太稳定。但是，瑜伽继续在召唤着我。无论我在哪里生活，我都会寻找一个新的课堂，有时候短期的加入进去，但要找到我当初的启蒙教练那般的却是枉然，已少有有使命感的教练了。

During my time in Stella’s bedroom, I came to see how I hadn’t truly connected with my practice, as I was still looking to the teacher for the connection, instead of myself. This was changed permanently thanks to her guidance. I have enjoyed other classes since, as they push me a bit further than may happen in my home practice. They are also a great way to meet up with like-minded people. But my home practice doesn’t waiver now, as the practice itself is the teacher. Stella had made her mark on her final student.

> 我在斯特拉卧房练习期间，一方面我看看自己是如何没有连接上应该做的动作，而同时，我还在留意找个能衔接起动作的指导老师，而不是靠我自己捉摸来衔接。感谢斯特拉的指点让我永远改变了这个想法。后来，我享受了其他的瑜伽课堂，它们使得我的技能比在家里自己练习时更进一步。走进课堂让我能与志同道合的人们相遇，但是，我仍然不放弃在家里练习，因力练习本身就是老师。斯特拉在她的最后一名学生那里还可以崭露头角，过把瑜伽瘾。

Her biggest frustration was that she was ready to die and it wasn’t happening. I would arrive of a morning and ask how she was feeling. “Well how do you think I am feeling?” she would reply. “I am still here and I don’t want to be.”

> 她感到最为沮丧的是她已经做好死亡的准备，而那一天却迟迟等不来。通常我会在早上到她那里时问她感觉如何。她会回答我说，“那么，你会怎样认我现在的感觉？”或者“我还在这里苦苦煎熬，我不想这样。”

She was also unable to meditate anymore. After all of Stella’s years of mental discipline and the connection she had experienced with herself through meditation, she thought it would be a natural thing now as she approached her return home. In fact, she thought her practice would intensify.

> 她再也不能够做冥想了。斯特拉经过多年的心理训练和通过冥想所经历的自我心身相连使她平静地认为自己现在走向回归之路是一种自然现象。实际上，她觉得她的练习应该增强。

But it was my practice that did. Every afternoon when she drifted off again, I would do my afternoon sit. “You’re so lucky,” she would say to me later. “This is so frustrating. I can’t meditate and I can’t die.” “Perhaps you are still here for me. Perhaps there are still things that I need to learn through you and that is why your time has not yet come,” I suggested.

> 结果反而是我的练习得到了提高。每天下午，当她再次入睡的时候，我会进行我的下午练习。稍后，她会对我说，“你真幸运”“我不能做冥想却不能现在就死去，这太令人沮丧了。”我暗示她说：“或许你是为了我而继续活着。或许我还需要从你那里学到东西，那就是为什么你的时间还未到来的原因。”

She nodded. “I can accept that.” As is always the case when any two people interact though, we were there to learn through each other. When I broached the subject of surrender, Stella began to find more peace within. As I sat beside her bed and spoke of days gone by, of learning to let go, she listened with interest.

> 她点点头说：“我可以接受你的说法。”事情总是这样，当任何两个人在互动的时候，都在相互学习对方。在我提出臣服的主题时，斯特拉开始寻找更多的内心平和。我坐在她的床边，讲述着自己过去的生活，以及如何学会放下，她很有兴趣地听着。

Over the years, I had lived from one leap of faith to the next. I told her how I had hit the road south years earlier with nothing but a full tank of fuel, fifty dollars, and an intention to move somewhere cooler for a while. With a town on the far south coast of New South Wales in mind, I headed off in that general direction. Visiting friends along the way, I found a couple of day’s work, which allowed me to continue the trip.

> 多年来，我生活在从一个信念突变到另一个信念的过程之中。我告诉她早些年我是如何在仅有一缸汽油和50美元的情况下出发向南闯荡，然后打算迁移到凉快一点的地方住一阵子。当我计划好了去新南威尔士州南部远方的一个海岸城镇之后，我向着那个大方向出发了。沿途我拜访了我的一些朋友，并找到了几天的临时工作机会，这样可以继续我的行程。

Having already been so nomadic, I had friends dotted all over the place and it was wonderful to see them again, some I’d not seen for almost a decade. I eventually arrived in the town intended but with little money. A caravan park on the headland had the best view in town, overlooking the mighty Pacific Ocean. So I stopped there for a night.

> 多年的流浪生活使得我的朋友遍布各地，能够再次与他们相见是令人愉悦的。有些朋友大约十年没有见过面。最终，我如期地到达了那个城镇，然而，身上的钱所剩无几。海岬上的大篷车公园是观赏这个城镇风景最好的地点，并可俯瞰浩瀚的太平洋。因此，我在那里停顿了一个晚上。

The back seat of my old jeep had been removed and replaced with a mattress. Curtains went up before I’d hit the road and there it was, I was mobile. Checking out the work opportunities in the town, things looked a bit challenging initially. But it was autumn, my favourite time of year. So I just savoured the perfect weather for a couple of days and did a lot of walking.

> 我的吉普车后座被拿掉了，放了一个床垫。我出发之前，车窗安了窗帘，这就是我的移动屋。我到镇上查看了是否有工作机会，结果看上去有点难度。而那时正是秋天，是我最喜欢的季节。所以，我干脆不去想工作的事，先欣赏几天这里的完美天气再说。于是我到处逛逛，走了许多路。

Paying for my site at the caravan park though, was not going to be possible on an ongoing basis. My money was running out and I was really only there for a shower and as a base, while I made some contacts. So I bought some food and headed into the bush, following signs to an inland river not far away. Having lived by leaps of faith before, I knew I would have to face my fears head-on yet again. If I was going to will something to me through faith alone, I had to get my head out of the way and that is always the hardest thing.

> 虽然我已经支付了大篷车公园的车位，但不可能再维持生存下去了。在我到处寻求工作之时，我的钱快用光了，仅能付一次洗澡费和最基本的生活费用。我买了些食物，往森林走去，跟着路标来到不远的一条内河。以往我是靠信心猛涨而生活，我明白，我必须再次迎头面对自己的恐惧。假如说我将仅以所剩无几的信念支撑我自己的话，我一定要清除头脑里的某些杂念，而那总是最难做到的事。

Unhealthy patterns surfaced in my mind, results from my past conditioning and society telling me I couldn’t live this way. Fear started rearing its ugly head as I wondered how on Earth it was all going to come together, yet again. Bringing myself back to the present moment was the only thing that had saved me before and was the only thing that would save me now. And there is no better place to face your fears than in nature, where you can get back into the true rhythm of life.

> 由我过去的生活环境和指责我不能有这样的生活方式的亲友们所引起的那些种种不健康的想法浮现在了我的脑海。当我想弄明白所有的事情到底是如何再次都撞到一起的时候，恐惧感开始出现了。以前，让自己回到当下是唯一能拯救我的办法，现在也是如此。世上没有哪个地方比大自然能更好地面对个人的恐惧，在大自然面前，你可以真实地回到自然生活节奏当中，没有压力，无拘无束。

When fears were sleeping, I enjoyed wonderful days in a healthy, uncomplicated routine of eating simple, wholesome food, swimming in the cleansing, crystal clear river, watching the curious faces of wildlife come and go, listening to birds with a variety of songs, and reading. It was a reverent time, spacious and beautiful. Almost two weeks went by before I saw another person. The day I did see people was pleasant. It was a family of three generations, out at the river for a picnic lunch.

> 当恐惧还在沉睡之时，我享受了好多天的美好生活：一日三餐有规律地吃着简单且有益健康的食物，在干净而清澈的河水里游泳，观赏着过往野生动物那好奇的面部表情，聆听着林中鸟儿不同的歌声以及放松地阅读各类书籍。这是一段充满虔诚之心的时光，宽松而美丽。大约两周之后，我遇见了另外几个人。那一天我很愉快，这是三代人组成的家庭，当时他们到河边野餐。

This told me it was probably the weekend. I left my jeep open and went for a big bushwalk, giving them the place to enjoy. In the late afternoon I lay in the back of the jeep, still with the back and windows wide open, and read for a while. The beautiful light of dusk filtered magically through the trees.

> 这意味着那天是个周末。为了让他们一家人单独享受野餐的欢乐，我打开吉普车门，独自到树林里去健身步行。傍晚，我躺在吉普车后面阅读了一会儿，车的后门和窗户仍然是大开着的。在那美丽的黄昏时节，夕阳西下，暮色之光神奇地穿过树林，洒在我的车旁。

As the family was leaving, the woman my age, the mother of the two children, broke away from the group. Her husband, parents and children continued to their car. She walked over to me quietly and lent inside my jeep. I looked up from my book a little startled and smiled, as she simply whispered to me “I envy your freedom”. With that we both laughed and she left, without another word or time for me to reply.

> 当这家人要离开的时候，跟我差不多同龄的那位两个孩子的母亲，脱离了大家。她的丈夫、父母和孩子们陆续上车。她悄悄地向我走过来，钻进我的吉普车里面。我被她吓一跳，然后对她微笑，她悄悄对我说了句简短的话，“我嫉妒你的自由”。随后，我们两个都大笑起来，她不容我回答，没有说更多的话就离开了。

Lying in the jeep that night, the curtains open, frogs singing by the river, and a blanket of a million stars keeping me company, I smiled thinking of her. She was right. I was as free as they come. I didn’t have enough money or food beyond the next few days, but right at that moment, I was as free as a person could be.

> 那一夜，我躺在吉普车里面，开着车窗，河边的青蛙在歌唱，天空中百万繁星一直在陪伴着我，我微笑着想到那位母亲的话。她是对的。如同他们自由来往一样，我是自由的人。虽然，接下来的日子里我没有足够的钱或者食物，但此时此刻，作为一个人我是自由的。

People have often asked me since about the various trips I did to the bush and other places in the country, and if I was ever scared for my safety. The answer was no, rarely did I have reason to be. There were a couple of potentially dodgy situations, like the hitch hiking thing. But I came out OK and put those rare occasions down to good learning. As every move was done intuitively, I tried as best to always move forward with trust, knowing I would be looked after.

> 后来，人们常常问我在这个国家不同的森林和地区旅行，对安全问题是否有过害怕。我的回答是没有，但也没有明确的理由。虽然其中出现过两次危险的情况，如上次搭便车的经历，但总的说来都还好，我把那些偶尔的危险状况看成是好的教训。凭着直觉，随着每次迁移的完成，我尽量满怀信心地向前移动，我知道沿途总会有人照顾我的。

We are mostly social creatures though, so I headed into town again. I phoned my mother, with whom I have a healthy and loving relationship. Being a mother, she was always a little concerned for my welfare. But another part of her understood that nomadic life was a part of me. She didn’t judge my choices, but was always relieved to hear from me.

> 既然我们多数都是社会性动物，那我就再次下山到镇上。我给我母亲打了电话，我们之间有着与其他母女一样的亲密关系。作母亲，她总是关心我的幸福和安宁，但是，她也理解流浪生活是我人生的一部分。她不评判我的选择，但总是以听到我的消息而感到宽慰。

The day before she had spent two dollars on a lottery ticket, with the intention of winning me some money. She is such a naturally generous person that life blessed her. “You give to me in so many other ways,” she said. “I insist you take this money. It was with the intention of helping you that it came to me anyway.” So I gratefully found myself with money to get through another couple of weeks.

> 那天，她在买两块钱的彩票之前，祈祷着能为我赢得大奖。她是一个非常自然、大方的女人，是生活赋予了她这种特性。我母亲对我说，“你以太多的其他方式给予了我很多的东西”“我坚持要你拿这些钱，不管怎样，这些钱是出自于我想帮助你而已”。因此，我充满感激之情地发现这些钱可以维持我两个星期的生活。

Waking up in my jeep at the caravan park the next morning, I headed down to the rocks to watch the sunrise over the ocean. I love that first glimpse of light, where there are still stars out but a whole new day is coming. As the sky turned pink then orange, I sat on the rocks watching a pod of playful dolphins swimming by, flipping themselves out of the water for pure delight. I knew then that everything was going to turn out just fine.

> 第二天早上在大篷车公园，我从吉普车中醒来之后，向海边岩石走去，观赏海上日出。我爱第一道曙光，那时的天空仍有星星，但一个全新的日子已经开始。随着天空从粉红色变成橘黄色，我一直坐在岩石旁观看着一群嬉戏的海豚在附近游泳，它们翻出水面纯粹出于快乐。那一刻我顿时明白了，一切都会过去，一切都将变得很好。

After a long and enjoyable chat about life and travel later in the day, the owner of the park returned to my jeep dangling a key. “I don’t need van number eight for another ten days. It is yours and I will not allow you to pay one cent for it. If my daughter was sleeping in the back of her car, I would hope someone would do the same for her,” Ted declared. “Bless you Ted, thank you,” I said fighting back tears of gratitude.

> 当天晚些时候，我跟大篷车公园员主进行了长而愉快的聊天，我们聊了生活与旅游，之后他摇晃着车钥匙回到我的吉普车旁。“我不需要8号车再付十天的钱。这是你的车钥匙，我将不收你的一分钱。如果是我的女儿睡在她的车后座，我也希望有人会这样帮助她。”特德很慎重其事地对我说。我感动得含着眼泪说，“上帝保佑你！特德，谢谢你。”

So I had a roof over my head for the next ten nights and somewhere to cook. During this time though, fears were starting to rage fiercely in me again about my situation. I had to earn some money. My food supply was again diminishing. Each day, I visited all of the businesses in town and while I met plenty of fine people, no work was forthcoming. Walking back up the hill to the headland and caravan, I took a deep breath, trying to stay present, but trying to find a solution too.

> 所以，接下的十天我有了栖身之地和可以煮东西的地方。在这期间，因这种状况，恐惧再次从我心中激烈喷发，我不得不去挣钱了。我的食物供应逐渐变少。每天，我都去拜访镇上的商家们，我虽然遇到了许多好心人帮我寻找，但还是没有现成的工作机会。走回到山岬和大篷车公园，我深深吸了一口气，我试图保持现在的生活方式，也试图找出解决问题的办法。

I hated this part of my life, this compulsion to always throw caution to the wind and create such challenging situations for myself, time and again. Yet it was also addictive. Each time I did this, I challenged my fears head-on and somehow I always, always landed on my feet again. In some ways each leap of faith became harder, as it brought me closer to the core of my deepest fears within.

> 我恨这是我人生的一部分，恨自己一次又一次强制性地豁出去，永远给自己制造如此有挑战的境地。这也是一种成瘾症。每次我这样做，我都是在迎头挑战自己的恐惧心，而在某种程度上，又总是让自己陷入困境。在某些方面，一次次信心的猛涨变得更难，就好像带我越来越更靠近自己内心最深处的恐惧核心。

Yet each leap also became easier. I had tested my faith to the limit on many occasions previously and had gained wisdom and stronger faith in myself through the process. Life also made more sense to me this way, regardless of how hard it was at times. I just didn’t fit in with the way conventional society works. It was at that point, as I watched the high tide wash back out that I remembered the importance of surrender, of letting go and allowing nature to weave its magic.

> 然而，大部分时候，重获信心也变得容易。以前很多次我测试我的信心极限，在其过程中我又获得了智慧和更坚强的信心。无论有时生活怎样的艰难，这种生活方式对我而言都更加有意义。我只是跟传统的社会生活方式有偏差而已。当我看到高高的海潮冲洗着海滩又退却时，我回想起臣服、放下、让大自然施展魅力的重要性，我要说的重点正是这个。

The same force that balances the flow of the tides, the force that sees seasons come and go perfectly, and creates life, was surely capable of bringing the opportunity to me I needed. But I had to let go first. Trying to control the timing and outcome was a terrible waste of energy. My intentions were already out there and I had taken what action I could. My only job now was to get out of the way.

> 平衡潮汐流的力量同样是让不同的季节完美更新以及创造生命的力量，而这个力量无疑带给我所需的机会。但是，我不得不首先放下。试图掌控时间和结局是一种可怕的精力浪费。我的目标和打算已经形成，只差付诸于行动。我现在唯一要做的是摆脱目前的困境。

I laughed gently at myself remembering I had forgotten this. It was a lesson from previous times. When I was out on the very end of a thin, flimsy, bending limb, the only thing to do was surrender and see where I landed. It was time to let go again.

> 我对自己嫣然一笑，哎呀，我差点忘了这一点。这是通过以往多少次的教训得来的经验。每当我处于弹尽粮绝、意志脆弱和痛苦不堪之极，唯一需要做的事就是臣服，看清自己在哪里。此刻，就是该再次放下，臣服于现实生活的时候。

Surrender is not giving up, far from it. Surrender takes an enormous amount of courage. Often we are only capable of doing so when the pain of trying to control the outcome becomes too much to bear. Reaching that point is actually liberating, even if it is not fun. Being able to accept there is absolutely nothing more you can do, other than hand it over to the greater force, is the catalyst that finally opens the flow.

> 臣服不等于放弃，非但如此，臣服需要极大的勇气。通常当我们试图控制某种结果的痛苦变得难以忍受的时候，我们只能去臣服，即使臣服是痛苦的。做到这一点实际上是一种解脱。你现在唯一需要做的事是把能够接受臣服变成一种从内心产生的更加强大的力量。能够接受臣服是最终打开心扉、随遇而安的催化剂。

The next morning I headed down the rocks to the water, where dolphins at play greeted me again at sunrise. I was feeling completely empty and drained after the onslaught of fear, pain, and resistance, finally leading to the surrender. Emotional exhaustion had worn me out. But watching the dolphins, I absorbed the new dawn and slowly, gently, allowed myself to be refuelled by hope.

> 第二天早上，我下山往海边走去，当日出的时候，那些海豚再次在那里游玩，并向我问候。经历了恐惧、痛苦以及抗拒的百般折磨之后，我彻底感到空虚和筋疲力尽。精力疲惫让我烦透了。然而，望着这些海豚被新的黎明曙光所照耀，渐渐地我又重新点燃起新的希望。

In casual conversation with some people on holidays in the caravan park a few days later, a job was offered to me in Melbourne, another seven or so hours south. “Why not?” I thought. I was free to go anywhere and had wanted to live in a cooler climate anyway. Melbourne soon became my favourite Australian city and remains so. But I hadn’t considered moving there at the time and had no idea how much I would benefit from living in such a creative city. It was only through surrendering and staying present that I could allow the job opportunity to flow my way.

> 几天之后，在大篷车公园与一些休假的人闲聊中，我获得了一份在墨尔本的工作机会，这需要往南开车大约7个小时。我想“何不去试试”。无论如何，我可以自由自在地去任何地方，加上也想到更凉爽的地方生活。很快，墨尔本成为了我偏爱的澳大利亚城市，如今依然喜欢它。而当时，我并没有想要搬到那里，也不知道在这个富有创造性的城市里生活能给我带来多少好处。仅仅是因为臣服和保持现在生活方式我才允许工作机会把我带到那里。

As I finished telling my story to Stella, we both smiled. She ate her half a strawberry, agreeing without ego. She had been trying to control the moment of her passing. It was time to surrender that control and as much as she didn’t particularly like the idea, she accepted it might still be some time before her day came. It takes the body nine months to form. Sometimes it takes a little while for it to close down too.

> 当我跟斯特拉讲述完了我的故事后，我们俩都笑了。她吃了半颗草莓，不假思索地认同了我。她正在与死神搏斗，试图控制离开人世的时间。尽管她不特别喜欢臣服这个字眼，但她仍认为该是臣服于认清死亡的时候了，她承认她的大限日子将在某个时间点到来。她的机体衰退已有九个月，有时候还需要一点时间才能最后结束。

By now though, she was very weak and almost off her food entirely. There was no energy to eat but she accepted small pieces of fruit simply to taste things. The day before, it had been two grapes. Today it was half a strawberry.

> 现在她非常虚弱，病入音肓，几乎完全不能进食。尽管她没有了力气去咀嚼正常食物，可她还是接受吃一小块水果，不过那也仅仅是为了尝尝味道。昨天是两颗葡萄，而今天只是半颗草莓。

Her illness should have seen her in a lot of pain, especially for how far along it already was, before she was diagnosed. But there was very little pain, which amazed her doctor. It was mostly exhaustion she experienced as it spread. All of the work she had done on her spiritual journey gave her a very strong connection with her body, now blessing her with being almost pain free. It was also what allowed her a smooth departure when the time came.

> 她的疾病被诊断之前就可以看出她有很多的疼痛，尤其是疼痛永远没有消停过。而现在，疼痛只有一点点，让她的医生都感到惊讶。目前，她多数时候是感到疲惫在全身蔓延。她一生的精神之旅所做的功夫使得她的心身达到非常强大的连接，如今，她幸运的几乎没有了疼痛。这样也使得她能安详地离开人间。

Two or three days previously, I had noticed her fingers had swollen to the point that her wedding ring was now causing deep indentations in her finger. It looked like it was affecting the circulation there. Phoning my employer, I was advised by the nurse that the ring had to come off. With George lying on the bed beside her I worked on the finger with water and soap, gently removing it. It took so long to do and by then, both Stella and George were crying.

> 约两三天前，我注意到了她的手指关节处发生肿胀，她戴结婚戒指的手指出现一条深深的痕印。看上去手指的血液循环受到了影响。我打电话给我的老板，专业护士给我的意见是必须把戒指取下。于是乔治躺在斯特拉的床旁陪伴着她，我用水和肥皂抹在她的手指上，轻轻地取下戒指。这个过程花了很长时间，在这同时，乔治和斯特拉都哭了。

I felt like the devil’s advocate, except that by the time I had succeeded in removing this symbol of their love that had been there for more than half a century, I was crying too. Always such a dear man, George called her by a special, affectionate name that had been a part of their married life for so long. I left the room while they shared a rare moment of private closeness, lying in each other’s arms for perhaps the last time ever.

> 我觉得自己像是一个吹毛求疵的人，而此时，我成功地取下了象征着他们超过半个世纪爱情的戒指，那一刻，我也哭了。乔治是一位非常可爱的人，很长时间了，他总是用一个非常特别的亲切名字来称呼他的太太，这是他们婚姻生活的一部分。我离开了房间，让他们分享一段自斯特拉生病后罕有的私下亲密时光，那或许是他们最后一次手臂相拥躺在床上。

As I stood in the bathroom crying, I felt blessed to witness the depth of love that was between them. It was unlike any I had seen previously. They were true friends and both gentle, considerate people with everyone, and especially with each other. But it was still painful for me, watching them cry as the wedding ring was removed from Stella’s finger forever.

> 当我站在卫生间里哭的时候，我感到很荣幸能够见证他们之间的深厚爱情。这与我以前所见的任何爱情都不一样。他们之间是真正的朋友，都很温柔，很体贴所有的人，尤其是都那么的相互体贴。然而，随着结婚戒指从斯特拉的手指上被取下，看到他们俩伤心地哭着，这对我也是痛苦的体验。

Their son and daughters visited regularly and were there a great deal more now, as time was drawing in. I liked them all. They were very different to each other. But each of them was a decent and lovely person. One of the daughters in particular though, I had grown especially close to.

> 他们的儿子和女儿们定期来看望他们，随着时间的推移，现在更为频繁。我喜欢他们每个人。他们虽然各有其差异，但每个都是正派和可爱的人。其中一个女儿特别好，我们的关系尤其亲近。

A cool change came along unexpectedly one day and I found myself at work without enough layers. George had insisted I put on one of Stella’s cardigans. Both he and Stella then agreed how much it suited me. It was one of those things you wouldn’t normally notice in the shop, as it wasn’t your style.

> 有一天，气候突然地变冷，我在上班的时候没有穿足够的衣服。乔治坚持要我穿上斯特拉的一件羊毛衣。他们两个都认为这件毛衣太合我的身。这是一件在商店里正常情况下不会引你注意的衣服，因为那不符合你的穿衣风格。

But when you put it on, you fell in love with it instantly. On this day the family, including Stella, gave me the cardigan to keep. Years later I still wear it. She had style, our Stella.

> 但是，当你一旦穿上它，你就会立即喜爱上它。那天，他们家所有人，包括斯特拉都希望我可以保留着这件毛衣。多年以后，我依然穿着它。我们的斯特拉穿衣很有品位。

That night, she fell into a coma when I was at home sleeping. I returned the next morning to find a solemn house. George and their son David were there. As the soft breeze blew in through the bedroom door George laid on the bed beside his beautiful wife. His hand held hers, which was now getting cold.

> 那天晚上，当我在自己家里睡觉的时候，斯特拉陷入了昏迷。第二天早上我回到他们那里，整个房子显得很冷峻。乔治和他们的儿子大卫在那里。柔和的微风吹进斯特拉的卧房，乔治半躺在他美丽妻子旁边，他用手握着斯特拉那冰冷的手。

Stella was still alive but in these cases, as death gets closer, the circulation is affected in the extremities. Her feet had also lost their warmth. David sat on a chair holding her other hand. I sat on a chair further down the bed, my hand on her foot. I guess I just needed to touch her too.

> 斯特拉仍然活着，但死神正在靠近她，她的末梢血液循环已经受到影响，她的双脚也失去了温度。大卫坐在床边椅子上，握着母亲的另一只手。我坐在床的另一头，把手放在斯特拉的脚上。我恕我也需要触摸她。

After more than twelve hours of being in a deep coma, Stella opened her eyes and smiled at something towards the ceiling. George sat up. “She’s smiling,” he declared startled. “She’s smiling at something.”

> 进入深昏迷超过十二小时之后，斯特拉睁开了眼睛，望着天花板的地方微笑了。乔治坐了起来。“她在笑，”他惊讶地喊道。“她在为某件事而微笑。”

Stella had no awareness of us anymore. But the smile she gave to whomever or whatever she was looking at, cemented something in me that has never waivered. Having had meditations previously that took me to blissful places well beyond the usual human plane, I had never doubted an afterlife. But looking at Stella’s amazing happiness as she smiled to the ceiling with her eyes open, I knew now with total conviction that nothing would ever sway me from this belief. There is something more to go to, or return to.

> 斯特拉再也没有回应我们了。但是，无论她在对谁微笑，或者无论她在寻找什么，她的微笑坚定了我的某种信念使之永不放弃。当看到斯特拉睁开眼睛望着天花板微笑的那种惊人的快乐感，我十分肯定地明白了，没有任何事能动摇我的这种信念：死亡其实更多的是灵魂出窍。

After she smiled, Stella let out a small sigh, her eyes rolled back and all was quiet. George and David looked to me for confirmation. Having only experienced Ruth’s passing before now I waited for the big breath, which was not forthcoming. “Is she dead? Is she dead?” they asked in despair and heart-broken sorrow.

> 斯特拉在微笑之后，轻轻叹了一口气，她的眼睛回滚了一下，一切都平静了。乔治和大卫期待我的确定。以前仅有鲁思去世的经验，此刻我等待的那种深呼吸还未到来。“她走了吗？她走了吗？”他们带着绝望和心碎的悲伤口气追问我。

I tried to find the pulse in her neck but my own heart was beating so hard, I could only feel that rhythm. I was under immense pressure and had no idea what I was doing. They looked at me desperately. I didn’t want to declare she had died then find her living for another day or two, or even just taking one more big breath. So I prayed for guidance.

> 我试图在她的脖子处找到脉搏，可我的心跳得非常厉害，我只能感受到自己的心跳节律。我在极度的压力之下都不知道自己在干什么。他们绝望地看着我。我不想去立刻宣布她已经去世了，结果发现她还可以再活一到两天，或者仅仅再深呼吸一次。因此，我祈祷有人指导我。

A calm came over me as I looked at her then and I knew she had left. It had been such a smooth, graceful and gentle departure I hadn’t been able to tell. But this wave of love now sweeping through me confirmed she had gone. I nodded, and then George and David left the room immediately. The most heartbreaking sob echoed through the house as George acknowledged his beloved wife was gone. I sat in silence with Stella, as my own tears then also fell.

> 随后，当我仔细查看她的时候，我突然感到很镇静，我知道她已经走了。那是一种我无法用语言来形容的非常安详、优雅和温柔的离去。此刻，爱的浪潮席卷了我去确定她已经离开了人世。我对他们点了点头。乔治和大卫立刻离开了房间。当乔治得知他心爱的妻子去世了之后，最令人心碎的哭泣声在整个房子里回响。我静静地陪伴在斯特拉身边，然后，我自己也潸然泪下。

A couple of hours later, with the rest of the family there and practical details attended to, we said our goodbyes to each other. The morning had now warmed into a very hot day and I was contemplating what to do with myself, really just wanting a superficial distraction. Still driving the same jeep from all of those miles travelled previously, I had to slam the driver’s door to get it closed properly. It had been this way for some time.

> 几个小时后，家里的其他成员也都来了，并商谈具体的处理细节，我们也互相道了别。清晨的气温升高了，这是一个很热的一天，我沉思着自己该去做什么，此时，真的想让自己分散点心思。驾驶着以前跑过许多路程的吉普车，我不得不用力才能关好驾驶车门，以前经常都是这样的。

As I did so this day, the whole driver’s seat window shattered and fell inside the door panels. I sat there staring at it, already numb from the morning’s events and now even more shifted, due to the huge bang that came with the shattering. I looked out of the window, glass free except for a few fragments, and accepted that perhaps the best thing for me was just to go home.

> 而我今天这样用力关门的时候，整个驾驶座的窗户破碎了，碎玻璃往门板内落下。经历了早上发生的一系列事情，我已经麻木了，呆呆地坐在车上盯着这一切，而现在这巨大的猛撞声导致的玻璃粉碎甚至让我更加地心神不定。我看看窗子，没有了玻璃，只有几块碎片还在那里，我承认，也许现在对我来说，最好是回家去。

It took three days for the replacement window to arrive. So I spent those days at home and down by the harbour. I thanked Stella constantly during this time for sending me home. It was the best thing, allowing me to simply be. A couple of months later I received a letter from Therese, the daughter of Stella’s I had grown close to.

> 更新车窗需要三天的时间，所以，这几天我就呆在家里或者到港口走走。我不断地感谢斯特拉在这个期间把我送回了家。能使自己过着简单的生活是最好不过的了。几个月之后，我收到斯特拉另外一个女儿特里萨的信件，我与她的关系比较亲近。

The day after Stella had passed, Therese was walking down the street, naturally thinking of her mum. A huge white cockatoo flew right down in front of her, so close she could feel the wind from its wings. Stella was that sort of woman, capable of sending us signs, and I delighted in reading Therese’s letter.

> 斯特拉去世后的那一天，特里萨向街上走去，自然地想到她的母亲。一只巨大的白色凤头鹦鹉飞下来，越过她的面前，由于离她非常的近，她能够感觉得到那翅膀所刮起的风。“斯特拉就是那种不平凡的女人，能够传送给我们迹象或者预兆。”我很愉快地阅读着特里萨的信。

A year or so went by and I visited the family for dinner. I was looking very forward to the night, especially to see darling George again and to see how he was getting on. Therese and her husband came along too. The evening started out well and it was lovely to hear how George was becoming quite social, playing bridge and other things.

> 一年多过后，我拜访了这个家庭并跟他们一起吃晚餐。我一直都很期待有这样一个晚上，特别是再次看望乔治，看看他现在怎么样了。特里萨和她的丈夫也来了。当晚开始的时候一切都很好，我很高兴地听到乔治如何的成为了社交人士，比如跟别人玩桥牌及参与其他社会活动。

Then somehow the dinner conversation ended up back in the “lie” department. Therese was asking questions as to how different her mum’s passing had been to all of my previous clients, or something along those lines. That was my big chance to come clean and tell them how I had been so inexperienced when caring for Stella.

> 然后，不知怎么的，晚餐上的谈话内容在结束时却回到了“说谎”的那部分。特里萨问起了是否她母亲的过世与我以前那些所有客户的过世有怎样的不同，或者某些事跟一般人去世一样。此刻，是最大的机会去告诉他们当我照顾斯特拉的时候，我怎样的完全缺乏经验。

I really don’t think they would have minded by then, as they were more than happy in the service they had received. But I couldn’t come clean, as George was just so delighted to have me there and kept making a point of how beautiful it was for us all to be together again. It took him back to Stella I am sure. I wanted to get Therese alone that night and tell her the full story, but no chance came up.

> 我真的不认为那时他们会介意，你看他们因我的工作表现，而多么的愉快。看到乔治仅仅因我的到来而特别的开心，并且他还一直强调我们再次相聚对他们全家人来说是多么的美好，因此，我不能够告诉他们真相。我确信，我的出现把他带回到斯特拉身边。那一晚我很想跟特里萨待在一起，然后告诉她整个故事，然而，一直没有机会。

We lost touch soon after that night as life moved on. Some years later though, we all reconnected and I was given the chance to tell the family of my inexperience, and of my regret in not being straight with them from the start. They were beautifully accepting and forgiving about it, saying I more than made up for it with empathy and compassion. They had felt from the start I was the right person to care for their mother, as had I.

> 随着岁月的流逝，那一夜之后，我们很快地失去了联系。经过许多年之后，我们重新又联络上。我有机会告诉了他们我的经验缺乏，以及后悔从一开始就没有直接告诉他们真相。他们很宽容地接受了我的道歉并原谅了我，还说我的同理心和怜悯远比撒谎更为重要。如同我真的那样做到的，他们从一开始就感觉我正是照顾他们母亲最合适的人。

It was lovely to re-connect and remember what we had all shared together. Every winter, I still wear my cardigan and I think of Stella at times. Last winter I was wearing it as I re-read a book she had given me, pausing and smiling at my own memories. This work sure introduced me to some beautiful people.

> 很高兴与他们再次取得联系，并回忆了我们曾经共同度过的日子。每个冬天，我仍然穿上我的那件羊毛衣，并时常想到斯特拉。上一个冬天，我穿着它，当再次阅读一本斯特拉送给我的书时，我停顿了一会儿，回根起那些日子我微笑了。这种工作的确使我结识了一群美好、优秀的人。

But either way, the lying thing was a great lesson. After my time with Stella, I decided I would never again lie to clients. The main thing was that I had learned from it. I was an honest person and regardless of how difficult honesty may be to deliver, it was the only path I would ever walk comfortably. Learning from what happened then allowed me to forgive myself, and this, is the greatest forgiveness of all.

> 但无论哪种方式，撒谎的确是一种教训。与斯特拉相处之后，我决定再也不能对我的客户撒谎。重要的是我从这次教训中成长了。我是一个诚实的人，无论做到诚实有多么的困难，那依然是唯一会让我心安理得并永远走下去的路。从教训中成长，然后原谅自己，这是最大的宽恕。
