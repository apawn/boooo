# True friends · 真正的朋友

The hectic pace of Sydney was wearing me down a bit. No house-sits were on the horizon to keep me there, so I moved down south to experience another chapter in Melbourne. It had been several years since I had left, so it was lovely to be back enjoying the delights of such a wonderfully creative city and to see old friends. My house-sitting reputation also arrived before I did, so my calendar had bookings again in no time.

> 在悉尼的忙忙碌碌让我感到有点疲惫。由于在那里没有了帮人看管房子的机会，我就向南迁移到了墨尔本去体验我的另一段重要的人生经历。自从我上次离开墨尔本已经有好多年了，所以，我很高兴再次回来享受这个如此伟大且具有创造性的城市的种种乐趣，以及再次拜访我的一些老朋友。在我来到这座城市之前，我那帮人看房子的声誉就已经传到这边，所以，我的工作日程很快就再次被安排满了。

The first house I lived in though was the holiday house of Marie, my boss at the pre-natal centre back in Sydney. It was about an hour south of Melbourne, on the lovely Mornington Peninsula and it had her energy all through it, making me feel at home instantly. It was autumn when I arrived and the first couple of weeks were spent walking rugged cliffs as the water lapped below me. Walking long distances while being rugged up in a big coat and hat, with cold ocean winds gusting by, left me feeling very alive. I enjoyed walking like this and did it while I could.

> 我在那里看管的第一栋房子是我原来在悉尼那家产前中心打工时的老板玛丽的度假屋，位于摩宁顿半岛上，从墨尔本往南开车大约一个小时。这所房子充满了玛丽的活力和灵性，让我即刻有一种在自己家的感觉，很温馨。当我到达那里的时候正是秋天，头一两个星期，我每天都在崎岖的悬崖峭壁边漫步，海水在我脚下轻轻地拍打着浪花。我用大衣和帽子把自己紧紧地裹起来，阵阵寒冷的海风从脸上刮过，让我感觉整个人充满了活力。我非常享受这样的漫步，并尽可能花多的时间这样一个人走在崎岖的海岸边。

Then snug inside, sitting by an open fire, my cosy evenings were spent writing and playing guitar. As much as I could have done this forever, income was needed too, which is what brought me to caring for Elizabeth. In some ways, her situation was heartbreaking to me, but I was learning to accept we all have different lessons to learn. What may appear as tragic situations to others, were also great opportunities for growth and learning for the person involved.

> 之后，我就呆在温暖而舒适的屋内，坐在燃着火的壁炉旁。几乎每天晚上我都是在写歌和弹奏吉他的惬意中度过的。如果我希望自己永远过着这样悠闲的生活，那么，我需要有足够的收入才行。生活的需求又把我带到照顾伊丽莎白的工作之中。某种程度上讲，她的情况令我感到心碎，然而，我正在学会接受这样一个事实：我们每个人都有各自不同的经验教训需要吸取。别人身上出现的悲剧也可以是我们成长和学习的极好机会。

Working through my own stuff was teaching me to find the gifts in the learning and I was coming to find a lot of blessings through my past. Many good things were discovered, gifts that could not have come to me had I been raised in a perfect home situation, if such a thing really existed. Strength, forgiveness, compassion, kindness, and many other lessons had been offered to me through my circumstances, all of which I was not only grateful for but were shaping me into a better person every day.

> 我在工作中学会了从吸取的经验教训中挖掘其中的珍贵礼物，这让我在过去的工作中寻找到了很多的福音。我发现了很多在我以前的家庭生长环境里所没有看到过的好东西。我的工作环境我提供了力量、原谅、同情、善良以及其他各种的经验教训。我不仅要感激所有的经验教训，而且那些教训使得我每天都在塑造自己成为一个更完美的人。

So I had to detach a little with the clients and accept I did not know what they were here to learn. For whatever reasons they had attracted the life they had, it wasn’t up to me to save them. I was there to bring them loving care, friendship, acceptance, and gentleness in their final weeks. If this helped them to find their peace, as it sometimes did, then it made my work even more satisfying.

> 然而，我必须跟我的客户们保持一点点距离，我不必打听别人的隐私，不必知道他们在自己的生活中吸取了哪些教训。不管什么原因，他们已经很依恋他们曾经拥有的生活，不需要我来拯救他们。我只是在他们生命的最后几个星期里给他们带去我的关爱、友情、包容和温柔。犹如我平时这样做的那样，假如我能帮助他们寻找到平静，那么，我对自己的工作就会更加满意。

As they say, it is in giving we receive and I was definitely receiving many blessings in this field. Working with the dying was an honour too. Through all of their reminiscing and stories, my own life was being transformed. To be exposed at such an age to the insights they discovered about themselves was an incredible gift.

> 正如人们所说，施也是受，在这个领域中，我绝对地获得了许多的福与受。为临终的人工作也是光荣的。通过他们的全部人生追忆和各种经历，我自己的生活也得到了改变。我这样的年纪就获得了他们一生探索所得来的见解和洞察力简直是难以置信的礼物。

I was already implementing much of the learning from my clients into my own life by now, without having to wait until I too was on my deathbed and then regretting the same things. Arriving at each new client’s home, I was stepping into a whole fresh world of learning for myself, all over again. Each home was a different classroom, with either new lessons on offer or similar lessons from different perspectives. Either way, I was absorbing a great deal.

> 如今，我已经吸取了客户们的很多教训，并在我的生活中借鉴他们的各种经验教训，我不必等到我在临终之时跟他们一样后悔人生的那些事。每当我踏进一个新客户的家，就等于我再次步入了一个能让我学习经验教训的崭新世界。每个家庭都像一个课堂，我在里面可以学到一些新的教训或者一些从不同视角来看的类似教训。不管怎样，反正我正在吸收大量的人生体验。

Elizabeth was not an elderly woman, only about fifty-five. She had been an alcoholic for the past fifteen years and was now dying from an associated illness. While she was still resting on the morning I arrived, her son gave me the run-down of the home and of her condition. He also explained how the family had decided not to tell her she was dying. “Oh boy,” I thought, “here I go again”.

> 伊丽莎白不算是一个老妇人，只有五十五岁而已。过去十五年她曾经是酒精成瘾者，现在因患上酗酒相关疾病而临近死亡。我到达她家的那天早上她还在睡觉，是她的儿子带我进入这个破旧的家并向我介绍她的身体状况的。他也跟我解释了他们家庭成员如何决定不要告诉她已经病入膏肓。“噢，我的天，”我想，“我现又遇到了不要告诉实情的事。”

Through my desire for self-improvement and inner peace, I always tried to live as much as possible in the present moment. In the case of Elizabeth, I realised this was going to be the only way through. If she asked me if she was dying, I would deal with it at the time, rather than wonder how to deal with it in the meantime, accepting that she may never ask – but I wasn’t going to lie to her.

> 由于渴望达到自我提升和内心平静，我总是试图让自己尽可能活在当下。就拿照顾伊丽莎白来说，我意识到我也只能以这样的心态行使我的职责了。如果她向我打听她是否快要不行的话，我会立即应付这个问题，而不是在此期间不知所措，反正我不会对她撒谎，当然，我也接受她可能永远都不会问这个问题，她不问，我也不说。

Confusion and despair surrounded Elizabeth. The family had removed all of the alcohol from inside of the home, locking it in a cupboard in the garage, which they helped themselves to whenever they wished. As she was ill and dying, they decided to remove it from her access completely. This was one of the things I found heartbreaking. She was dying anyway so why put her through the pain of withdrawing, on top of things? But again, it wasn’t my life, nor was it my decision.

> 闲惑和绝望纠缠着伊丽莎白。她的家人将房屋里的所有酒都拿走了，并锁在车库的橱柜里，以便他们自己随时可以拿出来喝。当她病重得快要去世时，他们决定在她的身边完全不可以再出现任何酒的影子。这是我看到的最揪心的事之一。不管怎样，她都是即将离开人世的人，为什么还要让她经受这么重于一切的痛苦？话又说回来，这不是我的生活，也不是我的决定。

Alcoholism in others was something I had been exposed to at too young an age. Then later working in the hospitality industry, on the island and while travelling, I was exposed to it even more. Alcohol doesn’t bring out the best in anyone and not only ruins the goodness of the alcoholic him or herself, it ruins families, friendships, careers, and the innocence of children exposed to it. This is the case with other drug addictions too. The only true thing that brings out the best in anyone is love.

> 我在年轻的时候也曾经让自己置身于酗酒的危险之中。后来在酒吧的工作以及四处旅行使得这一事态变得更为严重。酒精不会让任何人展现出自己好的一面，酒精不仅毁掉酗酒者的良好品质，也使得家庭遭到分裂、友情被破坏，失业和事业上的挫败，或者孩子失去童真。酗酒跟吸毒都属同一类成瘾症。

Alcoholism is also an illness though. And while it is one that can be treated, the sufferer needs ongoing and loving support to break the patterns, and to start believing in themselves and their potential for a better life. Taking a chronic alcoholic off their addiction, with no loving support or explanation, appeared to be a pretty awful thing to me.

> 酗酒同时也是一种病态。其实，酗酒是可以治愈的，在治疗过程中，酗酒者需要不断的爱的支持才能戒掉酗酒行为，与此同时，他们也需要开始相信自己，相信自己有获得更好生活的潜力。对我而言，没有爱的支撑或者理解，要想让慢性酗酒成瘾者戒掉这个恶习似乎是很难办到的事。

All Elizabeth knew was that she was ill. Her energy was exhausted. She needed assistance with almost everything and her appetite was fading. She was also desperately missing the alcohol. The family had only told her that the doctor said to take her off the drink ‘for a while’. It took some strength for me to not judge them, particularly when I saw them sneaking alcohol into their own bodies regularly while denying a dying woman of it. Who was I to say though, what her lessons in life were to be?

> 伊丽莎白所知道的只是自己生病了。她的体力已经开始衰弱，几乎生活中任何事都需要有人协助，她的食欲也日趋降低。她同时也极度地想念酒。她的家人们只告诉她医生说的“暂时”不要喝酒。每当我看到他们在拒绝一位快死的女人喝酒的要求的同时，他们自己却时常偷偷摸摸地把酒灌进肚子的时候，我不得不一再提醒自己，给自己一点力量不要去评判他们。我在这里能说谁呢？

Her general physical weakness would not have allowed Elizabeth to be out and about anymore. The family had also banned some of her friends from visiting, as they were drinkers. It was not surprising then to see Elizabeth experiencing despair and confusion, having had all of her pleasures removed from her.

> 伊丽莎白的身体状况已经不允许她外出和做任何事情了。她的家人也禁止她的某些朋友来探访，因为他们都是酗酒者。我每天都习以常地看到伊丽莎白经历着绝望和困惑，以及她的所有快乐都被剥夺。

She accepted the ban of her drinking friends with quiet resignation, though it had removed more than that from her. Elizabeth had been on the boards of a couple of charitable societies before becoming so ill. These friends were her link to the outside world and to her former life.

> 尽管禁止她的酒友们来探访比禁止她喝酒更力使她难受，可她还是相当顺从地接受了。在她生病之前，伊丽莎白曾经是几个慈善社团的董事会成员。那些老朋友也是她与外界社会联系的纽带以及让她能过着正常的生活的唯一指引。

After six or seven weeks together, her strength was disappearing even more significantly, while her need for rest increased. Elizabeth was quite funny, in an unobtrusive way. Very dry humour came out of her at some of the most unexpected moments. Sometimes one of her remarks would come to mind at home after a shift and I would find myself smiling as I thought of her.

> 我与她呆在一起六七个星期之后，她的精力更加不如从前，越来越需要躺下休息。伊丽莎白其实是一位相当幽默的人，属于那种不引人瞩目的冷幽默者。她那非常冷幽默往往在最令人意想不到的时刻出现。有的时候，我下班回到家后，才不经意想起她刚才说出的某些话很好笑，所以，我经常会独自一人在家里回味她的话。

We had grown to like each other and had established workable routines, within the restraints of her illness. One of those was our cup of tea every morning in the sunroom. It was the nicest room in the house by far and at this time of year, the sun shining in was glorious. It was while in the sunroom one morning that things moved to a new level between us.

> 我们越来越喜欢对方，在她精力和体力所承受的范围内，我们制定了一些可行的常规日程。其中之一就是我们每天上午在日光房内喝茶。到目前为止，在每年的这个季节，这间日光房是该住房里最好的房间，在里面沐浴着灿烂阳光真的是太舒服了。有一天上午，当我们跟往常一样在日光房沐浴阳光之时，我们之间的友好关系提升到了一个新的台阶。

“Bronnie, why do you think I am not getting better? I am not drinking but I am still getting weaker by the day. What do you think?” Elizabeth asked. Looking at her directly and lovingly, I replied tenderly with a couple of questions. “What do you think is the reason? I am sure you’ve thought about it a bit before now?” I was very gentle with her, but needed to know her line of thinking first.

> “邦妮，为什么你认为我的病不会好了？我并没有喝酒呀，可我还是觉得自己一天天变得更加虚弱。你是怎样认为的？”伊丽莎白这样问我。我深情地直视着她，以很温柔的口气问她两个问题来作为我的回答：“你认为会是什么原因？我想在这之前你已经思考过这个问题了吧？”我需要先知道她所猜想的程度。

“I dare not say what I am thinking,” she sighed. “It is too big to grasp. Yet deep down I know the answer anyway.” We sat in silence for a while, watching the birds out of the window, the sun warm on us both. “If I ask you, will you tell me? I really need some honesty here,” she admitted. Lovingly, I nodded.

> “我不敢说我正在想什么。”她叹气地说。“这个问题太大太难去思索，不过不管怎样，事实上我知道答案。”我们俩坐在那里，望着窗外的小鸟，双方沉默了片刻，那时阳光一直温暖着我们。“如果我问你，你将告诉我吗？此刻，我真的需要一些诚实的答案。”她直言不讳地说。我深情地点点头。

“Is it what I think?” she asked, but with the question almost unfinished. I waited, sending her love, seeing if she wanted to go on. She did. “Oh God, it is,” she said, answering herself with a sigh. “I am dying aren’t I? Kicking the proverbial bucket. Flying with the angels. Passing on or over or whatever it is. Dying! I am dying. I’m right, aren’t I?” With a heart tangled up in the bitter-sweetness of her now knowing, I slowly nodded.

> “是我想象的那样吗？”她试着问我，可是问题只有一半。我等待她继续问下去，并温柔地看着她，同时也想看看她是否想要继续提问。她还是继续问了：“噢，我的天，这是…⋯”随后，她唉声叹气地自我回答说：“我快要死了，是不是？就像众所周知的那样两脚一蹬死了，或者跟天使一起飞走了，或者叫过去了、挂了，管它叫什么。死亡！我正走向死亡。我的猜想是对的，是不是？”我的心因她的死亡认知而在苦与甜中纠结着，最后我慢慢地点了点头。

Silently we both sat watching the birds until Elizabeth was ready to talk again. It was a while before that happened, but I had grown used to comfortable silences with my clients. They had so much to think about and to absorb, that sometimes conversation could just get in the way. There was no need to fill the silence at such a time. They would talk when they were ready. After some time, Elizabeth did.

> 我们俩又默默无语的坐在那里望着窗外飞来飞去的鸟儿，我等待着伊丽莎白做好再次谈话的准备。这种事以前经常发生，我早已习惯了跟我的客户们像这样保持一阵舒适的沉默。此刻他们有太多东西需要去思索和吸收，有的时候，我们之间的谈话就只能是这种方式。此时此刻，不必打破沉默，当他们的心理准备好了可以讲话的时候，他们自然会跟你讲话。过了一会儿，伊丽莎白又开始跟我交谈起来。

She said she had suspected this for a while now and how she was frustrated with her family’s lack of honesty. Taking her friends and social life away was cruel she said, to which I somewhat agreed. Elizabeth understood she wasn’t strong enough to go out of the house, but said she would have liked to seen her friends from time to time. Acquaintances dropped in sometimes, people the family approved of and trusted to come without alcohol. They were pleasant people, she said, but there was no closeness.

> 她说她最近一段时间已经对这样的结局有所警觉，并告诉我说她为家人们缺乏诚实而感到多么的沮丧。她还说，把她的朋友拒之门外和剥夺她的社交生活是残酷无情的。对此，在某种程度上我同意她的说法。伊丽莎白明白自己已经体力不支，不能够外出，可是她多么喜欢能时不时地看到她的朋友。有的时候，也有她所认识的人过来看望她，那些都是经过她的家人证实了并相信不会带酒进来的人。她说他们都是可爱而有趣的人，但跟她不是很亲近。

Once we reached this level of honesty, our conversations flowed unhindered. There was no time for holding back. So Elizabeth and I found ourselves enjoying each other’s company more each day. After years of being so withdrawn, I often surprised myself now, by how easily I expressed personal thoughts. With death on her doorstep, Elizabeth too enjoyed the openness of our constant exchanges. Her initial reaction was anger that her family had not told her she was dying. Eventually, it shifted to acceptance. She expressed that the controlling behaviour from her family had possibly been fear based. For that, she was able to forgive them.

> 一旦我们俩达到了如此坦诚的地步，我们的谈话便没有了秘密，变得更加自然和流畅，简直可以说是无法打住。因此，伊丽莎白和我都觉得我们更加享受相互间的相伴相随。经过多年非常孤僻的生活之后，我现在时常对自己感到惊讶：我现在轻易就可以向别人表达内心思想。虽然已经站在了死亡的大门口，伊丽莎白依然很享受我们俩开诚布公地不断交换各自生活的故事和心得的过程。对于家里人没有告诉她临近死亡这件事，最初她很生气，但后来她转变为接受。她表示，他们对她的控制可能是基于害怕，对此，她可以原谅他们。

However, she was unable to pretend that she didn’t know she was dying, and addressed this with them on one of my days off. It brought them all closer, with the family feeling relief that none of them had had to tell her the big news. It was good for me to hear this and not to be under anyone’s wrath for my honesty but they remained fixed. Her drinking friends could only contact her by phone.

> 然而，她不可能假装自己还不知道快要离开人世，有一天我休息，她就向她家人表明了她已经知道了真相这件事。这反而使得他们变得更加亲密了，他们全家人都感到松了一口气，变得都不必冒着风险告诉她这个可怕的消息。我很高兴得知这一切，也不必担心因为我的诚实而遭众人的怒骂。他们之间的关系也保持得很稳定。她的酒友们也能通过电话与她联络了。

Elizabeth was evolving enormously though and became accepting of this, now without resignation. She admitted to me, though wouldn’t admit to her family, it was probably only drinking that had truly held that circle of friends together anyway. Drawing on my own experiences, I told Elizabeth how several years earlier my friendships had changed immensely, when I began extracting my life from the pot-smoking world. It had sorted out who my friends truly were and who were mates simply because we shared a smoke together.

> 如今，她不会一味顺从家人，她脱胎换骨发生了很大的变化，变得完全接受现实。尽管她不会向家人承认，但她向我坦诚了，不管怎样，她酗酒是因为或许仅有在一起喝酒才能真正保持住圈内的朋友们。借鉴我自己的生活经历，我告诉伊丽莎白好几年前当我开始从吸大麻的圈里抽出身的时候，我的友情关系发生了很大的改变。我从所有朋友关系中慢慢清理出哪些是真正的朋友，哪些仅仅是在一起吸毒的伙伴。

Some people who I thought were quite good friends were actually not comfortable around me at all, if I was not stoned with them. It didn’t make anyone a bad person. But when I stopped moving in that world, I saw it was only the smoke that had kept some links joined. Without that, there was no longer a common denominator to hold our friendships together. So we had drifted off naturally, in entirely different directions.

> 我觉得，如果我不跟他们同流合污的话，那些“相当好的朋友”实际上也并不是永远在你的身边让你感到轻松愉快。这个圈子不是让每个人变坏。但是，当我停止融入这个圈子，我才领悟到仅有吸毒才能保持把大家联系起来。一旦停止吸毒就再没有什么共同特性来维系我们之间的友情。因此，我们自然的就分道扬镳，各奔前程。

“I wish I’d stayed in touch with my friends, my real friends,” she said, as I recognised the words from previous people. “My drinking moved me out of those circles and now fifteen years later, there is little to connect on with my old friends. They have all moved away anyway.” While discussing the acquaintances who were allowed to visit, Elizabeth said she wouldn’t really call them ‘friends’. We spoke about how loosely that word is used at times and how there are so many different levels of friends.

> 当我表明我从以前照顾过的客户有些也有认知到了这些话的含义时，她说：“我希望我跟我的朋友，那些真正的朋友一直保持联系。”“我的酗酒行为让我脱离了那些朋友圈子，至今十五年过去了，我跟原来那些老朋友很少联络了。他们都已经搬到另外的地方居住了。”当我们讨论那些允许来探访的熟人的话题时，伊丽莎白说她不会真的称他们为“朋友”。我们谈论了朋友这个词在许多时候被夸大应用以及朋友的不同的层次。

I had begun to think of some of my own ‘friends’ more as warm acquaintances lately. It didn’t mean I thought less of them. They were still a blessing in my life but having been to some pretty dark places in myself by now, I understood what a real friend was. It is easy to have a lot of acquaintances and I did love those people for the enjoyable role we played in each other’s lives. When it comes to the crunch though, not many people can hang around through the very worst of the pain with someone else. Those who do are truly friends.

> 所幸在我的生活中还是得到了上帝的祝福，依然能不断有朋友出现，但至今对于朋友这个词我还是存在某些阴影，我懂得什么才是真正的朋友。想结识很多熟人是不难办到的事，我也喜爱那些人，因为他们在我们彼此的生活中扮演着愉快的角色。然而，一旦出现紧要关头，不是很多人都可以继续留下来与最为痛苦的另外的人相伴左右。而那些能够留下来的人才是真正的朋友。

“It is about having the right friends for the right occasion I suppose,” Elizabeth pondered. “I simply don’t have the right friends for this occasion, for my departure. Do you know what I mean?” Agreeing, I shared with her that while it was not at all such a serious scenario as her situation, I held a very clear memory of such an occasion for me, of missing the right friends for the right occasion. Because of this memory, I could definitely understand that there are different levels of friendships and associations, and sometimes it is a specific quality of friendship we yearn for, rather than just anyone.

> “我想这就等于说在关键的场合成就了关键的朋友。”伊丽莎白琢磨着说。“我就是在这个快要离开人世的关键时刻却没有关键的朋友。你明白我的意思吗？”我表示同意她的说法，并跟她分享了我的亲身经历，虽然不是所有的关键时刻都像她的情况那样严重。我的一次在关键场合里却没有合适朋友出现的体验至今让我记忆犹新。正是因力这种体验，使我能够清楚地明白友情的不同层面。有的时候，我们期盼一种特别的友情，而不是仅仅结识某些人。

Following the years on the island, I worked for a short time in the printing company in Europe. My co-workers were nice people and I appreciated the opportunities that were presented to me, opening my world up even more. The island community had been like a family though. Whenever any of us went away, on a holiday to the mainland for instance, we would all say how beautiful it was to come home to our family on the island.

> 在那个岛上生活的几年期间，我曾经还在欧洲一家印刷公司短期工作过一阵。我的同事们都是很友好的人，我感激有机会与他们共事，并让我的世界变得更加开放。这个岛屿社区就像一个大家庭。比如，无论什么时候，只要我们任何人离开岛屿到大陆去度假，我们都会说再次回到这个岛上大家庭是多么的美妙。

New friends were made in Europe, though in hindsight I would now call them pleasant acquaintances. Through these people, I ended up on a trip across a couple of countries, to the Italian Alps with three others around my age. We had rented a cabin high up in the Alps, with no electricity or running water. It was gorgeous and unlike any terrain of my beloved Australia, which has its own magnificence, but is hugely different. So I found the Alps overwhelmingly beautiful.

> 在欧洲我结交了新朋友，如今，我会称他们为快乐的泛泛之交的熟人。通过这些人，我最后完成了一次跨越几个国家的旅行，那是同三位跟我年龄相当的人一起出游意大利的阿尔卑斯山。我们在阿尔卑斯山高地租了一间小木屋，那里没有电和自来水。但那里的风景非常的壮观，与我们可爱的澳大利亚的任何地形的所有的风貌都不同，那是一种压倒一切的超级美！

Bathing was done in a flow of water running down the mountain. Though it was summer, the water was freezing. It was really just melting snow forming a downhill stream. As the water rushed down all around me I sat in its flow, gasping for breath. All the while though, I was still enjoying the magnificent views, and feeling invigorated. But the water was absolutely freezing, biting as it hit me, rushing past.

> 我们在山间溪水里洗澡。虽然是夏天，但水依然很凉，那些水都是山上的冰雪融化之后形成的。我坐在流水的中央，大口地呼吸，尽情地让那凉悠悠的清水在我身边流淌。自始至终，我充分地享受着这壮丽的风光，感觉精力充沛，精神焕发。虽然这里的水绝对的冰冷，在我身边奔流而过，像根根针刺扎在我的骨头里。

Whenever I had found the courage to swim in a freezing river or ocean, I’d always felt a bit playful after, a bit like a dog after they’ve had a bath. They rush around like mad, all crazy and energetic, regardless of whether they’ve enjoyed the bath or not. That’s sort of what bathing in this freezing mountain stream did to me. It left me feeling ridiculously silly afterwards.

> 我喜爱游泳，特别是在冰凉的河水或海水里游泳。每当我游泳之后，我感觉自己有点像爱玩耍的小女孩，如同一条狗在洗完澡之后那样兴奋，到处奔跑，像疯子一样，疯狂而精力充沛，完全不在乎它们是否享受了洗澡的过程。我在冰凉的山水溪流里洗澡的状况就是这样。每次洗完澡之后都让我感到自己是多么的可笑和愚蠢。

So I was a bit off my head with excitement and playfulness after drying off, getting dressed, and returning to the cabin. Carrying on with good-natured humour, entertaining myself immensely, and sharing silly anecdotes with my new friends, I realised every single one of my jokes was totally lost on them. The worried smiles that said, “What is she on about?” told me so in a second. Their puzzled faces sort of just cracked me up even more. At least I was enjoying the jokes anyway.

> 随后我擦干身体，穿上衣服，回到那间小木屋，我因兴奋和顽皮有点神志恍惚。回到房间，我又继续我的善意幽默，一方面自找乐子，另一方面也跟我的新朋友们分享一些愚蠢的轶事和趣闻。我发觉他们完全不明白我的每一个笑话。每次我讲完笑话后数秒钟，他们都面带微笑却忧愁地说：“她在说什么呀？”他们那种疑惑的表情让我更加觉得好笑，不管怎样，至少我是享受了这些笑话。

They were happy and lovely people. It was just that the humour of our cultures was so different. In an instant, I missed my old friends with painful longing. They would not only have related to the silliness but would have been cracking up with me by now, adding their own jokes, turning it all into an even greater laugh. That evening, after a massive afternoon hike to the top of a mountain, we all sat by the light of lanterns as we ate and chatted a while. It was nice.

> 我的新朋友都是快乐且可爱的人，只是我们之间的幽默文化非常的不同而已。这期间，我痛苦地思念起我的一些老朋友，他们不仅会跟我一样有可笑滑稽的行为，而且还会跟我一起咧嘴大笑，然后，他们再添加一些他们自己的笑话，最后变成了我们一起狂笑不止。那天下午，我们徒步很长时间到达了一座山的山顶，晚上，我们围绕着坐在几盏灯旁，边吃东西边聊天。那种感觉真好。

Not long after though, everyone retired for the night, everyone except for me. The hike had been amazing and I was still in a jubilant mood. Really, all I wanted to be doing was sitting around with friends and just generally having a laugh, to wind up a fantastic day. Certainly I didn’t want to be going to bed yet anyway.

> 很快的，除了我，他们每个人都想睡觉了。下午的长距离徒步太令人惊叹，我的心情还处在兴高采烈之中。我此时此刻真的是希望有几个朋友坐在一块，一起说说笑笑，重温这美妙的一天。我现在的确一点睡意也没有。

But all was now quiet in the hut as my friends slept. Carrying a lantern into my little room, I placed it on the table and spent the next two hours writing. In the distance I heard bells ringing as cows moved about in the night. I smiled in bliss that here I was, in a gorgeous little cabin, writing by lantern light high up in the Alps, and listening to sound of distant cow bells. It was a world away from my own world, and while I was overwhelmed by the peace of the moment, it made me miss my old friends terribly.

> 我的朋友们都憨憨睡着了，整间小木屋非常的安静。我拿了一盏灯进入我那小小的房间，把灯放在了桌子上，又写作了两个钟头。这时我听到遥远的地方传来一阵铃铛声，好像是牛群在夜幕中行走。我微笑了，并沉浸在眼前的幸福之中：一栋华丽的小木屋，在阿尔卑斯山高地的油灯下写作，聆听着远方传来的牛铃声。我感觉以往的世界已经消失，此刻我只活在自己的世界里。当下的宁静和平和使我突然感到不能自持，让我好想念我的老朋友。

It was a perfect night but with the wrong people. There were plenty of reasons to like each of my friends on this trip and I did. I was experiencing a very special moment in myself though and I wanted to be sharing it with the right people, with friends who truly knew me. Of course, this was never going to happen. So I savoured the blessing of the moment all to myself instead.

> 这是一个完美的夜晚，而伴随着我的却是不合适的人。有充足的理由可以说明这次旅行我和这几位朋友就是不对牌。我那时正在经历一段非常特别的时刻，很想与合适的人一起分享，尤其与了解我的朋友分享。当然，这是永远办不到的。所以，我只有独自一人在这里品赏着这一刻难得的幸福。

I knew what Elizabeth was now talking about when she said she wished she had the right friends around. Sometimes there are just particular people who understand you, no matter what – and these are old friends. It was like that for me that night in the Alps and it was like that now for Elizabeth as she began accepting that her life was coming to a close.

> 现在我明白了伊丽莎白所说的她希望有合适的朋友在身边的含义。有些时候，无论发生什么事，你尤其只想有可以理解你的特殊朋友，也就是老朋友在你的身边。对我来说，像阿尔卑斯山的那个夜晚就是如此。现在对伊丽莎白来说，当她开始接受自己的生命即将结束的时候，也多么希望有老朋友在身边。

When her doctor came to visit, I asked him privately if it would make any difference to Elizabeth’s condition if she were still drinking. He shook his head. “No, she’s on the home straight now regardless. I told her family if she wants a little brandy of an evening to allow it. Are they not?” he asked me. I shook my head. He restated it would make little difference now.

> 有一天，当她的医生过来看望她的时候，我私下询问他，假如伊丽莎白还在喝酒是否对她的身体状况造成不一样的后果。他摇头回答说：“不会的，不管怎样，她现在已经踏入死亡的道路。我已经告诉她的家人如果在晚上她想喝一点点白兰地的话，可以让她喝。他们没有让她喝吗？”他反倒问我。我摇了摇头。他又向她的家人重新提出了这个建议，现在可能她的家人会同意她喝酒了吧。

Later I spoke to her family quietly about it. But again, it had been a family decision and no, they would definitely not be giving her any drink. They then went onto explain why. It seems that the Elizabeth I was spending time with and the Elizabeth they knew when she had been drinking were two entirely different personalities. In fact, they couldn’t believe what a pleasant person she was again now, as they hadn’t seen this side of her for at least fifteen years.

> 晚些时候，我轻轻的对她的家人提到这个事。结果还是那样，不行，这是家庭的决定，他们绝对不会给她任何酒喝。稍后他们给我说明了原因。那一刻我所认识的伊丽莎白和他们知道的酗酒后的伊丽莎白完全是两个截然不同的人。事实上，他们不可能相信她现在还会变回愉快的人，因为他们至少有十五年没有看到她的这一面。

Over the next couple of weeks, I asked her more about her drinking habit, if she brought it up. Elizabeth said that as much as she was still craving it now, she was kind of glad too to be able to remember who she was before alcohol had taken over her life. It had begun easily enough. She’d always had a few wines with the family at dinner and had done so for years with no problem.

> 在接下来的几个星期里，我向她询问了更多有关她的酗酒的事，比如，是否酒后会吐等等。伊丽莎白说现在她还是很渴望喝酒，也很高兴能够记得她在酗酒之前的她。她在很早以前就开始喝酒了。在与家人共进晚餐时，她总是要喝上几杯，许多年都这样过了，没有任何问题。

Then she became active socially, being on the boards of various charities. She admitted that many people she met through these scenes did not drink excessively at all, but she had been drawn to those who did. She didn’t feel noticed at home anymore. But felt like her presence mattered to these new friends. Now that she was feeling clearer, she realised they were all just as needy as her, all needing validation through this circle of friends and their drinking.

> 后来她成为了社会活动家，担当几个慈善团体的董事。她承认，她在这些机构所认识的许多人大都不会过量饮酒。然而，她却被那些酗酒者给拖下了水。她再也不觉得在家里受到重视，反而觉得她的新朋友们比较在意她。如今她感觉清醒多了，并意识到她的那些新朋友只是需要她而已，而她也需要通过这个圈子的朋友和跟他们饮酒来认同白己。

Elizabeth said alcohol gave her confidence, or when she was drunk she thought it did, but she became outspoken, loud, and eventually quite bitter and nasty to others. This is what had made her lose her original circle of old friends. They had tried to reach her with love and support, trying to help her see her own demise, something they were watching with heartache, but she was arrogant with them all, eventually pushing every last one of them away.

> 伊丽莎白说酒精给了她自信，或者说当她喝醉了的时候是这样认为的，可是，她也变成了心急口快和大声讲话的人，最后是相当苦涩和厌恶他人。这就使得她失去了最早那个圈子里的老朋友。他们也曾经尽量给她爱护和支持，试图让她认清自己的堕落，他们看到她这样都觉得痛心。但是，她对他们所有人都很傲慢，最终，她把他们一个个都推开了。

This only validated to her drunken mind just how loyal her new friends were who didn’t judge her for her drinking habit. Of course this was because they were drinking too. The other reasoning for her drinking that she had justified to herself during these years, was that at least now her family noticed her. While it may not have been in a positive way, at least she didn’t feel ignored like she used to, before she had started drinking excessively. Her loss of control ensured they had to start paying attention.

> 唯有她那喝得醉醺醺的心智认同她，她认为她的新朋友多么地忠实于她，他们没有评判她的饮酒习惯。当然的，因为他们也饮酒。这些年，她已经自我证明了让她酗酒的另外一个原因就是至少这样可以引起她的家人对她的关注。虽然这不是一种正面积极的方式，但至少她没有感觉被忽视，在她刚开始过量饮酒之前，家里的人时常不是那么关心她。她失控的饮酒的确开始引起了他们的关注。

The more Elizabeth’s capabilities declined due to alcoholism, the more her family had to help her and the worse she eventually began to feel. It had begun with her enjoying their attention. But in the end, she was incapable of helping herself and this lack of control left her feeling even more insecure and negative about who she now was. So while in the early days she could see she was hurt about her family not valuing her presence or opinion, in the end, she came to truly depend on them and hated herself for it. This just perpetuated the cycle of low self-esteem even more.

> 伊丽莎白越是因酗酒而能力下降，她的家人就越是不得不帮助她，最后，她开始感到越来越糟糕。当初，她很享受他们的关注，可到了后来，她已经无法自助，缺乏控制力让她感觉更加没有安全感和更加自暴自弃。因此，早期的时候，她还能够看得出因她的家人不重视她的存在和意见而使她受到伤害，但是到了后来，她变得真正的依赖他们，并因此憎恨自己。而这正导致她的自卑感更加严重，完全不能自拔。“你知道的，邦妮，不是每个人都希望自己恢复正常。

“You know, not everyone wants to get well either Bronnie. And for a long time I didn’t. The role of the sick person gave me an identity. Obviously I was holding myself back from being a better person this way. But I was getting attention, and trying to fool myself this made me happier than being courageous and well.” This admission from Elizabeth was the hindsight of a woman who was now on a fast track to wisdom. Being dry for almost three months and facing the fact that she was dying was changing her enormously.

> 很长一段时间我都没有恢复。扮演病人的角色给了我一个特定的身份。显然，这样的方式阻碍了自己成为一个正常的人。从此，我得到了关注，并尽量愚弄自己，好像这样比勇敢和好转更为快乐。”伊丽莎白坦白地表达了她的后见之明。禁酒了差不多三个月以及临近死亡的现实让她整个人发生了巨大的变化。

Knowing Elizabeth’s full and honest story about her addiction also helped me to understand her and her family more. In the end, their severe actions had actually helped her to become a better person again. While I may not have done it in such a closed and secretive fashion, I came to respect that they were really trying to help her and themselves. And they were successful in doing so. Part of their success too though, was Elizabeth herself. Facing her death had made her look at life very differently and she had courageously embraced her learning.

> 全面了解了伊丽莎白的真实酒精成瘾故事也帮助了我更加理解她和她的家人。到最后，她家人的强硬行动实际上也帮助她重新变成一个好人。我可能没有做过这种如此极端的事，但我还是敬佩他们真心的试图帮助她和帮助他们自己的勇气和毅力。他们成功地做到了。他们一部分的成功还在于伊丽莎白自己。面对死亡使得她可以从完全不同的角度审视自己的生活，并勇敢地学习人生的经验教训。

During her last two weeks, I watched some extraordinary healing unfold between Elizabeth and her family. One of the most beautiful things I was learning through palliative care was to never underestimate anyone’s capacity for learning. The peace I witnessed Elizabeth find was something I had also seen in previous clients. It was a very gratifying thing.

> 在她最后的两个星期里，我观察到了一些非凡的伤痛愈合表现出现在了伊丽莎白和她家人之间。通过姑息护理我学到的最好的东西之一是永远不要低估一个人的学习能力。我见证了伊丽莎白终于寻找到了平和和宁静，以前我在我的客户那里也见证过这样的平和。这是非常值得感恩的事情。

About a week before she died, I spoke with her husband and one of her sons about Elizabeth’s regret of losing her old friends and wondered if it might not be too late to reach some of them, even if they could just speak on the phone. By now, there was no concern about friends sneaking alcohol into Elizabeth. It was the least of everyone’s concerns. Her comfort was all that mattered now and as the family had healed so much, they immediately jumped to the idea.

> 大约在她去世前一个星期，我跟她的丈夫和其中一个儿子讲了伊丽莎白后悔失去了她的老朋友，我想知道是否能联系上他们，就算他们之间仅仅是通个电话也可以。如今，没有必要担心她的朋友会偷偷摸摸带酒给伊丽莎白，这已经是最不重要的担忧，现在她的安慰和舒适才是最为需要考虑的。随着她家人的态度的大改变，他们立刻同意并实施了这个建议。

A couple of days later, two beautiful, healthy and lovely women came in to Elizabeth’s room, just after I had sat her up comfortably offering her some tea. One of them was now living in the mountains outside of the city, about an hour away. The other had flown to Melbourne from the Sunshine Coast in Queensland, as soon as she had heard the news. Now they sat around Elizabeth’s bed, talking with her, holding hands and smiling.

> 几天之后，当我刚让伊丽莎白舒适地坐好并给她提供一些茶水之后，两个漂亮、健康、可爱的女士来到了她的房间。其中一位现住在这座城市的山区，大约一个钟头的距离。另外一位当她得知伊丽莎白的消息后很快就从昆士兰州的阳光海岸赶到了墨尔本。现在她们都坐在伊丽莎白的床边，握住她的手，微笑着跟她聊天。

Leaving them to their privacy and me to a quiet tear of joy, I left the room. As I was doing so though, I heard Elizabeth apologise to them both and their instant forgiveness returned. It was past. It didn’t matter they said. Her husband Roger and I sat out in the kitchen, both of us teary but delighted.

> 我离开了房间，让她们单独呆在一起，我悄悄地流出喜悦的泪水。在我离开房间的时候，我听到伊丽莎白向她们两个道歉，她们也不断地表示原谅。她们说这都是过去的事了，不必放在心上。伊丽莎白的丈夫罗杰和我坐在外面的厨房里，我们两个都在落泪，但是都很高兴。

The friends stayed for a couple of hours, by which time Elizabeth was both exhilarated and completely exhausted. She fell into a deep sleep instantly and I had no chance to chat with her before going home. When I returned a couple of days later, she was very weak, but wanted to talk.
