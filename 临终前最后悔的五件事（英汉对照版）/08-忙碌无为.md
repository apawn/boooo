# Regret 2: I wish I hadn’t worked so hard · 忙碌无为

Drying the dishes, I could hear my client John in his office giggling like a schoolboy. “Yes, she’s just the right age too,” he chuckled, continuing to describe me to his friend on the phone. John was almost ninety. I was still in my thirties. Recalling a line a seventy-year old man had once told me, ‘all men are boys,’ I smiled to myself shaking my head.

> 我正在厨房擦干刚洗过的碗盘，听到我的客户约翰在他的书房里像个小男孩一样咯咯地笑。“是的，她正好是这个年龄。”他轻轻地又笑了，接着继续在电话上对他的朋友描述我。约翰差不多九十岁，我是三十多岁。回想起以前有位七十岁的老先生曾经对我说，“每个男人都是男童。”我摇了摇头自己对自己微笑。

Coming out of his office later, John was the diplomatic gentleman I knew, with no sign at all of his mischief. He wanted to take me out to lunch though and did I have a pink dress to wear? If not, could he buy me one? I laughed and politely declined his offer of buying me a dress, because I did in fact own a pink one.

> 稍后，他假装若无其事的样子（一点看不出他刚才的调皮）走出了书房，我了解约翰是一个有外交手腕的绅士。随后，他邀请我跟他一起到外面共进午餐。我开玩笑地说，我有一条粉红色的裙子穿吗？如果没有的话，他可以帮我买一条吗？我大笑起来，并婉言谢绝了他答应给我买裙子，因为事实上我有一条粉红色裙子。

While it was not a part of my uniform for care work, I informed him I was happy to oblige an old dying man. His delight was wonderful. A table for two was booked at a very expensive restaurant. It was the prime table, front and centre overlooking a park across to the harbour.

> 那不是我的工作服，没有必要由他买。我告诉他我很乐意满足一位垂暮的老人。他那么兴奋的样子美妙极了。他在一间相当昂贵的餐馆订了一张只有两把椅子的位子。我们的位子非常好，中间靠前面，可以俯瞰着窗外公园对面的港湾。

John looked dapper in his navy jacket with gold trimmings, a fresh dose of aftershave lingering in the air. With his hand on the small of my back, he guided me to our table. After looking out at the view, I glanced back to catch him winking to the four men sitting at a table nearby. They were all giggling while checking me out, but instantly dropped to straight faces when they realised they were busted.

> 约翰身穿一件海军夹克，上面配有金色装饰，看上去英俊潇洒，一股刚喷洒的剃须后搽面香液的味道在空气中飘散。他将手扶着我的背，把我领到我们的餐桌旁。观赏了一阵外面的风景之后，我转回视线看到了他正在跟对坐在我们桌子旁边的另外四位男士眨眼睛。他们都一边打量着我，一边咯咯地笑，当他们察觉我看到了他们的时候，所有的脸立刻变得面无表情、一本正经的样子。

“Friends of yours then, John?” I asked smiling. He stammered and admitted that he wanted his friends to see how lucky he was to have a carer of such great physical quality. I cracked up laughing. “Any woman my age is of great physical quality to a room full of eighty-nine year olds.”

> “约翰，那都是你的朋友吗？”我微笑着问他。他支支吾吾的承认了，他想让他的朋友们看看他是多么的幸运有了一位如此年轻健康的看护者。我咧嘴大笑起来。“对满屋子的八九十岁的老年人来说，任何一个像我这样年龄的女人都是非常年轻健康的。

I must admit though, his manners were impeccable and I did wish more men from my generation still carried the charm and dinner etiquette he bestowed upon me. We had a lovely lunch. John had phoned ahead and explained he was bringing a vegan to the restaurant. They had obliged with a beautiful vegetable loaf, especially baked.

> “我必须承认，他的行为举止是不可挑剔的，我倒希望更多的与我同辈的男士们能拥有这样的魅力以及像他那样给予我的正餐礼仪。我们愉快地开始吃午餐。约翰提前给餐馆打了电话，说明会带一位素食者过来。他们满足了我们的要求做了漂亮的素食面包。

It turns out that his friends were all banned from interrupting our lunch or even coming to the table. He would introduce me afterwards. So while their lunch had long finished, they all sat patiently until John and I had finished our lunch and conversation. Then, again with his hand on the small of my back, he guided me to their table where I played the perfect girlfriend by charming them all, but ensuring John received the most attention. He reminded me of a rooster with all of his feathers up in pride and mating. It was a fun time.

> 结果，他的朋友们被他限制不允许来打扰我们的午餐，甚至不准走到我们的桌子边来。他说吃完饭后会把我介绍给他们。因此，尽管他们几个早已吃完午餐，也只能耐心地坐在那里一直等候约翰和我吃完饭和聊完天。随后，他又再次用手扶着我的背把我领到他的朋友们的桌子旁，我把他们都迷住了，成功扮演了一个完美女朋友的角色，而值得高兴的是约翰赢得了最大的关注。他让我联想到一只公鸡骄傲地竖立起它的全身羽毛，等待交配。我跟他度过了有趣的时光。

Underneath all of this though, there was a man dying. What harm did it do to oblige such a harmless game in what would be one of his last outings? Once home and out of my pink dress into more practical work clothes, much to John’s disappointment, I assisted him to bed. The outing may have delighted him, but he was exhausted too.

> 被眼前所有的快乐掩盖的事实是这个男人正临近死亡。用无伤大雅的游戏去满足一位临终老人的愿望有什么不妥？这可能是他力数不多的最后的外出活动机会。我们到家后，我脱掉我的粉红色裙子，换上了工作服，这很让约翰失望，我随后帮助他上床午睡。外出活动虽然给他带来快乐，但是也让他感到疲惫。

The energy of dying people is so weakened that a small outing becomes like working an eighty-hour week lifting bricks. It completely drains them. Family and friends too don’t often realise how their well-intended visits can exhaust sick people. When they are in their last week or so, visits of more than five or ten minutes can become hard work for the patient, yet this is when they are usually bombarded with visitors.

> 即将走向生命尽头的人的体力非常的虚弱，以至于一次小小的外出活动就好像让他们一个星期工作了八十小时的搬砖重活，完全把他们的体力榨干了。家人和朋友们也不总是明白，他们自以准备得很好的探访可以把生病的人累倒。尤其当病人们处在他们生命的最后时期，5~10分钟的探访也可能让病人倍感精疲力尽，所以，每当这时，他们就要轰赶探访者。

It was just John and I this afternoon though and he slept deeply. Folding my pink dress away in my bag, it was lovely to have brought him the pleasure he gained from the lunch. It had brought me pleasure too. John had also benefitted from my youth in other ways. Understanding computers better than he did, I returned to the job in his office that had begun the previous month.

> 这个下午只有约翰和我在一起，他睡着了。我把我的粉红色裙子折叠好放进了包里，我很高兴我们这次午餐带给他快乐。其实，我也同样获得了愉快。在其他方面，我的年轻也给约翰带去许多实惠。我比他更加懂得电脑，我回到他的书房帮他整理电脑中的文件，从一月以前我开始这个活计。

For a man of his age, his approach to computers was admirable as he embarked on grasping the age of technology. His files were a mess though, as he hadn’t known about folders and ordered filing. While he slept, I carried on creating categories and finding homes for hundreds of documents, all the while creating an index so things could be found. But like I say, for a man his age, he was already doing remarkably well on the computer.

> 像他这么大岁数的男人还能使用电脑，抓紧跟上时代的确是值得钦佩的。他电脑里的文件乱七八糟，他不懂什么是文件夹和文件排序。在他睡觉的时候，我帮他把几百份的文件分类和放入到首页的位置，并编辑一个索引，这样所有的东西都很容易被找出来。然而，就像我说的，对一个他这么大年纪的人来讲，在电脑方面，他已经做得非常棒了。

When I saw the deterioration in John the following week, I felt very grateful we had already been out to lunch. He would not be leaving the house again. There may still be a few weeks to go, or maybe not, but his strength was disappearing very quickly. Sitting out on his balcony late that afternoon, we watched the sun set over the Harbour Bridge and the Opera House.

> 接下来的一个星期，当我看到约翰的病情在恶化时，我感到唯一庆幸的是，我们已经外出午餐过了。他不会再有可能外出了。他也许还有几周的时间就要离去，或者还不到几眉。他的体力正在快速地消逝。那个傍晚坐在他家的阳台外面，我们欣赏了港湾大桥和歌剧院那边的日落。

John was in his dressing gown and slippers, trying to eat a little, but struggling. “Don’t worry about it John, only eat what you can or want to,” I said, as we both knew the unspoken words behind that sentence. John was dying and it wouldn’t be too far away. Nodding, he put the fork on the plate, handing them to me. I sat the tray aside and we continued to watch the sunset.

> 约翰穿着睡衣和拖鞋，他试图要吃一点东西，但又没有食欲。“不必担心，约翰，只吃一点你可以吃或者想吃的东西。”我对他这样说。我们双方都明白这句话里面所隐藏的含义是什么。约翰临近死亡，那个日子不会太远了。他点了点头，把叉子放进盘子里，然后全部递给了我。我坐在旁边手拿着托盘，我们继续观赏日落。

Out of the afternoon peace John stated, “I wish I hadn’t worked so hard Bronnie. What a stupid fool I was”. Sitting in the other lounge chair on the balcony, I looked across at him. He needed no encouragement to continue. “I worked too damn hard and now I am a lonely, dying man. The worst part is that I have been lonely for the whole of my retirement and I need not have been.” I listened as he told me the whole story.

> 后来，约翰打破了傍晚的宁静，他说，“邦妮，我真希望从前不是拼命在工作。我当初太愚蠢了。”我坐在阳台的另外一张休闲椅上，我望着他。不必我敦促，他继续地说，“我以前太努力工作，如今成了一个孤单的快要死的人。最糟糕的是我的整个退休生活一直是孤独的，我不想要这样。”我聆听了他的全部故事。

John and Margaret had raised five children, four of whom now had children of their own. The other one had died in his early thirties. When all of the children were adults and gone from the home, Margaret asked John to retire. They were both fit and healthy and had enough money behind them to retire well.

> 约翰和玛格丽特养育了五个孩子，其中四个已经成家有了自己的孩子。另外一个孩子在他三十多岁的时候就去世了。所有的孩子长大成人后都先后离开了家，玛格丽特开始要求约翰退休。当时他们两个人身体都很健康，也有足够的钱过好退休生活。

But he always said they might need more. Margaret replied each time that they could sell their huge, now almost empty house and buy something more suitable if required, freeing up more money. For fifteen years this battle went on between them, while he kept working.

> 而他总是说他们可能需要更多的钱。每次玛格丽特都会申辩说，他们可以卖掉他们这套如今几乎空着的大房子，如果有必要的话，另外买一套更适合他们两个人住的房子，这样就可以有更多的现金在手上使用。他们俩的这种战争一直持续了15年，而约翰则继续他的工作。

Margaret was lonely and longed to discover their partnership again without children or work. For years she devoured travel brochures, suggesting different countries and regions to visit. John also had a desire to travel more and agreed with wherever Margaret suggested. Unfortunately, he also enjoyed the status his work gave him. He told me he didn’t particularly like the work itself, just the role it gave him in society and amongst his friends. The chase of closing a deal had also become a bit of an addiction for him.

> 没有孩子和工作，玛格丽特在孤独和寂寞中继续去维持他们俩的夫妻关系。多少年来，她收集各种不同的旅游册子，提议到不同的国家和地区去观光。约翰也有愿望去更多的地方旅游，也同意玛格丽特所提议的任何地方。不幸的是，他也享受目前的工作状态。他告诉我，他不是特别喜欢工作本身，对他而言，拼命追逐完成交易成了一种癖好。

One evening with Margaret in tears, begging him to finally retire, he looked at this beautiful woman and realised that not only was she desperately lonely for his company, but they were both old people now. This wonderful woman had waited so patiently for him to retire. Looking at her, she was still as beautiful as the day he had met her. But it was the first time in his life John considered they were not going to live forever.

> 一天晚上，玛格丽特哭着求他停止工作，看着他的漂亮太太，他承认不仅是她很孤独拼死要他陪伴，而且他们俩现在都已经老了。这个绝好的太太已经很有耐心地在等待他的退休。望着他的太太，他感觉她依然跟他第一天遇见她的时候一样美丽。此时，约翰生平第一次思考了他们不会永远活着的问题。

Although petrified for reasons he could now not justify, he agreed to retire. Margaret had jumped up and hugged him, her tears switching from sadness to joy. But the smile didn’t last long, disappearing the minute he added “in one more year.”

> 虽然他找出的那些理由不能自圆其说，他还是同意退休。玛格丽特兴奋得跳了起来，并紧紧地拥抱约翰，她从悲伤而落泪马上转变成高兴而落泪。可是，玛格丽特欢喜得太早，停顿一分钟后，他加一句“再做一年”。

There was a new deal being negotiated at the moment in the company and he wanted to see it through. She had waited fifteen years for him to retire. Surely she could wait for one more. It was a compromise, but one she reluctantly agreed to. As the sun dropped out of view John told me he felt selfish for his choice even then, but he couldn’t retire without doing just one more deal.

> 目前公司里有一笔新的生意正在商谈，他想看着这个过程如何进行。为了他的退休她已经等待了15年，当然她可以再多等一年。这是她忍痛同意的和解条款。随着太阳已经落下，约翰告诉我，即使他们达成了妥协，他仍觉得自己做出这样的选择非常自私，然而，没有完成再多一次的交易，他是不可以退休的。

Dreaming of this time for years, things started to become real for his beloved wife. She made some actual plans, on the phone to the travel agent regularly. Each night as he wandered in, she would be waiting for him with dinner prepared. As they ate at the table that had once accommodated their whole family, she shared her thoughts and ideas with great excitement. John was starting to warm to the idea of retirement now too, although still insisted on seeing the twelve months out if Margaret ever suggested otherwise.

> 对他心爱的妻子而言，梦寐以求了多年的退休生活现在开始就要成了现实。她着手进行了一些具体的计划，定期地给旅游公司打电话了解最近旅游状况。每天晚上，当他下班走进家门，她都把晚餐准备好了等他。当他们坐在那张曾经容纳整个家庭成员的餐桌共进晚餐的时候，她很兴奋地跟约翰分享着她的想法和建议。尽管约翰还是坚持等12个月结束后再考虑玛格丽特的提议，但现在，他也开始了对退休后的生活抱以热情的态度。

With four months since his acceptance to retire and eight still to go, Margaret began feeling queasy. At first it was a bit of nausea, but after almost a week it had not passed. “I’ve made an appointment with the doctor tomorrow,” she told him as he came in from work. The night was already dark. Traffic continued in the distance, as other workers headed home. “I’m sure it’s nothing though,” she said with attempted cheerfulness.

> 自从他接受退休后已经过去了四个月，还剩八个月时，玛格丽特开始感觉反胃。刚开始只是一点点恶心，但是，大约一周以后恶心感仍然存在。“我约好了时间明天去看医生。”当约翰下班后，玛格丽特告诉了他。天色变黑，来往车辆继续在路上行驶，其他上班的人正在回家的路上。“我确信没有什么大的问题。”她说这样的话试图让约翰快乐起来。

While John was concerned she was not feeling well, it didn’t cross his mind it may be more until the following night, when Margaret said the doctor had suggested some tests. Even if the results had not said so within the following week however, the increase in her discomfort and then pain would have told them something was wrong. They just hadn’t expected how wrong. Margaret was dying.

> 在约翰担心她不舒服的同时，他并没有想过她的不适可能是更严重的，直到第二天晚上玛格丽特对他说医生建议做一些化验检查，他才有所紧张。在接下来的一周里，即便是化验结果没有说明什么问题，但是玛格丽特的越来越不舒服以及后来感到的疼痛已经在提示医生们她可能有什么疾病。只是他们还没有想到她的不对劲有多么的严重时，玛格丽特就去世了。

We spend so much time making plans for the future, often depending on things coming at a later date to assure our happiness or assuming we have all of the time in the world, when all we ever have is our life today. It was not difficult to understand the deep regrets John was now living with. I understand how people can love their work and there is no need for guilt in doing so. I too now loved now my work, despite the sadness that often accompanied it.

> 当我们拥有的只是我们今天生活的时候，我们却假设我们的未来会幸福，或者假设我们在地球上还有很多很多的明天，去花费许多的时间和精力为未来制订种种的计划。不难理解为什么如今的约翰活在深深的后悔之中。我理解人们是怎样的热爱白己的工作，没有必要为此感到内疚。尽管现在悲伤时常伴随着我，但我同样很爱自己的工作。

But when asked if he would have enjoyed his work so much if he hadn’t had such a supportive family life, John shook his head. “I liked the work enough, sure. And I definitely loved the status, though what’s the point of that now? I gave less time to what truly kept me going through life: Margaret and my family, my dear Margaret. Her love and support were always there. But I wasn’t there for her. She was a lot of fun too. We would have had such a good time away together.”

> 然而，当我问他如果不是为了要养家糊口承担责任的话，是否会非常享受他的工作。约翰摇了摇头说，“是的，我喜欢那份工作。但绝对是热爱那种职业身份。现在这个叫什么？我没有给自己足够多的时间过真正的家庭生活，没有跟玛格丽特和孩子们享受生活，我亲爱的玛格丽特，她总是热爱并支持着我们这个家庭。可是我并没有为她尽到应尽的责任。她也是一位很有风趣的人。我们在一起度过了很多的幸福时光。”

Margaret had died three months before John was due to retire, though he had retired by then due to her health. John shared how his retirement had been plagued with guilt ever since. Even when he was able to come to a certain place of acceptance about his ‘mistake’, as he called it, he longed to be travelling and laughing with Margaret now.

> 由于玛格丽特的健康问题，约翰决定了退休，可是，就在约翰退休前三个月玛格丽特却去世了。自那以后，约翰的退休生活一直被内疚所困扰。甚至当他能够在某些方面接受了一点按他的说法是他的“错误”的时候，他还是渴望如今能与玛格丽特一起旅行和欢笑。“我想我是害怕。是的，我害怕。

“I think I was scared. Yes, I was. I was petrified. My role had come to define me in a way. Of course now as I sit here dying, I see that just being a good person is more than enough in life. Why do we depend so much on the material world to validate us?” John thought out loud, random sentences filled with sadness for both past and future generations who wanted everything, basing their importance on what they owned and what they did, rather than on who they were in their heart.

> 我被吓呆了。在某种程度上，我担当的角色明确了我的命运。理所当然，现在我该在这里坐以待毙，我明白了，人的一生只要做个好人就足够了。为什么我们要如此地依靠物质世界来证明我们自己？”约翰欲大声向那些追求物质生活的过去和未来的几代人说出心声，因为他们决定他们的人生价值不是依据他们扪心自问他们是谁来决定，而是依据他们拥有了什么和做了什么来决定。约翰的几句话充满了伤感。

“There’s nothing wrong in wanting a better life. Don’t get me wrong,” he said. “It’s just that the chase for more, and the need to be recognised through our achievements and belongings, can hinder us from the real things, like time with those we love, time doing things we love ourselves, and balance. It’s probably all about balance really, isn’t it?” Agreeing, I quietly nodded. A few stars were out above us now and the colourful city lights were reflected on the water. Balance had always been a bit of a challenge for me too.

> “想要过更好的生活是没有错的。不要说我错了。”他说，“只是追求更多的物质东西以及需要通过我们的成就和拥有的东西来被别人认同可以阻碍我们拥有一些真正的东西，比如与那些我们所爱的人在一起的时间、做一些我们自己钟爱的事情以及平衡家庭和事业的关系。所有的一切或许真的就是平衡的问题，不是吗？”我默默地点点头表示同意。现在，天已经变黑，一些星星在我们头顶上出现了，五颜六色的城市霓虹照射在水面上。对我而言，达到平衡也是一种挑战。

It seemed to be all or nothing, even in this role. Twelve-hour shifts were my normal working day and as the clients neared the end, they and their families wanted as much consistency with the carers as possible. So it wasn’t unusual to work six days per week during their last month, sometimes even taking on a sleepover shift in between, meaning I was there for thirty-six hours straight. An eighty-four hour week is not healthy for anyone, even if you love the work you do.

> 即使是现在这份工作，似乎都是全或无。12小时的倒班是我的正常工作时间，而当客户们临近死亡，他们及他们的家人们要求护理员尽可能多一些的连续性工作。因此，在病人最后一个月期间，每周工作六天是常有的事，有的时候甚至连续工作不能回家，晚上也住在病人家里，意思就是一直工作24个小时。每周84个小时的工作量对任何人来说都是不利于健康的，即使你很热爱你的工作。

Sometimes the clients would sleep, but I still had to be there. Plenty of other duties called. It felt like my own life was on hold, though in hindsight it wasn’t of course, as this too was a part of it. When the client’s life had passed, I was exhausted. It usually turned out that another regular client would not arise for some time after a run like that.

> 有的时候客户睡觉了，我还是必须守在那里。有很多其他的事得随喊随到。这时仿佛我自己的私人生活都暂停了。每当一个客户过世了，我也累得精疲力尽。通常的情况是一段急行军之后下一位客户的常规工作会暂缓一些时间。

So I welcomed the time off, caught up with friends again, returned to my music and writing, and then did it all over again. The time off was wonderful, particularly in large blocks with only a random shift or two somewhere in between. But the inconsistency put a lot of pressure on my income. If the work stopped, the money stopped.

> 所以，我乐见有那段休息时间，可以再次跟朋友聚会，并回到我的音乐和歌曲创作上，随后又是周而复始的工作周期。暂停工作的休息太棒了，尤其是住在拥挤住宅区里从事着一份仅有一到两段临时倒班休息的工作。但是，这种间断性的工作给我的收入带来了压力。如果我的工作没有了，我的收入也没有了。

Around this time, I was offered one day a week as an office manager in a pre-natal centre. It was steady work and I loved it. The centre ran birthing courses for pregnant women and mothers’ groups. There were weeks when I would go from nursing people who were ready to die that week or very soon after, to having toddlers climbing over me while I worked, placing sloppy kisses on my cheeks.

> 这段期间，我又在一个产前保健中心当一名办公室经理，每周工作一天。这是一份稳定而让我喜欢的工作。这个中心为孕妇开设一些有关分娩的课程以及组织年轻母亲们的交流活动。有几个星期，当我刚从照顾一些即将过世的病人那里走出来，又很快地回到该中心工作。我工作时，一些幼童爬到我的身上跟我玩耍，那些软软的小嘴吻我的脸颊。

It was a healthy reminder about the joys of life and the full circle. As a client passed on, a new babe would arrive at the centre. The little ones were so incredibly fragile and beautiful. My boss, Marie was one of the most wonderful people I have ever known, with a heart as big as they come. I loved her and still do.

> 这是一种有关生命的喜悦和圆满循环的温馨展示。仿佛当一位病人过世，另一个新的生命会来到这个中心。那些小生命简直是难以置信的娇嫩和漂亮。我的老板玛丽是我所认识的人群当中最为优秀的人之一。她天生有一颗宽容的心。我至今仍然爱戴她。

Part of my role would be to update the course material for the pre-natal classes. As a result, much of my day would be spent reading about how women of different cultures around the world approached pregnancy and the whole birth process. It reinforced how fear is conditioned into us as Westerners, when I saw how naturally many cultures approached things and how reduced the pain of birth was for some. It was treated as a joyous, beautiful celebration from start to finish.

> 我的部分工作是为产前保健中心准备更新课程所需的资料。结果，我工作的大部分时间是在查阅有关世界各地不同文化中的妇女们在怀孕和分娩过程中有怎样的差异。当我看到许多文化是如何自然的进行怀孕和分娩，以及一些文化如何减轻分娩疼痛时，我更加明白了西方人的担心是如何形成的。他们把分娩的整个过程想象成欢乐和美好的庆祝。

Being around birth and life was very healthy for me. Hanging out with the dying and having such a strong empathy for the clients and families, did wear me down sometimes. There are people all over the world who devote their whole lives to working with dying people. Perhaps they have mastered detachment more than I had. Or balance. I don’t know.

> 对我来说，周围都是新生命的诞生，这样的生活非常的美好。与临终者居住以及对病人和家属的极大同情有时候消耗了我的体力和精力。世界各地总有一些奉献了他们的整个生命去照顾那些临终的人。也许他们比我更加劳累，又或者已经自我调整到了平衡状态，我不知道。

My full respect goes out to them regardless. What I do know is that having one day a week revolving around the beginning of the life cycle rather than the end, brought lightness to my life I hadn’t known was missing during these years. The energy was fresh and alive, like someone had opened the windows for me, and let clean air blow through.

> 无论如何，我对他们完全的尊敬。而我所明白的是，每周一天围绕着一个生命周期的开始比目睹一个生命结束的感觉要好些，这能给我的生活带来那些年我不知不觉失去了的光明。此时，我的身上充满着鲜活的能量，如同上帝为我打开了所有门窗，让清洁的空气吹进来。

Having the contrasts on a weekly basis also helped me to see my dying clients as babies once. And as new mums proudly showed me their dear newborns, I too considered the babies would hopefully grow older and live a full life. Then one day they too would reach the end of their lives, as my clients were doing. It was a pretty interesting time, to be so exposed to both ends of the spectrum. It was a blessing.

> 每周一次的临时工作让我在目睹生命的死亡之后有机会鉴证生命的诞生。当新妈妈很自豪地给我看她们的可爱宝贝时，我在默默为这些婴儿们祈祷，希望他们健康成长，并将拥有自己完整的生活。然后，某一天他们也会像我的那些老年客户那样走到他们生命的终点。我有幸奔波在生命的两端是相当的有趣，这是上帝对我的祝福。

From that time, I was able to grow in compassion more for others in my life, as I recognised that they too were once just fragile little babies and they too will one day die, as will I. I started to see my parents, siblings, friends, and strangers as babies and young children, once trusting in life with the innocence and hope that young children do. I thought about who they were before the wounds of others, whether that be family, peers, or society, were dumped on them, affecting the natural trust and openness they were born with. The goodness of people’s hearts became clear to me and I began to love them all with the protectiveness of a caring mother.

> 从那以后，我具备了更加同情我周围的其他人的能力，我承认，他们也曾经是娇嫩的婴儿，跟我一样，他们某一天也将会死去。从此，我开始把我的父母、兄弟姐妹、朋友以及陌生人都想象成婴儿和曾经天真无瑕的相信生活和充满希望的儿童。我联想到他们在伤害别人之前是谁抛弃了他们，并影响了他们那种天真的信任和与生俱来的率直，是否是家庭、同伴或者是社会。对我来说，人们内心的善良变得更加的清晰，有着那慈母般的保护特质，我开始去爱护他们所有的人。

No longer did I see the hurtful things said over the years as truly coming from them. The words were coming from their wounds, not the beautiful, pure beings they were born as. Each of those precious babies born decades ago were still a part of them. A dear, little, innocent child also still lived within each of them. And one day they would also receive the wisdom of hindsight that comes to so many people with the dying process.

> 我再也不去理会历年来他们真的伤害过我的那些痛苦往事。他们的脏话来自于他们的伤痛，他们不像出生时那么漂亮与纯洁。每个珍贵“婴儿”出生几十年的年龄依然属于他们的一部分。一个可爱、娇小和天真的小孩也依然与他们一起生活。某一天，他们也会获得“事后诸葛亮”的智慧，就像约翰那样，许多人在走向死亡的过程中才领悟到真理。

There may be times I thought I didn’t love particular people in my life. But I saw it was only their behaviour and words I did not love. Now I loved their innocent hearts, hearts that once trusted the world would bring them happiness and look after them. When it didn’t, suffering kicked in and their pain and disillusionment saw them reacting in ways that were not wholesome.

> 很多时候我都在想，在我的生活中我好像没有爱过某些人。可是，我看出了我只是不爱他们的某些行为和说出的某些话而已。现在，我爱他们的那颗天真无辜曾经相信这个世界会带给他们幸福和眷顾他们的心。一旦这个世界并没有如他们所愿，悲伤袭来，他们的痛苦和幻想破灭见证了他们以怎样不健全的方式来回应。

I was no different. I too had caused others pain, through my own suffering, my own disappointment that life hadn’t turn out as I’d hoped. That little girl whose trust had been broken by being exposed to other’s pain had then reacted with pain of her own. The hearts of my dear family, and of everyone, still contained that original purity. It was just clouded over with pain and life.

> 我也不例外，也有通过自己的悲伤和失望造成他人痛苦的时候，只因为生活没有按照我的期望而转变。那个“小女孩的信任”因暴露他人的痛苦而被彻底破碎，最终也痛苦缠身，自食其果。我亲爱的家人或者每个人的内心依然保存原始的纯洁。那些只是被痛苦和生活所掩盖了。

Whether I would ever find the happiness and friendship I had once hoped for with some people was still to be determined. Either way, it didn’t really matter anymore. I now saw that they were all once beautiful little babies with all of the trust and innocence a baby has. Anything said that was not kind to others was simply the suffering, manifesting from a little child who had lost their way, as I had too.

> 我是否能找回我曾经期望与有些人共享的快乐和友谊还有待确定。但无论怎样，真的没有什么关系了。如今，我看到他们都曾经是漂亮的小宝贝，有着婴儿对世界的信任和天真。俗语都是这样说的，一个迷失了方向的孩子所显现出来的结果是恶人有恶报。

And for that alone I could continue to love them. Sitting beside John on the balcony, I saw the fragile child in him too; a precious little boy who somehow decided through whatever he was exposed to, that proving himself through his work, was going to make him happier than to go travelling with his wife. He was an old man now, yet that little innocent child within him was still obvious. Slow tears fell down his cheeks occasionally as he sighed deeply. Leaving him to his thoughts and privacy I took the dishes inside and cleaned up. On returning I placed a rug over his legs and kissed him on the cheek before sitting down again.

> 当我在阳台上坐在约翰的旁边，我也把他想象成一个娇嫩的小孩，那是一名珍贵的小男孩，在某种程度上，“小男孩”决定了无论怎么样，也要通过工作来提升自己，他觉得干工作比陪着太太旅行更让他感到快乐。现在他虽然是一个老男人，但也看得出一个天真无瑕的孩子依然存在于他的身上。他深深地叹了一口气，同时眼泪慢慢地落到了他的脸颊。此时，我拿起碟子走进房内清洗，留一点私人空间让约翰独自在那里思索。我又回到阳台时，拿了一条毯子盖在他的腿上，在他的脸上轻吻了一下之后又坐在了他的旁边。

“If I can tell you one thing about life Bronnie, it’s this. Don’t create a life where you are going to regret working too hard. I can say now that I didn’t know I was going to regret it, until I was at this time facing the very end. But deep in my heart, I knew I was working too hard. Not just for Margaret, but for me too. I would love to have not cared what others thought of me, as I do now. I wonder why we have to wait until we are dying to work things like this out.” Shaking his head, he kept talking.

> “邦妮，如果我可以给你一个生活忠告的话，那就是：千万不要给自己建立一种让你后悔当初工作太拼命的生活。我现在可以承认了，以前我从不认为自己有这个遗憾，事到如今，面对生命即将结束的时候才明白过来。我知道自己以前工作太拼命了，不仅是为玛格丽特，也是我自己。我应该像我现在这样不要在乎别人怎样看待我。我捉摸着什么我们要到了临终的时候才弄明白像这类的事情。”他边摇着头，边继续地说，

“There is nothing wrong with loving your work and wanting to apply yourself to it. But there is so much more to life. Balance is what is important, maintaining balance.” “I agree John. It has been a lesson for me already but I’m working on it, don’t worry,” I admitted honestly. He knew what I meant. We had shared enough stories by now for him to understand me. John then started laughing to himself. So I questioned him, encouraging him to share the joke.

> “热爱自己的工作且全身心投入并没有什么错。但是，生活还远远不止这些。劳逸结合是最重要的，要保持工作与生活之间的平衡。”“约翰，我同意。对我而言，那已经是我的一种教训，请不必为我担心，我现在正注意这个平衡。”我老实地承认了。他也知道我的意思。为了让他了解我，我们也分享了很多自己的人生故事。随后，约翰突然开始对自己大笑起来。我问他笑什么，敦促他把那个笑话讲出来。

“Well, I said if there was one thing I could tell you, it is to not regret working too hard. But I just thought of another, almost as important.” “Go on then,” I smiled. He looked at me with mischief in his eyes and said, “Don’t you ever throw out that pink dress!”

> “其实，我是说，如果我可以告诉你一件不是后悔工作太拼命的事，而我刚才正想到另外一件要告诉你的事，也是很重要的哦。”我微笑地说：“请说吧。”他以一种搞怪的眼神看着我说：“永远不要扔掉那件红裙子！”

Laughing, John pointed to my chair then tapped the side of his, indicating for me to bring my chair over, which I did as I laughed too. Another couple of hours passed as we sat side-by-side overlooking the harbour, a rug each over us. Now and then the conversation would drift into a comfortable silence, before more conversation resumed. Other moments of silence though were simply broken by a deep sigh from John. I took his hand and he squeezed mine in response.

> 他边大笑，边用手指指了我的椅子，然后用手拍拍他的椅子，意思是要我把我的椅子挪过去挨着他坐，我也边笑边挪椅子。我们俩紧靠着坐在那里俯瞰着港湾，一条毯子盖住了我们俩的腿，又有几个小时过去了。在这期间，我们时而谈话，时而转化舒适的沉默。有一阵的沉默被约翰的一次深深的叹气声打破。我握住了他的手，他也轻轻地捏了一下我的手以示回应。

Looking across at me with a sad smile, he told me, “If I can leave any good in this world besides my family, I leave these words. Don’t work too hard. Try to maintain balance. Don’t make work your whole life”. Smiling back gently, I lifted his hand up and kissed the back of it. John passed away not long after that night. Although I didn’t know it then, his words were to be repeated to me time and again by others I came to care for. He had made his point though and it was one I would never forget.

> 他苦笑地望了望我一眼，然后说：“除了我的家人，如果可以留给这个世界任何好的东西，我只留这几句话：千万不要拼命工作，试图保持平衡。千万不要让工作占据你的整个生活。”我温柔的对他微笑，拿起了他的一只手，在手背上亲吻了一下。那个晚上后不久，约翰过世了。虽然当时我并不知道他过世，但在我照顾其他病人的时候，他的那些话仍旧一遍遍在我的耳边回荡。我完全体会到了它们的涵义，我将会永远记住约翰给我的忠告。
