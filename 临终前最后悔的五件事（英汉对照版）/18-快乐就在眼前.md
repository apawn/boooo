# Happiness is now · 快乐就在眼前

Of all of my clients, Cath was by far the philosopher of them all. She had an opinion on everything. But it wasn’t blind opinion, rather very informed. As a lover of knowledge and philosophy, she had absorbed a huge amount of learning in her fifty-one years. Cath also still lived in the house she was born in. “My mother was born and died here. I am going to do the same,” she stated with determination.

> 在我照顾过的所有客户中，凯西是目前为止唯一爱卖弄大道理的人。她对什么事都要发表意见。不过，也不是无的放矢，都是相当有根有据的。作为一名科学和哲学爱好者，在五十一年的生命里，她吸收了大量的知识。凯西一直居住在她出生的房子里。“我的母亲就是在这里出生和去世的。我也跟我母亲一样将在这里离去。”她很肯定地这样说。

She was also a lover of baths, so the best conversations we had in our first couple of months together were usually with her in the bath and me sitting on a stool beside it. With my own love and appreciation of a good soak, I was determined to help Cath use her tub for as long as possible. After a while though, she had grown weaker and wasn’t going to be strong enough to get in or out anymore, even with my assistance. The risk of a fall was also too high.

> 她也是一个特别喜欢泡澡的人，所以，在我们先前几个月一起相处的时间里，我们之间最愉快的交谈通常都是在她的浴室里进行的，她泡澡，我就坐在旁边的凳子上。由于我自己也喜爱和欣赏泡澡，我决定帮助凯西，尽量让她有机会用她的澡盆泡澡。一段时间之后，她的身体变得虚弱，体力也大不如以前，现在她已经不能自己跨进或者跨出澡盆，甚至需要我的协助才能办到。摔跤的危险度也增加了。

When she knew it was to be her last ever soak in the bath, Cath started to cry, her tears falling into the water surrounding her. “It’s all going. It’s the bath now,” she cried. “Then it will be my walking. Then I won’t even be able to stand, then me, myself, gone. It’s all going. My life is winding down.” Her crying soon became sobbing, raw and uninhibited. As much as my heart felt for her, and my own tears were very near the surface, it was also good to see someone able to release their emotions with such honesty.

> 当她明白这可能是她最后一次在澡盆里泡澡的时候，凯西开始哭了，她的眼泪掉进了澡盆的水中。“所有都将过去了。现在就只是浴缸。”她哭着说。“我将要离去。看嘛，我甚至都不能站起来，那么，我，自己，没了。所有一切都会离去。我的生命也将渐渐结束。”她的哭泣声很快变成了悲泣声，赤裸裸而肆无忌惮地尽情放声大哭。我的心情也差不多跟她一样，眼泪也真的快要流出来。看到某个人能够以这种诚实的方式释放自己的情绪也不妨是一件好的事情。

From the depths of her soul, Cath cried a river of tears. When it seemed there was nothing left to come out, she sat in the bath quietly, exhausted from her sobbing, staring at the water or drawing patterns on the surface. Then it would start again, each sob coming from a place even deeper and more primal than before. She cried for every sad memory ever held within her, for all of the people she had lost, for all of those she would lose by going. But especially, Cath cried for herself.

> 发自灵魂最深处的悲伤使得凯西的泪水流成了河。当那泪水似乎流干的时候，她静静地坐在澡盆里，因过度悲泣而疲惫，她的眼睛盯着澡盆里面的水，用手在水面上随意地划动。过了一会儿，她又开始哭了，每一次的抽泣甚至比上一次更加来得深和更加的放肆。她是为埋藏在自己内心每个悲伤往事的记忆而流泪，为她失去了所有的亲朋好友而哭泣，也为她会失去一切而感到绝望。凯西尤其是为自己而痛哭流涕。

Any time I would attempt to leave, to give her some privacy, she would shake her head and ask me to stay. So I sat on the stool, sending her love, saying nothing, just being there while she sobbed. It was heartbreaking but healthy at the same time, knowing she was letting go from such a deep place.

> 每一次，我打算离开她去拿什么东西，她都会摇摇头请求我留下来，不要离开她。所以，我坐在凳子上，充满爱意地看着她，一言不发，只是在她悲伤的时候陪她待在那里。这既是令人心碎的时刻，但同时也是健康的，我知道此刻她正在释放内心深处的那些情感，这对她有好处。

When another half an hour had passed and the water was losing its heat, I offered to top it up with some more. Cath shook her head. “No, it’s OK. It’s time,” and with that, pulled the plug and looked to me for assistance in getting out. Taking her in her wheelchair out into the sun soon after, wrapped in her pale blue dressing gown and fiery red slippers, she seemed peaceful.

> 半个小时过去了，水温也在下降，我又添加了更热的水。凯西摇着头说：“不要加了，没有关系，我该起来了。”同时，她拔出漏水塞子，看看我示意帮助她出来。很快地我把她扶到轮椅上，推她到外面晒太阳，并用她的淡蓝色长睡袍裹着她的身体，帮她穿上火红色的拖鞋，她看上去似乎很平静。

“Listen to the bird,” she smiled. We both sat quietly, delighting in its song, smiling even more when we heard its mate reply from a tree further up the street. “Every day is a gift now, you know. Every day was always a gift but it’s only now I have slowed down enough, I am truly seeing the huge amount of beauty each day offers to us. We can take so much for granted. Listen.” Different songs rang out from a few nearby trees.

> “请听，鸟在唱歌。”她微笑着说。我们俩静静地坐在那里，陶醉于美妙的歌声，当我们听到从离街更远一点的另外一棵树上它的同伴在那里回应它的动听歌声的时候，我们俩笑得更加开心。“你知道，眼下每一天都是一个福音。每一天都是上帝赐予的礼物，只是现在我已经足够衰老，每天我真的见到了许多美丽的东西。我们理所当然可以享受。请听。”此刻，附近一些树上也响起了不同的鸟语声。

Cath said how she had come to see what a powerful force gratitude is. It is too easy to always want more from life, she said, and that’s fine to a degree, since expanding who we are is a part of dreaming and growing. But as we will never have everything we want and will always be growing, appreciating what we already have along the way is the most important thing. Life goes so fast, she stated, whether you live until your twenties, forties, or eighties.

> 凯西对我述说她为何会拥有那么强大的一股感恩力量。她说，永远都希望从生活中要求得到更多是易如反掌的事，只要不过分就行，充实自己是我们的梦想和成长的一部分。而且我们决不可能做到想要什么就有什么，我们永远在成长，永远要感激我们一路上已经拥有了许多的东西才是最重要的。她陈述了她的观点，无论你活到二十岁、四十岁，或者八十岁，生活都是瞬间即过，岁月如梭。

She was right. Every day in itself is a gift and a blessing. It is all we ever have anyway, the moment we are in. For the past twenty years I had kept a gratitude journal, where I wrote a few things down at the end of the day for which I was grateful.

> 她说得一点没错。每一天本身就是一个礼物和祝福。我们都有这样的一天，都置身于时光的每一刻。过去二十年间，我保持书写我的感恩日志，在每天结束之前，我都会记下这一天我认为值得感恩的事。

Often there were lots of things to be grateful for. But occasionally, through the darkest times, I struggled to come up with any. Emotional exhaustion had worn me out to such a point that even finding blessings was an effort. Yet I always persisted. Even then I would manage to come up with things I was grateful for, like clean water, somewhere to sleep, food in my belly, a smile from a stranger, or a bird singing.

> 我经常觉得有很多事需要感恩。但是，偶尔在我的艰难时期，我绞尽脑汁也想不出需要感恩什么。情感上的疲惫使我精疲力竭到了如此的地步，以至于怎样的努力都发现不了身边的福音。可我总是坚持不懈。即便如此，我也会想象出身边一些细微的事和物来感恩，比如，一杯清澈的水、睡的地方、在我胃里的食物、陌生人的微笑，或者一只鸟的歌声等等。

But, as I explained to Cath, while I was appreciating things at the end of the night when I was writing them down, it had still taken some training to get into the habit of always appreciating things as they were happening as well, particularly intricate things. At the very least, it was a new habit to create by saying a silent prayer of thanks at the very time each of the gifts was given.

> 然而，如同我对凯西解释的那样，在我到了深夜记下我要感恩的对象的同时，我依然觉得需要训练自己把这种日记变成一种习惯，对所发生的一切永远充满感激之情，尤其对那些错综复杂的事更是如此。至少，当每个福音降临的那一刻，我立即默默地感谢祈祷成了我的一个新习惯。

Nature had always received thanks in the moment, definitely. An example I gave was if a soft breeze kissed my face, I was grateful for being healthy enough to be outside to feel it. But I wanted to be more grateful for other things along the way too. Although writing in the journal had certainly opened me up to a much better level of gratitude, it was the success of living more presently that had finally brought gratitude into my every day living situations. There is something to say thank you for every hour, I decided, and that was how my habit was formed.

> 绝对是这样的，在你感恩祈祷的那一刻，大自然总会收到了你的感激之情。我举个例，如果一阵轻柔的微风吹拂了我的脸，我感谢有足够的健康能走出去感受它。我感激其他的事情也都是采用这种方式。虽然在日志里写下感恩的确力我开启了更深层的感激之情，然而，在我每天生活状况中的现场实况感激是最成功的。比如，我决定每天每个小时说出自己的感激之情，我的这个习惯就是这样形成了。

“Then I’m sure you receive many blessings, if you are grateful along the way?” Cath enquired. “When I allow it Cath, when I remember my own worth and let it flow, yes. I’ve definitely had some great blessings in my life. Sometimes I just have to get out of my own way first. Like everyone, blessings come to me more when I am in a place of gratitude and flow.”

> “那么，如果你时时刻刻都在感恩，我敢肯定你收到了不少的祝福。”凯西向我打听。“凯西，当我允许这样的话，当我记住自身的价值并让它随意表达的时候，结果就是这样的。在我的生活中，我的确已经拥有了很多的祝福。有的时候，我只是不得不首先放弃自己的生活方式。如同我们每个人都是这样的，当我处于感恩状态，并随时流露出来，更多的祝福就会降临到我的身边。”

Cath laughed at my theory and agreed. “Yes, it wants to flow to us. But without gratitude and allowing it through, we block it I think. Most people just don’t realise how good they have it. I didn’t for a long time either. But thankfully I had started working it out before this illness struck, so I was able to live from a better place in myself then.” After some lovely time in the sun, Cath needed her lunch and a rest. Lunch was ice cream and stewed fruit.

> 我的理论让凯西大笑起来，但她同意我的说法。“是的，上帝希望我们流露。如果不去感激，我认为我们就阻断祝福的到来。大多数的人只是没有意识到他们拥有了这么好的祝福，很长时间我也不知道。然而，谢天谢地，在这疾病来袭之前我已经开始认识到了，那就是 何我以往能够生活在一个好的地方和环境里。”我们在阳光下度过了美好的一段时光之后，凯西需要吃午餐和午后休息。午餐是冰淇淋和一些水果泥。

That was all she could handle eating now. Everything else took too much effort to chew, she told me, and tasted lifeless. Afterwards, I lifted her legs up onto the bed and moved her into a comfortable position, then drew the curtains closed. Her painkillers had recently been increased which left her more comfortable, but exhausted. So in no time, she was in a deep sleep.

> 她目前只能吃这些。其他的食物她不容易咀嚼，她告诉我，这些味道毫无趣味可言。吃完午餐后，我把她的双腿抬上床，并移动到舒适的位置，随后，关上窗帘。她的止痛药的剂量有所增加，使得她感到更加舒服，由于她已经累了，很快就睡着了。

In the early evening, Cath’s ex-girlfriend dropped by to say hello. There were no hard feelings between them. They had remained good friends after their breakup more than a decade ago. It was a kind and respectful friendship. Other visitors were regular too, Cath’s older brother and his wife and children, and her younger brother. Some neighbours dropped in daily, and friends and work colleagues also came by every chance they could. She was a well-loved woman.

> 在下午早些时候，凯西的前女友顺道过来跟她打招呼。她们之间没有闹僵的感觉。她们的关系破裂超过十年之后现在依然是好朋友。那是一种友善和相互尊重的友谊。其他的探访者比如凯西的哥哥嫂嫂和他们的孩子、凯西的弟弟也定期过来看望她。一些邻居也天天过来，朋友及同事一旦有机会也会过来看她。她是一位深受人们喜爱的女人。

From the various stories shared by her visitors, Cath had been very driven in her work but usually with positive energy for everyone. Now, as with any dying people, she loved her visitors to bring her up to date on their lives and what was happening in the world outside her fence. When dying people can no longer live in that world themselves, they seem to savour every morsel of news from outside. Often friends and relatives don’t know what to say. Hearing about life outside keeps a person in the swing of things and this is positive for them, not negative.

> 从探访者们讲述的凯西的不同故事中我了解到，她曾经不是一个拼命工作的人，不过她通常都是以正能量对待所有的人。现在跟任何一位临终的人一样，她喜欢探访者让她跟上时代，带给她外面世界每天都发生了什么事情的种种新闻。当临终的人再也不能自己亲身体验世界的时候，他们似乎是在一口一口仔细品味外面的传闻。经常朋友和亲属们都不知道对他们讲述什么力好。打听外面的生活使得一个人保持跟随外面事件一起高低起伏的心情，对他们而言，这样与外界联络是只有好处没坏处的。

Such was definitely the case for Cath. She wanted to hear about happy things as much she could. It was hard for the visitors though, as they were often heartbroken at the impending loss of someone they loved. Because of our easy connection I was able to speak openly with Cath about anything. So at the request of her friend Sue, I touched on the emotions of her visitors one day.

> 凯西绝对是这样的人，她希望尽可能多地知道外面发生了什么。这对探访者来说，有时候也很难办到，特别是当他们为即将失去某个他们所爱的人而感到心碎的时候，他们很难对她开口。因为我们俩很容易沟通，我能够直率地对凯西交谈任何事情。所以，应她的朋友苏珊的请求，有一天，我真的被探望她的朋友的情感所感动。

Sue was battling every day to stay positive for her friend, when all she wanted to do was to sob her eyes out every time she visited. Sue told me how she would sit in her car outside, psyching herself into being strong and happy before each visit. Then she’d sit out there again afterwards, crying her heart out. “I sort of see that,” Cath admitted later. “I just don’t know if I can deal with Sue’s sadness on top of my own. I can’t carry that too.”

> 为了在她的朋友面前保持乐观，每天苏珊都在挣扎，当每次她去看望凯西的时候，她唯一想要做的就是大哭一场。她告诉我说在她进去看望凯西之前，她是如何的坐在外面的车上，强迫自己打起精神，假装自己是坚强和快乐的。看望凯西之后，她再次坐在车里痛哭一场。“我看到了。”凯西稍后承认了。“我只是不知道我自己在极度悲伤的状态下是否可以处理好苏珊的悲伤。我也不可能承担起她的悲伤。”

“But you don’t have to carry it,” I said. “Just allow her to express herself honestly by not changing the subject when she shares her feelings. She needs to say stuff and all you need to do is allow her to. You don’t have to carry it as well. She’s not asking you to do that. She just needs to tell you how much she loves you and she can’t do it without crying or without you letting her.”

> “你不必承担。”我说：“当她分享她的感受的时候，你只需允许她自己去真实的表达，不要转换这个话题就可以了。她需要对你说出来，她需要这样允许自己表达。你也不必去担当什么。她并没有请求你这样做。

Cath understood where I was coming from and said she felt slack for creating so much sadness for everyone. It almost embarrassed her. “Gosh Cath, at this time in your life, does pride really matter?” I asked directly, but kindly. She laughed in reply. “Just bring it out into the open and allow others to tell you how much they love you,” I said.

> 她仅仅是要告诉你她有多么的爱你，如果不让她哭或者你不让她说，她是不可能说出来的。”凯西明白我是代表谁在说话，她说因为她给每个人造成了这么多的悲痛让她感到无精打采。这几乎使她感觉难堪。“我的天啊，凯西，这都到什么时候了，骄傲真的那么重要吗？”我直接而友善地问她。她没有说话，只是用笑了一笑来回复我的问题。“请敞开你的胸怀，允许别人告诉你他们有多么的爱你。”我又直截了当地对她说。

Cath smiled at me and sat silent for a moment before responding. “Some time ago, when I was realising the seriousness of my disease, I learned to accept my feelings and not reject them. They come up and I allow them to now. That’s how I was so free sobbing in front of you that day in the bath. I’ve learned to accept my feelings as what they are in the moment, without rejecting them by trying to block them out. They are really just a bi-product of my thoughts and mind anyway. I know it is possible to create new feelings by focusing on better things. But those within me already are a part of the current me, and are best released, not carried forward. Yet here I am, not respecting the feelings of others by rejecting and blocking their honest expression.”

> 凯西对着我微笑，坐在那里沉默了一会儿，然后说：“不久前，当我意识到了我的疾病的严重程度，我学会了去接受我的所有感受，不排斥它们。它们来了我就毫无保留地让它们出现。那就是为什么那天在泡澡的时候我在你面前这样不顾脸面地自由放纵地哭泣。我已经学会了时时刻刻接受我的感受，不再试图压抑它们的出现。反正它们真的是我的思想和心灵的双重产物。我知道，关注令人高兴的事可能带来新的感受。而且，我内心的那些东西已经固定成为我现在的组成部分，我得到了最好的释放，不想再承受什么。

Cath shook her head at herself and sighed. Then after a moment’s thought, she looked at me smiling and said, “I guess it is time for me to be brave and let their tears flow too”. Nodding in agreement, I suggested that things could still possibly be light on occasions following. But the current build-up of emotions from her friends and relatives needed to be shared.

> 可是，我排斥和阻止他们的诚恳表达是不尊重他们的感受。”凯西自己摇了摇头并叹气。接着在一阵的思考后，她微笑着看看我说：“我猜想对我来说现在是该拿出勇气让他们也尽情地流泪吧。”我点头表示同意，并建议事情还是可以慢慢地进行。现在她的朋友和亲属积累起来的情感需要跟她分享。

They loved her and they needed to be able to say and show this, even if it meant through tears sometimes. Soon after, there were many tearful conversations between Cath and her visitors, but the love that flowed was inspiring. Hearts were opened and though they were breaking in some ways, they were healing too, through the expression of love now flowing.

> 他们爱她，也需要能够说出来并向她展示他们的爱，即使有的时候在那一刻将会意味着大家一起泪流满面。不久之后，在凯西和她的探访者之间有了许多的含泪交谈，但是大家流露出的爱是令人鼓舞和振奋精神的。打开了心扉，通过爱的表达，他们也疗愈了自己的伤痛。

On one particularly tearful day, the last friend had just left. She was laughing through tears of both sadness and joy as her and Cath cracked jokes with each other until she was out of sight. When she was gone, Cath looked at me with love. “Yes, it is important to let feelings come out, to accept them. And it is healthy for my friends too,” she said. “These will also be better memories for them. They won’t be blocked by carrying things they don’t need to.”

> 在一个特殊的含泪的一天，最后一位探访者刚好离去。在她与凯西互相开着玩笑的时候，她一直不停地笑，笑声里伴随有悲伤与喜悦的泪水，直到她离开了凯西的家那笑声才渐渐消散。当她完全离去的时候，凯西深情地看着我说：“是啊，让所有的感受表露出来并接受它们是多么的重要。这对我的朋友们来说也是有利于健康的。他们也将有更加难忘的记忆。他们没有必要承受心理压力，他们的情感表达不会被阻止。这真的是太棒了。”

Enjoying her analysis, I nodded in understanding. In my darker days, I’d finally been able to separate myself from my feelings, realising they were just an emotional expression of my pain, or joy, and were not who I truly was. Like everyone, I carried the wisdom of my soul within me. But to know my true self, that divine wisdom residing within, I had to allow my feelings out.

> 我点了点头表示理解，并且分享了她的分析。在我备受煎熬的那些日子里，我最终能够从我的种种感受中解脱出来，认识到了那些感受仅仅是我的痛苦或者喜悦的情绪表达而已，它们不代表真正的我。如同大家都是这样，我的灵魂装载着我的智慧。如果我要明白其中真正蕴藏着神圣智慧的自我，我必须允许自己将所有的感受发泄出来。

If not, they would always block me from reaching the potential of who I was truly here to be. So I loved to hear of Cath coming to similar conclusions, but expressing it in her own words. Already being a thin build, it took very little time for her to start looking ill as the weight continued to fall off. “My time is winding up. I can’t ignore the signs, that’s for sure,” she declared one morning, while sitting on the commode.

> 否则，这些感受总会阻碍我抓住表现自我的真正潜在性。我很高兴听到凯西总结出这一相似的结论，以她自己的语言方式表达了我想说的话。凯西的身材很矮小瘦弱，而随着体重的持续下降变得更加娇小，现在对她来讲，没有必要花时间去看医生。“我的时间接近尾声了。我不可忽视这些迹象，这是毫无疑问的。”一天早上，她坐在马桶上慎重其事地这样说。

So many conversations with clients happened as they sat doing their morning business on the portable toilet, while I sat nearby. The fact that they were having a bowel movement never really came into it. It was just a part of the routine and there was no point in letting such business get in the way of a good yarn. As I helped Cath back to bed after, I agreed that indeed, the signs were pointing to her time winding up.

> 我的很多客户都是这样，清晨他们坐在这种便携式厕所方便的时候，我坐在旁边，我们许多的谈话都是这样进行的。事实上，他们也只是肠蠕动而已，不是真正的排便。这是他们日常所要完成的事务之一，在排便的时候进行一次很好的交谈是没有任何意义的。在我帮助了凯西回到床上之后，我表示的确不反对她的意见，所有的迹象表明了她的生命是接近尾声了。

Once settled back into bed, she said, “I am not sorry for how I have lived, because I have learned through most of what I have done. But if I was to do anything differently, given the chance to again, I would have allowed more happiness in”. I was a little puzzled to hear these words from her. By now I had heard them from other clients of course, but Cath appeared as a happy person.

> 等她好好地躺下来，她说：“我不会因我这一生是如何过来的而感到伤感，因我一路走来已经学到了很多的东西。假如有机会让我再做任何不寻常的事，我会允许让更多的快乐进入我的身体。“我当时有点听不懂她讲的话。如今我理所当然已经从其他的客户那里听到过这样的说法，我明白了他们的意思。总的来说，凯西表面上看是一个快乐的人。

Well, as happy as one can be when you are dying and feeling absolutely awful in your body in the process. So I questioned her on this. Explaining that she had loved her work, she then told me of how she had put too much emphasis on the results. Cath had worked on projects for troubled youths and believed that making a contribution was vital for a satisfying life.

> 那么，这样快乐的一个人，当她临终的时候，反而感到绝对的害怕自己身体的变化过程。因此，我向她讨教了我的疑问。她解释说，她以前很热爱她的工作，随后，她又告诉我她如何的太过于强调工作的成果。凯西曾经工作的项目是帮助问题少年，她相信，为了获得满意的生活，做些贡献是至关重要的。

“We all have talents to share, every single one of us. It doesn’t matter what your job is. What matters is that you are trying to make a conscious contribution, hoping to create a better world,” Cath elaborated. “The only way things are going to improve is for us all to realise the inter-connectedness we share. Nothing good can be done alone. If only we could learn to work together for the good of all, instead of against each other in competition and fear.”

> “我们每个人都有与人分享的能力。这与我们做什么工作没有关系。问题是你要尽量主动地做贡献，希望创造更美好的世界，”凯西更详细阐述了她的观点。“我们每个人要想提升自己的唯一方法是认识我们应当分享相互之间那些关联的东西。只要我们能够学会一起工作对我们大家都有好处，而不是在竞争的恐惧之中相互对抗。”

Despite being exhausted and spending her time now mostly confined to bed, Cath still had a lot to say. The philosopher would be the last part of her to go, I suspected (which suited me just fine). I rubbed cream into her arms and hands as she went on. “We all have a positive contribution to make. I’ve made mine. But while I was searching for my purpose in life, I forgot to enjoy myself along the way. It was all about the result of finding what I was looking for. Then when I did find work I loved, work I could do with the heartfelt intention of contributing, I was still results-based.”

> 尽管她已经发困了，而现在也该是她上床休息的时间，凯西依然觉得有许多话要说。在她继续滔滔不绝地讲述她的观点的同时，我用护肤霜抹擦她的双臂和双手。“我们每一个人都对社会做出过积极的贡献，我也有。然而，在我寻找自己的生活目标的同时，我忘记了去享受自己的生活。这是我一直在寻找的唯一结果。然而，当我找到一份自己喜欢的，能够用我的全心达到奉献意图的工作的时候，我还是看重其结果。”

This was something I had seen often. They were also familiar words from other clients. While working towards goals, the present moment is too often neglected along the way. This is what Cath was talking about. Her happiness was based on the end result, and was not enjoyed during the process of getting there. I commented that none of us were immune from doing that at times, myself included.

> 我常常看到这种状况。其他的客户们也讲过类似的话。在向着目标工作的同时，往往忽略了活在当下。这就是凯西想表达的意思。她的快乐和幸福建立在结果之上，完全没有去享受获得结果的那些过程。我对此表明自己的看法，我们每个人都不可避免这样做，包括我自己。

She continued. “Yes, but this way, I have robbed myself of potential happiness. That’s what I mean when I say I would do it differently. It is important, sure, to work toward finding your purpose and contributing to the world, in any capacity. But depending on the end result for your happiness is not the way to do it. Gratitude for every day along the way is the key to acknowledging and enjoying happiness now. Not when the results come in or when you retire, or when this or that happens.” Cath sighed, exhausted from her fervent outburst but with a need to be heard, as was so often the case.

> 她继续说：“是啊，可是我因此而丧失了自己的潜在幸福。那就是为什么我说我会做得不一样的意思。在任何岗位上，为寻找你的人生目标去工作并这个世界做出某些贡献的确很重要。但是只依据最终的结果来决定你的幸福和快乐是不可取的。在我们人生旅途中，感激每个当下是获得享受幸福和快乐的关键。不要等到结果出现了和你退休了，或者这样或那样的事情发生了才明白。”凯西叹了叹气，激情澎湃的讲话让她非常疲惫，可是她需要有人听她讲述，我以前经历过很多像这种的情况。

After listening and then sharing my understanding of her thoughts, I adjusted her blankets and headed out to the kitchen to make us some tea. Cutting some fresh lemongrass from the garden, I thought about Cath’s words. Very similar words from other dying people came to mind too. As a bird sang and the scent of lemongrass, now in the teapot, wafted through the kitchen, it was very easy to feel completely present and grateful.

> 聆听了她的讲话以及让她知道我有理解她的含义之后，我帮她把毯子重新盖好，然后向厨房走去，为我们泡一些茶水。我到花园剪取几片新鲜柠檬香草的时候，想到了凯西刚才说的那些话。其他客户也说过非常类似的话。随着树上的鸟在歌唱，从茶壶里冒出的柠檬香气现在飘散在整个厨房里，令人立刻感觉活在当下和心存感激之情。

Wanting to relax and listen now, Cath asked me where I lived. Laughing a little, I explained how this was the first question any of my friends asked me whenever they called. “Where are you these days?” were words my ears knew well. So I told Cath all about my earlier years of drifting, followed by the recent years of house-sitting, and how recently, my energy for such a transient existence was beginning to wane.

> 可能是希望得到一些放松而听听别人讲话，凯西开始问我以前住过哪里。我笑了一下并解释说无论什么时候，这都是我所有朋友问我的第一个问题。“那些日子你在哪里？”那几个字早就让我的耳朵生茧。随后，我告诉了凯西我早些年的漂流生活，然后是近些年的帮人看房子，以及最近我那短暂的热情又如何开始暗淡下来。

House-sits were not as forthcoming or consistent for me in Melbourne as they had been in Sydney. Not knowing where I’d be living next was starting to leave me weary, as was the whole moving process. Something I had once thrived on and loved was now beginning to exhaust me.

> 在墨尔本，帮人看房子的工作没有像在悉尼那样唾手可得或者可持续。在我整个的迁移过程中，无法预测我下一步将会住哪儿，这已开始让我感到厌倦。我曾经热衷并迷恋的事情现在也开始让我感到疲惫。

After staying with a few friends in between house-sits, I had recently rented the spare room of a house belonging to a woman I knew a little. Although I was hugely grateful for her kindness and to not be moving every few weeks, it was still very much her domain. So it never truly felt like home and was not ideal for the long-term.

> 看房子工作的空当期间我跟一些朋友住在一起，目前我自己租了一间空房，从一位我有点熟悉的女人的房子里分租一个房间。虽然非常感谢她的善良，我也一直住在她那里有好长一段时间了，但那毕竟是她的地盘。所以，我真的一点都没有像住在自己家里的感觉，也不知道时间住长了结果会是怎么样。

It was all meant to be though as the situation served to intensify the longing for my own space again. It had been almost a decade since I’d had my own kitchen and home space. The desire for this continued to increase daily. Cath said how she couldn’t even imagine such a life when she had lived in the same house for fifty-one years. I said I couldn’t imagine her life either and that even though I was starting to long for my own space again, a part of me would always love to roam a little.

> 所有这些情形和生活现状加剧了我渴望重新拥有自己的空间。自从我曾经有过属于自己的厨房和家之后几乎有十年之久。这样的渴望日益增强。凯西已经持续住在这栋房子里长达五十一年了，她说她完全不能想象没有自己的房子生活该如何度过。我说我也无法想象她的那种生活，即使我开始渴望重新拥有自己的房子，但是，我骨子里面的一部分永远还是喜爱适当的周游各地。

Now though, I was thinking in terms of having a settled base and travelling from there, rather than moving my whole home base every time itchy feet kicked in. The years of roaming, which had been a part of me for my whole adult life were a big part of who I used to be. But changes were happening within me and I no longer had the desire or energy to maintain the life I had before. All I really wanted was my own kitchen again and the privacy to be in my own space.

> 如今，我在打算，每次脚发痒又踢我出去流浪的时候，按照分类只携带需要的一些东西，而不是把整个家搬迁。那些年的漫游生活已经成为了我成年生活的一部分，那也是我曾经是谁的重要组成部分。然而，我的内心发生了改变，我不再期望或者不再有能量维持以前那种生活方式。我现在唯一真正希望的是重新拥有自己的厨房和自己的私有地盘。

Agreeing that change is a guaranteed part of life, Cath laughed and said I helped the law of averages out by changing my life so often. Replying that people like me were needed to balance out people like her who lived in the same house for half a century, we both giggled. Our lives were so different yet we shared a very strong connection. It was due to our shared love of philosophy.

> 凯西听完我的讲述笑了，她同意改变是让生活有保障的一部分，她开玩笑说我如此高频率的改变我的生活环境违背了平均法则。我回应她说像我这样的人需要像她那样在同一个房子里生活了半个世纪的人来平衡。虽然我们俩的生活如此的截然不同，可是，我们之间也分享了很强的联结点，那就是我们共同爱好哲学理论。

Wanting to know how I ended up in this field of palliative care, Cath was astonished when I spoke of all of my years in the banking industry. “Oh, I cannot imagine it at all,” she said surprised. “Me either, thank goodness,” I laughed. It amazed me thinking back to then, just how much can be fit into one lifetime, and how it was difficult to even imagine myself being in that world, let alone for so long. “Stockings, high heels, and corporate uniforms just never sat right for me Cath, nor did the structured life.”

> 她还想知道我是如何选择进入姑息护理这个领域的，当我对她讲述了我在银行工作那些年的所有情况的时候，她感到吃惊。“哇，我完全不敢想象。”她说她很惊讶。“谢天谢地，现在我也无法想象。”说完，我大笑起来。回想过去简直令我惊叹不已，一个人一生中有多少时候可以活得称心如意？我多么的难以想象自己仅在那样的环境里让自己孤单了这么长的时间。“凯西，长丝袜、高跟鞋和公司制服永远不适合我，这种循规蹈矩的生活也不适合我。”

“I’m not surprised, considering the life you have chosen since,” she chuckled, before turning more serious and asking how long I was going to do this work for and if I had any other work aspirations. There was no point holding back. I had already learned the importance of honesty and it felt marvellous to be able to talk so freely on this topic. Many things had been on my mind in this regard recently, and talking them out with Cath gave me some clarity.

> “考虑到你已经选择了那种生活，所以我并不感到惊奇。”她捂着嘴巴轻声咯咯笑了，之后，她变得更严肃起来，并问我对于现在的这种工作打算还做多久以及是否有另外工作的愿望。隐瞒我的真实想法没有任何意义，我已经知道了诚实的重要性，能够如此自由讨论这个话题使我感觉很美妙。关于这个问题近期我思考了很多，向凯西说出来也让我的想法更清晰明了。

Somewhere along the way in the last twelve months or so, the idea of teaching songwriting in a jail had come to mind. The prison system was something I knew nothing about, yet the idea had stuck with me. Over this time the seed had continued to grow slowly. I had recently made some contact with a great woman who had taken me under her wing, guiding me through the possibilities of finding funding.

> 过去大约十二个月左右的某个时候，我有一个去一所监狱里传授歌曲创作的想法。不过我对监狱系统没有任何的了解，所以我的这种想法被卡住了。一段时间过后，这个想法继续缓慢地滋长。最近，我有经常与一位给了我很多关照的女士联络，她指导我如何寻找可能的援助资金。

“Yes, get back to the living Bronnie. It is beautiful work you do here and obviously a part of your purpose this time around. But it must wear you down sometimes,” Cath insisted. Telling her how it had now been almost eight years since I first began working in this field, I felt something shift within, an acknowledgment that I was indeed close to hitting the wall if I continued. I was starting to burn out. Seeing people find their peace and being a witness to their growth, in the sunset of their life, was an incredible honour.

> “是的，邦妮，回到你的现在生活来说，你在这里做的工作非常好，很显然这是你当前生活目标的一部分。但是，有的时候这也势必消耗了你的能量。“凯西坚定地说。我告诉她我从第一天在这个领域里工作至今差不多八年了，我意识到了如果我继续这样干下去的话，我的确会遇到一个瓶颈。因力我开始出现职业倦怠。目睹人们寻找到了他们的平静，见证了他们在黄昏时期的成长是多么难以置信的荣耀。

It brought me many, many rewards of satisfaction and fulfillment. There was no denying that I had loved this work and still did. But I also wanted to work where there might perhaps be a little bit of hope, around people who had a chance to grow and change their lives considerably before they were dying. The desire for working entirely in a creative field had also been building, as had the hope of being able to work more from home, once I did find my own space to live in again.

> 这给我带来了许许多多的满足与充实感。毫无怀疑，我已经爱上这份工作，并将继续干下去。而且，我也希望在一个或许给人一点希望的环境里工作，周围的人在他们临终之前有机会成长，并且在很大程度上改变他们的生活很美好。一旦我重新寻找到自己的生活空间，有了希望能够多在家里工作的话，全心投入创作领域的愿望也就可以实现。

Hearing myself speaking all of these thoughts out loud to Cath gave tangible energy to the process. Before I knew it, ideas about teaching in a jail were occupying my thoughts more and more. My time in care work was coming to an end. It needed to. I’d given almost all I could to it. Not long before passing on, Cath got a second wind and appeared to be getting better for a couple of days.

> 听到自己大声地对凯西说出了我的全部想法，我身上顿时产生出实现这种愿望的切实能量。不知不觉地，去监狱教授课程的想法越来越占据我的头脑。我感觉在照顾领域的工作即将结束。是需要结束了。我几乎已经做好所有的准备。凯西在去世前不久，又有了短暂精神，有两天看上去病情有所好转。

I’d seen this before and telephoned all of her regular visitors to come and spend some brief time with her, as she was about to turn onto the final, downward slope. Some of them questioned me after their visit, as she looked so great and her energy had improved. It seems to be a blessing we are occasionally given after someone has been ill for so long. It helps us to remember them with a bit of their old spark, before the illness had taken over. Laughter rang out from Cath’s room for two days while she cracked witty jokes and enjoyed beautiful clarity with friends and family.

> 我以前见过这种状况，比较有经验。我给所有定期来看望她的人打了电话，请他们过来花点时间陪她，因为实际上她的身体状况正在急剧下滑，接近终点。由于她看起来气色很好，精力也有所增加，有的朋友在探视她之后还对我的说法表示怀疑。一个人患病很长时间之后，似乎上帝会给大家一个偶尔的福音，在他们病入膏肓之前，帮助我们记得他们一点点旧日的时光。欢笑声在凯西的房间响彻了两天，她不断爆料诙谐的笑话，与朋友和家人尽情享受着美丽的清醒时光。

When I arrived the following day though, I was looking at a dying woman, hardly able to even respond to me verbally. Cath was limp with no strength at all and remained this way for another three days. She mostly slept, but when she was awake she smiled at me, as I changed her pads and washed her. Even the luxury of urinating on a commode was now in the past.

> 当我第二天到达的时候，出现在我的眼前是一个正在走向死亡的女人，她几乎不能用语言回应我。凯西在完全失去力量中挣扎着，她的这种状况又维持了三天。她几乎整天都在睡觉，但是，当她睡醒后在我帮她更换床上的护垫和给她清洗时，她都会对我微笑。她只能在床上大小便，即使一个豪华的马桶现在也即将成为了过去。

Friends returned and left solemnly, knowing they had just said their final goodbye to their dear Cath. At the end of the third day, it was obvious she would not make it through the night. So when my shift was finished, I stayed on there with Cath’s brother and sister-in-law. The night carer had never seen a dead body and was hugely relieved I was staying. Thinking back to when I had been in that position all of those years ago myself, I saw how far I had come. Little did I know just how many beautiful people I would meet, in such a personal way, nor the unforeseeable blessings of learning that would flow to me.

> 朋友们都再次回来看望她，然后很沉重地离开，他们知道这是他们最后一次跟亲爱的凯西告别。在第三天的晚上，很显然她不会熬过那一夜。因此，当我的当班时间结束的时候，我没有离开，留下来陪着凯西的哥哥和嫂嫂。那位夜间看护员从未亲眼见过人过世，我留在那里让她很放松。回想到许多年以前我也跟她一样对死亡全然不知，我豁然明白自己经历了多少个死亡才一路走到了今天。在这样一条个人的成长道路上，我不知道我会遇见多少美好的人，也不知道多少不可预见的学习福音会降于我。

Cath’s painkillers had been given to her intravenously for the last few days, as she could no longer swallow solid tablets. The palliative care nurse arrived in the evening to dispense some more. Cath was no longer awake or coherent. “This will be the last,” she told Cath’s brother and me. “She won’t make it through the night anyway.”

> 最后这几天，凯西的止痛药都是通过静脉注射的方式滴入的，因为她再也不能吞咽固体药物。那位姑息护理护士晚上过来配备更多的药。凯西再也不会清醒过来。“这将是她的最后时间，”她对凯西的哥哥和我说。“不管怎样，她不会度过今晚。”

We thanked her gently and I walked her out. “She’ll be gone within the hour,” the nurse told me as I said goodbye at the gate. There was so much joy and sadness in this role: Sadness in saying goodbye and letting go. Happiness for the end of their suffering and for the love we shared. It was bittersweet and some tears slowly fell.

> 我们感谢了她的温和和善良之后，我领她走出房间。“她在一个小时之内就会离去。”当我在大门口对她说再见的时候她对我这样说。我们这种角色有很多的喜悦和悲伤交织一起：在向他们最后悼别和随他而去之中产生悲伤；当他们的病痛结束和我们分享了爱而产生喜悦。这是喜忧参半的时刻，我的泪水不由自主地慢慢流了下来。

Cath didn’t wait another hour. She passed away as I was walking back to her room. Her breathing had simply slowed down and then stopped. Looking at her lying there, that beautiful spirit now elsewhere, I smiled through my tears, still hearing her voice in my head. “Don’t hang around with dying people forever, let some joy back in,” she had said to me in a dying whisper the previous morning.

> 凯西没有等到一个小时。在我回到她的房间的同时，她去世了。她的呼吸很平稳地缓慢下降，最后停止了。她的躯体躺在那里，而此刻，她美丽的灵魂已经出壳。望着她我含着泪微笑了，她的声音一直在我的脑海里回响：“不要永远跟快要死的人在一起，让愉快回到你自己的身边。”这是她昨天早上垂死之时低声地对我说的最后一句话。

My tears burst forth and I let them flow, as I stood beside her bed. “Happy travels my friend,” I said silently from my heart. Her brother and sister-in-law came around to the side of the bed, each giving me a loving hug, through their own tears. Formalities were then to be attended, which her family wanted to do. So I looked back at Cath’s body one last time, a body I had washed and massaged so many times. But Cath wasn’t in there anymore. Her spirit had moved on.

> 我的眼泪夺眶而出，我呆呆地站在她的床边，任凭泪水放纵自流。“我的朋友，一路走好。”我默默地从心里对她说。她的哥哥和嫂嫂也走到床边，一一跟我拥抱，他们也都泪流满面。等待他们家庭需要办理的一切例行公事的手续办妥之后，我最后一次回头看了一眼凯西的身体，这是我曾经清洗和按摩过多次的漂亮身体，而凯西再也不在里面，她的灵魂已经漂走到了其他的地方。

She was still in my heart though and smiling softly, I said my final goodbyes to her and her family. The night carer also said goodnight, before she headed off down the road. Then walking out of Cath’s home for the last time, the streetlights shining bright in the quiet suburban street, I pulled the gate closed behind me.

> 可是，她依然存在我心中并对我温柔地微笑。我最后向凯西和她的家人告别。那位夜间看护员在离开之前也向大家道了晚安，然后最后一次走出凯西的家向街头走去。那静静的郊区街头，路灯依然明亮。我也走出了这个家，轻轻关上了大门，仿佛不想打扰凯西睡觉，我迈着轻轻的脚步离去。

The world always felt surreal after I had experienced a passing. My senses were heightened and I felt like I was observing the world from somewhere else. As I walked up the stairs of the tram, I was hardly aware of others around me. The world went by outside as I sat thinking about Cath and the beautiful time we had shared.

> 每当我经历一次死亡之后，总会感觉眼前的世界是超现实的。我的感官变得高度敏感，感觉自己仿佛在另外一个世界里观察这个世界。在我走上电车之时，我几乎感觉不到周围人的存在。当我坐在电车里想着凯西和我们一起度过的那些美好时光的同时，我感觉整个世界已经不在我的身边。

When the tram stopped at a red light, I watched laughing people walking into a restaurant. It was a balmy evening and everyone I saw coming and going was jovial. My tired, weary eyes smiled, viewing signs of such happiness. Sounds from inside the tram then came to my ears, after having been zoned out for some time.

> 当电车在红灯前停住了的时候，我看到一群欢笑的人走进一个餐馆。这是一个温暖的夜晚，我看见来来去去的人都是那么的善良快活。我虽然很累，可是看到这么多如此快乐和幸福的场面，我带着疲惫的双眼微笑了。随着超感官所划分的时间段渐渐消散之后，一会儿，车厢里的声音又重新进入我的耳朵。

All I heard from there too were happy conversations. It was just one of those nights, where happiness was in the air. Although there had been certainly been sadness in my night, there was happiness too for having known Cath.

> 我在那里听到的所有声音也都是人们在快乐的交谈。此时仅是所有夜晚中的一个，空中充满了欢乐。虽然我的这个夜晚里有悲伤，但认识了凯西也给我带来种种快乐。

The sounds of laughter from others danced with me, bringing me happiness of my own. As the tram moved on again, I looked out the window and thought about the good hearts of people everywhere and of those who were in my view. Gratitude warmed my own heart, and I couldn’t help but smile. I wasn’t thinking of the past or the future. Happiness is now. And that is where I was.

> 欢笑声从跟我一起摇晃的其他人群中进发出来，也感染了我自己。在电车再次开动的同时，我向窗外望去，我想在我的视野里见到的所有的人都是好心和善良的人。感恩之情此刻温暖着我的心，我忍不住对自己笑了。我不必去想过去或者将来。快乐和幸福就在眼前。此时此刻我就置身于其中。
