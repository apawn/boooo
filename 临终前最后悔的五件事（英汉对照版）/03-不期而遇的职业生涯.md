# An unexpected career path · 不期而遇的职业生涯

After those years in England and the Middle East, I finally came home to my beloved Australia. I was a changed person, as one is after travel. Returning to work in the banking industry, it soon became obvious that this work would never satisfy me again. Customer service was the only highlight of the role now and while it was easy to pick up work in any town, I was restless and desperately unhappy in my working life.

> 几年的英国和中东旅行之后，我终于回到了我热爱的家乡澳大利亚。作为一个旅归者，我脱胎换骨似的转变成了另外一个人。重新回到银行工作后不久，毋庸置疑，这种工作再次未能令我满意。掌握这项工作并不难，为客户做好服务是唯一的最高职能要求。虽然在任何一个城市里都很容易做好这类工作，可我在工作中还是感到焦躁不安，极为不愉快。

Creative expression was also starting to flow from me. Now living in Western Australia, I sat by the Swan River in Perth one day and made two lists. One list was what I was good at. The other one was what I loved to do. From this I had to acknowledge there was an artist of sorts within me, as the only things that landed in both columns were creative talents.

> 表达创意的欲望也开始从我内心喷发出来。那会儿我居住在澳大利亚西部，有一天，我坐在珀斯的天鹅河边，自己列出了两个清单。一个清单写出我有哪些优点，另一个写出我喜爱做什么。在我的清单里，唯一同时出现在两边的单项是“创造才能”，那一刻我不得不承认了我有某种艺术家的潜质。

“Dare I think I could be an artist?” I thought to myself. Despite growing up around musicians, the reliability of a ‘good job’ has also been instilled in me, hence, why no one could understand my restlessness with a steady nine to five existence in the banking game. They were ‘good jobs’; good jobs that were slowly but surely killing me.

> “我觉得我有勇气去努力使自己成为一名艺术家吗？”我对自己这样问着。尽管我是在音乐人家庭里长大，但我的父母一直在灌输我找一份可靠“好工作”的观念。因此，他们都不能够理解我在银行固定的朝九晚五生活中所表现出的烦躁不安。那的确是一份公认的“好工作”，可这样的好工作正在缓慢而真真切切地扼杀我。

Intensive soul searching unfolded, as I tried to work out what I could do well, but also enjoy. They were hard times as everything was changing within me. I finally came to the conclusion that I would have to eventually be working from the heart, as working only from the intellect had already left me too empty and dissatisfied. So I began developing my creative skills through writing and photography, which led to songwriting and performing eventually, in a long and roundabout way. All the while I was still working in banking jobs, though mostly as temporary staff now. The trappings of a full-time job were just not possible to endure anymore.

> 加紧展开自我内心的反思，我试图找出自己到底能够做好什么样的工作才既挣得到钱又能让我享受其中的乐趣。当我的内在灵魂和思想都在发生着某种变化之时，要得出一个完美的结论是艰难的。最终我还是给自己下了个结论，我应该完全地发自内心地去工作，那种仅靠智商去实现的工作已经让我感到太乏味和极不满意。于是，我开始通过写作和摄影去发掘我的创造才能，从而引导我走向漫长而迂回的创作歌曲和表演之路。虽然多数时间我只是一名“临时”的艺术追求者，绝大多数时候我还是在银行工作。但是穿着全职工作的制服不可能让我再忍受下去了。

Perth was a long way from anywhere though and as much as I loved living there, the desire to be more accessible to those I cherished saw the eastern states calling me back. So across the mighty Nullarbor Plains it was, through the Flinders Ranges, along the Great Ocean Road, up through the New England Highway, until Queensland declared itself home for the next while. Some of this time found me working in a call centre for people subscribing to an adult movie channel. It was far more interesting than the banking industry at times. “Um.”

> 虽然，我在珀斯居住的时间比其他任何地方都长久，我也非常喜欢住在那里，但是，渴望更近距离了解那些我所钟爱的东部各州的冒险情愫又在召唤我。因此，我穿过了浩瀚的纳勒博平原、越过了弗林德斯山脉，沿着黄金海岸，通过了新英格兰高速公路，一直到了昆士兰州后才决定它就是我未来的家。这段时间的某些时候，我在一个成人电影频道的认购呼叫中心工作。这种工作有的时候比银行工作要有趣得多。“嗯。”

Silence. “I’m just ringing up for my husband.” “So you would like to subscribe to ‘Night Moves’ then?” I would reply in a friendly, accepting tone, always putting the women at ease. Or the guys would ask, “What’s it like? I mean. Do you see everything?”

> 顿时的寂静。“我只是为我丈夫打这个电话。”“那么，你愿意认购‘夜行客’吗？”我会以友善与理解她的音调回复她，并尽量让她不感到拘束。或者男人们会问：“那怎么样？我的意思是，你有看过每部片子吗？”

“I am sorry sir. I’ve not viewed it myself. But I can offer you a one night trial for $6.95 and if you like it enough, you can call back and subscribe to it on a monthly basis.” And of course, there were the expected calls of, “What colour knickers have you got on?” Bronnie hangs up. But when the giggles had subsided, it was simply another office job. Friendships were formed with other staff, which made it more enjoyable. But my restlessness continued to fester.

> “对不起，先生。我自己没有看过这部电影。但是，我可以为你提供6.95澳元包一晚的电影播放服务，如果你觉得满意，你可以再打电话过来认购包月的服务。”当然，也有一些意料之中的奇怪电话打进来，“你现在穿的内裤是什么颜色？”我马上挂断这类电话。不过，当随之而来的咯咯笑声平息之后，这样的工作跟一般简单的办公室工作没什么两样。我与其他同事建立起的友谊使得工作更加充满乐趣。但是，我焦躁不安的情绪继续日益恶化。

We moved back to my home state of New South Wales. Dean, the man I had been with in England and the Middle East, had moved back to Australia with me. Soon after we relocated back to New South Wales, our relationship came to an end. We had loved each other dearly for years now and had been best friends for most of that time.

> 我搬回到我的家乡新南威尔士州住了下来。陪我在英国和中东一起生活的男朋友迪安也跟我来到了澳大利亚。在我们一起回到新南威尔士之后不久，我们的关系就结束了。我们真诚地相爱了许多年，现在大多数时间我们也是最好的朋友。

It was devastating to watch our friendship fall apart. But our numerous lifestyle differences could no longer be swept under the carpet or laughed off, as had once been done. I was a vegetarian. He was meat eater. Working indoors all week, I longed for outdoor life all weekend. He worked outdoors all week and wanted to be indoors all weekend.

> 眼看着我们的爱情就这样破碎了，我的心简直悲痛到了极点。但是，我们太多的生活方式上的差异再也不可能以睁只眼闭只眼或者一笑置之的方式糊弄过去，虽然以前是这样做的。.我是素食者，而他是食肉者。因为平时上班都是在室内度过，我总希望周末都到户外活动。可他正好相反，整个上班日都在户外活动，老想周末就宅在家里。

The list went on and on, and seemed to grow larger by the week. The things each of us delighted in, no longer delighted the other. A mutual love of music still bonded us and kept us hanging in there for a while. But in the end, the communication channel between us lost its strength and we each battled with our own loss, watching our shared dreams disintegrate before our eyes.

> 我们的差异继续扩大，到了最后似乎发展成为不可调和的巨大分歧。我们各自喜欢做的事情再也不能令对方感到快乐。只有我们双方对音乐的爱还可以把我们联系在一起，让我们暂时保持不分离的信心。但最终，我们俩之间的音乐沟通渠道也失去了它的魅力和作用力，我们各自为自己的失去而挣扎，眼睁睁看着我们共同的梦想在我们的眼前渐渐破碎。

It was a heartbreaking time as the relationship finished and the grief of such loss arrived. As I curled up in a ball sobbing, wishing we could have made it work, I knew in my heart we couldn’t. Life was calling us in different directions and the relationship was now hindering our paths rather than helping them.

> 当这种关系结束以及失去对方的悲痛到来之际，那真的是撕心裂肺的体验。我极度悲伤地蜷成一团，期望我们能够和好如初，但是，我内心明白，那是不可能了。我们各自的生活正在呼唤着我们朝着不同的方向迈进，目前我们这种关系只能阻碍我们各自的人生道路，强行将两个人拉在一起对我们没有任何帮助。

The search for more meaning in my life intensified and as a result, the work issue increased in prominence. Waking up to the fact that being an artist is a very hard way to survive, until your work has gained momentum and a healthy reputation, I had to find a new direction in the meantime. Surviving as an artist was going to be possible eventually. If I could dream it, I could do it after all.

> 强烈追求对自己更加有意义的生活，其结果反而出现更多的问题。当我清醒过来时，我不得不接受这样的现实：除非你的作品获得了一定声势和良好的声誉，否则像一名艺术家那样生存的确很艰难。我不得不同时探索一条新的生活方向。当然，作为一名艺术家，基本生活还是不存在问题。

But I needed to get back to earning and I needed to do it in a field that allowed me to work from the heart and to be my natural self. The pressure of selling products within the banking industry had increased and I had changed too much. I no longer fitted into that world, if I ever truly had. Determined to continue on my creative journey, the choice was made to work as a live-in companion again. At least then I wouldn’t be stuck in a rent or a mortgage grind, which would also free me up from the rigidity of routine.

> 我明白如果我想做什么，无论有多难，终究我能够做到。然而，我需要回到工作岗位上去挣钱，我也需要一份既是我心甘情愿做的，又能保持我的原本自我的工作。在银行销售产品的压力不断增加，使得我改变了太多的自我。如果我真的要在那里工作的话，我想我再也不能融入那种世界，我的自我已经与他们格格不入了。我决心继续我的创作之旅，并选择再次当一名入住陪伴。至少，我不会为一份房租而苦恼，或者被房屋分期付款所折磨，同时我还能够从死板的常规日程中解脱出来。

Despite the years of soul searching that had led up to this point, the final decision was almost a casual, flippant one. Simply, I’d take a job as a companion for the benefit of my creative path and to also work from the heart, all the while enabling me to live rent-free. I had no idea then that my yearnings for a heart-felt job had been so clearly heard, and the years to follow were in themselves going to be such a significant part of my life and my life’s work.

> 尽管许多年的自我反省得出了这个观点，但最终的决定几乎也是偶然的、轻率的。原因很简单，我希望找到一份陪伴人的工作既有利于我的创作道路，也是发自我内心，甘愿做这种工作，而且自始至终我又不必缴纳房租。我万万没有想到我发自内心的渴望之声已经如此清晰地被他人听见。在接下来的几年光阴里，入住陪伴成为我生命及人生工作中的非常重要的组成部分。

Within two weeks, I had moved into a harbour-side home in one of the Sydney’s most exclusive suburbs. Her elderly brother had found my client Ruth unconscious on her kitchen floor. After more than a month in hospital she was allowed home, providing she had twenty-four hour care.

> 不到两周，我搬进了悉尼最高级的一个郊区的港湾边的一栋房子。我的客户鲁思的哥哥发现了她昏倒在厨房地板上。在医院住了一个多月后，她出院了，但她需要24小时有人看护。

My experience in the care industry had only been companionship with Agnes those years prior. I had not looked after sick people and was honest about this to the agency employing me, but they didn’t mind. Carers who were willing to live-in were a commodity and they were not going to let me slip through their net. “Just pretend you know what you’re doing and call us if you need any help.” Holy dooly, welcome to the care-game Bronnie.

> 我的看护从业经验也只是几年前陪伴艾格尼丝时积累的。我从未照顾过病人，我诚实地告诉了职业中介，但他们不介意。愿意入住的看护者们实际上是一种可以交易的商品，中介才不打算让我漏网。“你只需要假装懂得该如何照顾病人就行了，假如你需要帮助，打电话给我们。”我的女神，邦妮，欢迎你加入“看护游戏”行业！

My natural empathy enabled me to do the job reasonably well for a new person. I simply treated Ruth as I would my own grandmother, who had been precious to me. Catering to her needs as they surfaced, I worked it out as I went along. The community nurse would come in every few days and ask me questions about things I had no idea about. Because I was honest with her, she ended up helping me enormously as I learned about medications, personal care, and industry jargon.

> 对于一位护理病患战线上的新人，我那与生俱来的同情心使得我能非常恰当地做好这份工作。我干脆就把鲁思看成是曾经把我当宝贝的亲祖母。满足她任何及时的需求，并且在工作中尽量处理好各种事物。社区专职护士会每隔几天来看望一次，并问我一些我不知道如何回答的问题。由于我对她说了实话，我根本不懂得照顾病人，那位护士停止向我提问。在学习一些医学常识、个人生活护理和该行业的常用术语等方面，她给予了我极大的帮助。

My employers would drop in now and then too. Happy that the client was happy was enough for them and off they’d go. They had no idea I was becoming emotionally and physically exhausted in a rapid time. I am not sure I had yet realised it myself.

> 我的老板会隔三岔五地顺便过来坐坐。足以让他们高兴的是客户那边是快乐的，然后他们就会满意地离去。他们哪里知道这么快的时间里，我的精力和体力已经变得疲惫不堪。但我不知道自己还没有意识到这一点。

Ruth’s family was pleased because I was spoiling Ruth rotten. There were foot massages, manicures, facials, and lots of lovely bedside conversations over cups of tea. Like I say, I treated her as I would my own dear grandmother. I didn’t know any other way.

> 鲁思的家人也感到高兴，因我一直很溺爱鲁思。给她做脚部按摩、修指甲、面部护理以及许许多多时候在床边跟她边喝茶边愉快地交谈。如同我说的，我只把她当成我自己亲爱的祖母，而别无其他的想法。

Ruth would ring her bell throughout the night too and I’d be down the stairs in a flash, helping her onto the bedside commode to go to the toilet. “Oh you are glamorous,” she would to say to me as I wandered in. Her impression of my glamour was that I sometimes wore my hair in a bun to bed, simply because I was too exhausted to get the knots out at the time. And my so-called ‘glamorous’ nightdress was due to my mother’s insistence I take it.

> 在整个夜间，鲁思也会摇铃叫我。每当她摇铃，我会立刻下楼，把她扶起来坐在床边的便桶上解小便。“哦，你真妩媚。”当我漫步走进房间时，她会这样对我说。我给她妩媚的印象是因为我有时候把头发束成一个发卷睡觉，很简单，因我那是太累了，睡觉前没有把头发松开。而我所谓的“妩媚”睡衣是我母亲坚持要我穿的。

“You can’t go to this lady’s place and sleep naked or in any old thing,” Mum had pleaded. “Please take this and promise me you will wear it.” So in respect of my darling mother’s wishes, I found myself wearing a satin nightdress to bed. Glamorous I was, half sleepwalking into her bedroom four or five times a night, eyes struggling to open, longing for reprieve from my exhausted state. Ruth would need me all of the following day too, so little chance was ever presented to catch up on a few hour’s sleep. I was also doing housework, which I tackled during Ruth’s afternoon naps.

> “你不可以光着身子或者穿得旧旧的到化妆间和上床睡觉。”母亲申辩说。“请拿着这个，答应我一定要穿哦。”所以，遵照亲爱的母亲的愿望，我自己找了件缎面睡衣穿在身上。妩媚的我，整晚一半的时间有四五次梦游似的走进鲁思的卧室，了寻求缓解疲惫状态的办法，眼睛挣扎着想睁开。在接下来的整个白天，鲁思还会需要我照顾，甚至，白天很少有机会抓紧去睡上几个小时。同时，在鲁思下午午睡期间，我还得做一些家务事。

Sitting on the commode, she would also want to talk then. Ruth loved all of the attention after years of living on her own. I was enjoying our friendship too, except for hearing about what cups and saucers they had used at whatever dinner party thirty years ago, while she urinated on the commode at three in the morning, my body just longing to return to bed.

> 即使坐在便桶上，鲁思也希望有人跟她聊天。多年的独居生活使鲁思很喜欢别人把所有注意力都放在她身上。但若不是在清晨3点钟，听她坐在便桶上小便时还津津乐道30年前的一个什么聚会上他们曾用过什么样的杯子和碟子，而我的身体却渴望回床睡觉的话，我还是很享受与她的友情。

Over the weeks, Ruth spoke of the years around the bay and of the children playing down by the harbour. Horse and dray, plodding along through all of the quiet streets, had done the milk and bread deliveries. Sundays would see the neighbourhood all dressed in their best clothes for church. Ruth spoke about her children when they were young and of her husband long since passed.

> 几周过后，鲁思聊了那些年在海湾的事，孩子们如何在港口玩耍。马和运货马车缓慢地穿过宁静的街道，运输着牛奶和面包。星期天左邻右舍都穿着盛装到教堂做礼拜。鲁思还聊了她的孩子们小时候的事情，以及很久以前就过世了的丈夫。

Her daughter Heather, who I found to be delightful, would drop in every day or two and was a breath of fresh air. Ruth’s son and his family lived out in the country and had Heather not also mentioned her brother, it would have been easy to forget his existence. He did not play an active role in his mother’s life.

> 她的女儿希瑟是一个令人愉快的人，她会每隔一到两天过来看看，就当出来呼吸新鲜空气。鲁思的儿子及他的家人住在国外，希瑟也很少提到她的弟弟，这好像很容易忘掉了他。对母亲的生活他也没有发挥任何作用。

Heather was the rock that supported Ruth throughout her decades as a widow. Ruth’s elderly brother James also helped. He would wander down from his home about a mile away, every afternoon. You could set the clock by his visit. There he was in the same sweater, day-in, day-out.

> 在鲁思成为了寡妇后的几十年生活中，希瑟如中流砥柱支撑着她。鲁思的哥哥詹姆斯也有帮助她。每天下午，他会从约一英里外的家里散步过来看望她。他每次到访的时间都是固定的。你瞧，他日夜都穿同样的毛衣。

He was already eighty-eight years old and had never married. With a mind as clear as they come, he was a wonderful character and it was my pleasure to know him and to enjoy the simplicity of his life. Ruth was not recovering from her illness however and was still in bed after a month. More tests were done and it was then I was informed that she was dying.

> 他已经88岁了，从未结过婚。有很清晰的头脑，他是一个很棒的人物，我很高兴认识他，并欣赏他简单的生活方式。然而，鲁思的身体并没有得到恢复，一个月过去了还是躺在床上。更多的治疗也无济于事，最后，他们告诉我，她正走向人生尽头。

Walking down to the harbour with tears in my eyes, everything felt surreal. Children were playing in the shallow water. The footbridge hanging over the bay swung lightly as happy people walked over it. Ferries cruised by on their way to Circular Quay in the city centre. I walked as in a dream, as laughter rang out from a group of people having a picnic.

> 我眼含泪水地向港口走去，感觉周围的一切都是超现实的，不是真实存在的。孩子们在浅水边玩耍，快乐的人们走过那跨越海湾的人行桥时，桥轻轻地摇晃着，渡轮按照航线缓慢地驶向城市中心的环形码头，一群正在野餐的人们传来阵阵笑声。可此时我却仿佛在梦中游走。

Sitting against a sandstone cliff, the water almost at my feet, I looked up at the beautiful sky. It was one of those perfect winter days, when the warmth of the sun is like a balm. Sydney is never totally freezing in winter, not like European winters. It was a glorious day, where a light coat was sufficient.

> 坐在砂岩悬崖边，海水几乎淹到了我的脚，我仰望着美丽的天空。温暖的太阳如同香脂油洒在身上，这真是一个完美的冬天。悉尼不像欧洲，完全没有寒冷的冬天。那天的天气非常好，身上穿件薄外套就够了。

Having already grown close to Ruth, the thought of her passing left me tearful for my own inevitable pain. The shock that I would be losing her was my first reaction. My tears flowed as a yacht sailed by, full of happy, healthy people. It then also hit me that I would be her carer, the one nursing her until the end.

> 我已经跟鲁思很亲近了，想到她将死去，我自然而然地感到一阵心酸，眼泪流了出来。因会失去她而感到震惊是我的第一反应。当载满快乐、健康的游客的游艇从我旁边驶过的时候，我的眼泪唰唰地往外流。让我最受到打击的是，我是她的看护者，一个照顾她到最后的人。

Growing up on the cattle farm, then a sheep farm, I had seen a lot of animals dying or dead. It was not new to me, although I was still always terribly sensitive to it. But the society I was living in, the modern society of Western culture, was not one that exposed its people to dying bodies on a regular basis. It wasn’t like some cultures where human death is out in the open and a very visible part of everyday life.

> 从小生长在奶牛场，然后是牧羊场，我见过很多动物的垂死过程和死亡过程。虽然，我永远对死亡非常地敏感，但对我而言，死亡并不是头一回面对。然而，我所生活的这个社会是一个现代化西方文明社会，人们不会大肆渲染死亡。不像有些文化，人们公开认可人类死亡，它是每天生活的一个很明显的组成部分。

Our society has shut death out, almost as a denial of its existence. This denial leaves both the dying person and the family or friends totally unprepared for something that is inevitable. We are all going to die. But rather than acknowledge the existence of death, we try to hide it. It is as if we are trying to convince ourselves that ‘out of sight, out of mind’ really works. But it doesn’t, because we carry on trying to validate ourselves through our material life and associated fearful behaviour instead.

> 而我们的社会已经将死亡拒之门外，几乎否认它的存在。这种否认使得临终者或者他们的亲人和朋友完全没有任何的准备去面对这种不可避免的自然属性的来临。其实我们都将会死去。但是，我们的“文明”不是去承认死亡的存在，而是试图把死亡隐藏起来，这就如同我们试图说服自己“眼不见为净”。然而，那并不管用，因我们一直试图通过我们肉体的生命来验证我们自己，反而产生了相应的恐惧。

If we are able to face our own inevitable death with honest acceptance, before we have reached that time, then we shift our priorities well before it is too late. This gives us the opportunity to then put our energies into directions of true value. Once we acknowledge that limited time is remaining, although we don’t know if that is years, weeks or hours, we are less driven by ego or by what other people think of us. Instead, we are more driven by what our hearts truly want. This acknowledgment of our inevitable, approaching death, offers us the opportunity to find greater purpose and satisfaction in the time we have remaining.

> 在我们的生命尽头到来之前，如果我们能够真实面对不可避免的死亡，那么，我们还可以用仅剩的时间好好地处理自己人生最为重要的事情。这样反而使得我们有机会把自己的精力投入到真正有价值的生活方向上。虽然，我们不知道自己的人生尽头是否在几年、几周或是几天内到来，然而，一旦我们承认了有限生命的时间所剩不多，我们反而更少被自我或者别人怎样评价自己所牵制。取而代之的是，我们的一切行为将更多的由自己内心真正需要的那些愿望所驱动，能更加清楚地明白自己真正想做什么。这种接受一个人不可避免的将走进死亡的心态，可以使我们有机会去发掘出在剩余的时间里有可能实现的更为宏大的生活目标和满足感。

I came to realise how detrimental this denial is in our society. But at the time, on that sunny winter’s day, it simply left me completely ignorant of what lay ahead with Ruth and of my role in caring for her. Resting my head back against the sandstone wall, I prayed for strength. Having already faced plenty of challenges in my upbringing and adult life, I believed I wouldn’t have been brought to this place had I not been capable of doing the job. This didn’t particularly ease my personal sadness and pain.

> 我现在意识到了，在我们这个社会里否认死亡是多么的有害。而在当时那个冬日晴天里我完全不知道鲁思的未来将会怎样，作为她的看护者，我的职责到底是什么。我头往后仰贴在沙岩壁上祈祷着自己要坚强起来。在我的成长以及成年的生活道路上，已经面对过无数次的挑战，虽然我相信，如果我没有能力做这份工作，我不会被带到这个地方，但这种自信并没有减轻我的悲伤和痛苦。

But sitting in the warmth of the sun that day, quiet tears falling, I knew I had a job to do and would give Ruth all of the happiness and comfort I possibly could for her dying weeks. I sat for a long time, contemplating life and how I hadn’t seen this coming. Yet I was also accepting that I had gifts to share and this is what I was being asked to do. Walking back to the house a strong resolve developed within me. I would give this situation the absolute best I had, and would catch up on sleep later.

> 那天，我坐在温暖的太阳底下，眼泪悄悄顺着脸颊往下流，我知道还有一些事情我应该去做，我要尽可能在鲁思临终的时候给予她所有的快乐和安慰。我在沙岩旁坐了很久，仔细思索着这段日子，以及我怎么都没有看出鲁思有今天这种状况的原因。然而，我也承认自己具有与他人分享的天赋，这正是我的工作职责。在我走回鲁思房子的路上，一个强烈的决定从我心中油然而生，我要让鲁思生命最后的阶段过得更为体面，同时会稍稍补上我的睡眠以便更好地应对鲁思的需要。

My employer dropped in later that day. Explaining that I had never even seen a dead person, let alone cared for someone towards their passing, I heard my words fall on deaf ears. “The family loves you. You’ll be right.”

> 那天晚些时候，我的老板也来了。我向他们说明我从未见过一个死人，更不用说是一个我一直在照顾的人走向死亡。我的话被他们置若罔闻。“这个家庭喜欢你。你将不会有事的。”

‘You’ll be right’ (as in, ‘You’ll be alright’), is such a common expression in Australian language that I accepted I would be. Ruth’s deterioration was quite fast from that point. Other carers came in to relieve me on days off and as her needs increased, I was relieved of night duty. I was still called on by the other carers, as I oversaw the running of things. But, at least sleep was now possible.

> “你将不会有事的”，（如同“你会好起来的”）在澳大利亚语言里这是一种很常见的安慰术语，我承认我不会有事。可鲁思的病情却在这种安慰语的伴随下急剧恶化。随着鲁思需要被更多的照顾，又来了其他护理员，以解救我不用值夜班，让我有休息时间。由于我负责这里的一切工作，晚间其他护理员有时候还会叫醒我。但至少现在我可以有睡觉的时间。

The days were still special and more often than not, it was mostly just Ruth and I. It was a quiet neighbourhood, with occasional laughter ringing up through the trees from the harbour-side park below. Heather would drop in on us, as would James and a string of specialists doing their jobs. The learning on offer was immense and I was growing enormously in my role, without then realising the full extent of it. I simply did whatever needed doing and asked a lot of questions from everyone I could.

> 那些日子几乎多半就只有鲁思和我两个人在一起，这对我们俩都非常的特别。周围的邻居很安静，只是偶尔在海港边公园下面的树林里传来阵阵笑声。如同詹姆斯和一连串执行公务的专业人员一样，希瑟也会定时过来看望我们。此刻我提供了很多的学习机会，我的职业能力正在大大地增强，不过我还没有完全掌握所有职能，于是我干脆做一些需要我做的事，同时尽可能向大家请教更多问题。

One morning as I was about to leave for two days off, excited to be heading out of the city to visit my cousin and to enjoy some lightness after the weight of it all, I noticed the smell from the bedroom. The night carer had either not noticed it, or had not wanted to, hoping it could be left for the day carer who was about to come on. I saw a lot of this in coming years.

> 一天清晨，我很兴奋正要准备出门前往表兄家休假两天，享受在高度工作压力之后的片刻轻松时光之时，我注意到了从鲁思卧房里传出的味道。那位夜间护理者既没有注意这些，也不想尽心照顾病人，她正期望尽量把工作留给即将来的白天护理员去做。在以后的几年间，这种事我见得多了。

There was no way I could leave my beautiful friend lying in it for another minute. Her bowels had opened and evacuated completely. Lying limp, Ruth was only able to reply to me with quiet grunts. Her major organs were collapsing.

> 我怎么能让我的漂亮朋友这样躺在那里，再多一分钟都不行。我帮她解了大便和清理完毕后，鲁思软弱无力地躺在床上，她仅能以轻柔的咕噜声回应我。她身体的主要器官都在衰退。

The night carer reluctantly tore herself away from the gossip magazine she was reading and helped me, as we cleaned this gentle woman and changed her sheets from underneath her. It was a relief when the day carer arrived, dropped everything and pitched in immediately with pleasant cheer. We had Ruth clean and resting, and in no time she was in a deep sleep, exhausted.

> 当我们不得不给鲁思清洗身体和换掉她床上的床单时，那个夜间护理员很不情愿地把正在阅读的杂志放下过来帮我。当白天的那位护理员到达后，她放下东西，立刻愉悦地投入了工作，这下我就解脱了。我们帮鲁思清洗干净，让她躺下休息，她立刻进入了深睡眠状态，她太累了。

Sitting out in the bush with my cousin later that day, my heart was still back at the house. Welcoming the lightness and humour his company always provides, I was glad to be hanging out with him. But it was not going to be possible for me to stay away for two nights. Ruth was in my thoughts too much and I was sure she didn’t have that long left.

> 那天晚些时候，我和表兄坐在外面的丛林中休息，可我的心却一直留在鲁思那里。我很乐意接受表兄的陪伴，他说话总是很幽默，让我感到轻松愉快，每次与他外出我都很高兴。但现在对我而言，离开鲁思两个晚上是不可能的了。我满脑子都是鲁思，我敢确定鲁思也不想我离开她太久。

I had only been at my cousin’s for a few hours when my employer called and said that Ruth was in her last stages and could I come? Arriving back around dark, the somber mood of the house was palpable before even walking inside. Heather was there with her husband, as well as the new night carer who had just arrived. She was a lovely Irish girl.

> 正当我在表兄那里才待了几个小时时，我的老板打电话过来，说鲁思正处在她生命的最后阶段，问我可不可以立刻回去。天快黑的时候我回到了那所房子，房间里的忧郁情绪不言而喻，甚至在进屋之前就能察觉得到。希瑟和她的丈夫都来了，一位新的夜间护理员也刚到。她是一位可爱的爱尔兰姑娘。

Heather asked would I mind if she went home? I replied gently that she had to do whatever felt right for her. So home it was. After she had left though, it was initially a little difficult for me to remove judgment from the situation. I could only imagine my own mother dying and how I would move heaven and earth to be with her at her passing.

> 希瑟问我是否介意她回家去？我友善地回答她可以去做任何自己感到合适的事。家就是一个人最为合适去的地方。而她离开后我还是有点难以去除我当初对这种态度的看法。我只能想象假如自己母亲临终之时，我可能会怎样地竭尽全力，想方设法在她生命最后时刻和她在一起。

They say that everything comes down to love or fear: every emotion, every action, and every thought. I concluded that fear was driving Heather’s decision and for that I felt a rush of compassion and love for her. From the start of our association, I had found her to be a very practical person and somewhat detached. But this situation was foreign to me. I didn’t want my own beliefs and conditioning to hinder my regard for someone I had come to care for, simply because she handled things differently to how I would have.

> 有人说，每种情感、每个行动以及每种思想，一切的一切归结于爱或者恐惧。我将希瑟的行为断定为，此刻她离开鲁思的原因可能是出于恐惧。那样的话，我会对她产生一股同情和爱的感觉。从我与她开始交往，我就发现她是一个很现实的人，有点冷漠。然而，在这种情形之下我是局外人。我不要因我的信念和所受的教育妨碍我对曾经关心的人的尊重，很简单，我们俩的处事方式明显不同。

Sitting in the darkened room with Erin, the other carer, I came to accept and respect Heather’s actions. She did what she’d had to do, because she had done all she could. For decades, she had kept her mother’s life in order as well as that of her own family. She was completely and totally exhausted by now, both physically and emotionally. She had given all she possibly could and wanted to remember her mum sleeping peacefully, as she was when Heather left. I smiled with respect and my assumed understanding.

> 当与新来的另外一个护理员艾琳一起坐在漆黑的房间里时，我接受和尊重希瑟的行为反应。她做了她应该做的，因为她尽了她力所能及的一切。几十年来，她不仅照顾好自己的家庭，还维持着她母亲正常有序的生活。事到如今，她的体力和精力已经完全彻底地疲惫不堪了。她尽可能地付出了，现在她只想记住母亲在她离开时那种安静入睡的样子。我默默地微笑了，我尊重希瑟的选择，我认为能理解她。

In conversation with Heather in the days afterwards however, I found out that Ruth had implied to Heather that she wanted her to go. Heather knew her mother well enough to read her wishes. So it had been in love she left, not fear at all. Similar situations also became somewhat familiar in the years to come. Not every dying person wanted his or her family there.

> 然而，几天之后在与希瑟的交谈中，我得知鲁思曾暗示过希瑟，她要希瑟离开。希瑟非常了解她的母亲，完全能够明白她的愿望。因此，希瑟那天离开母亲是出于爱，而不是恐惧。接下来的几年间，我也遇到过与此相似的情况。不是每个临终的人都希望他的家人在那里陪着自己，在他们还神志清醒的时候就已经与亲人们相互道别。

They said their goodbyes while conscious and occasionally preferred to be seen out by carers, allowing their families to keep other memories instead. Erin and I chatted quietly in Ruth’s room, the presence of death lingering. Erin explained how in her family, the room would be filled with everyone by now. Aunts, uncles, cousins, neighbours, and children, everyone would come along to say his or her farewell, to send the person out.

> 偶尔有些人宁愿在最后见见照顾他们的人。他们允许家人在他们的脑海里仅保留以往其他的美好记忆，而不是死亡的回忆。我和艾琳在鲁思的房间里静静地聊着天，拖延至今的死亡就这一夜了。艾琳描述着如果死亡出现在她的家里，此时这屋里肯定会站满了人。叔叔阿姨们、表亲们、邻居及孩子们都会过来跟临终的人道别，最后把他送走。

We fell into spells of silence, both of us looking at Ruth, watching, and waiting. The night was incredibly still as I quietly sent Ruth love from my heart. Erin and I would chat a little again, then go back into silence. She was a beautiful person to share the experience with, as she cared. It was in her naturally to do so.

> 我们俩陷入一段沉默，都盯着鲁思观察和等待着。那晚很是不可思议，我仍然悄悄地向鲁思送去我内心的爱。我和艾琳又聊一会，然后又是沉默。她是一个漂亮女孩，愿意与我分享照顾人的经历。她也是偶然机会开始做这样的工作。

“She’s opened her eyes,” Erin suddenly said to me, startled. Ruth had been in a semi coma for the entire shift so far. “She’s looking at you.” I moved closer to the bed and held Ruth’s hand. “I’m here, Sweetheart. It’s OK.” She looked directly into my eyes and a moment later her spirit began to leave the body. It shook for a short time. Then all was still.

> “她睁开了眼睛。”艾琳惊吓似地突然对我说。整晚中，到目前止，鲁思一直处于半昏迷状态。“她正在看着你。”我靠近床边，握着鲁思的手，“我在这里，亲爱的，不用担心。”她直视着我的眼睛，瞬间过去，她的灵魂开始离开她的躯体。她的身体在很短暂的抽动之后，一切又回到原来状态。

Instantly, tears rolled down my cheeks. Speaking silently to her from my heart, I thanked Ruth for what we had shared, told her I loved her and wished her well on her journey. It was a very reverent moment, full of stillness and love. Standing in the darkened room, all of my senses alive, I silently thought what blessings I had been given by having this time with her.

> 顿时，眼泪顺着我的双颊汹涌而下。我默默地在心里跟她说话，感谢她和我分享了那些时光，告诉她我爱她，但愿她一路走好。这是一个非常虔诚的时刻，充满了宁静和爱。伫立在这个黑暗的房间里，我所有的感官都活跃起来。我默默地想着这一次的工作机会和与鲁思相处的日子，以及我所一直得到的那些祝福。

Then Ruth’s body surprisingly took another huge breath. I jumped back, swearing, my heart beating out of my chest. “Holy Shit!” I said to Erin. She laughed at me. “That’s quite normal you know Bronnie. It happens a lot.”

> 随后，鲁思的身体突然地出现了另外一次深长的呼吸，如人们所说的“回光返照”。我惊吓得后退一步，全身冒着冷汗，心快蹦出我的胸口。我对艾琳说，“我的天那！”她笑我说，“那很正常，邦妮。常有这种事发生。”

“Yes well, thanks for telling me,” I replied in shock, smiling at her. My heart was pounding hard and all of the reverence of the moment was gone. I stepped back beside the bed with great hesitance. “Is it going to happen again?” I whispered to Erin. “It could.”

> “是啊，谢谢你提醒我。”我惊愕地回复她，并对她微笑。我的心怦怦跳得厉害，那瞬间的所有崇敬消失了。我满怀顾虑地走回床边，并低声对艾琳说：“还会再次发生这种情况吗？”“有可能。”

We waited in silence for another minute or so, hardly breathing ourselves. “She’s gone Erin. I can feel she’s gone,” I finally said. “God bless her,” we both uttered quietly, at the same time. Moving our chairs closer, we sat with Ruth for a while in sacred silence and loving respect. I also needed to settle a bit, after the fright of the moment before.

> 我们屏住呼吸，沉静地等待了大约一分钟。最后我说：“艾琳，她走了。我可以感觉到她已经离去。”“愿上帝保佑她吧！我们俩同时轻轻地祈祷。我们将椅子移近床边，带着崇敬的沉默和爱的尊重，靠近鲁思坐了一会儿。在没有被死亡恐惧心理吓倒之前，我还需要处理一点事情。

Heather and my employer had asked me to call them when it had happened, which I did. It was about two thirty in the morning. There was no more either of them could do now. I’d also been directed earlier that day with how to proceed from here. So I called the doctor to come and issue a death certificate. When that was done, the funeral home was called.

> 希瑟和我的老板要求过我，当鲁思走的时刻，打电话通知他们，我照着做了，这大约是清晨的2点半钟。此刻，他们也没有什么事可以做的。之前他们教了我有哪些程序需要去做。因此，我给医生打了电话，要求开死亡证明书。当死亡证明书开好之后，我又给殡仪馆打了电话。

Erin and I sat in the kitchen until Ruth’s body was taken away about the time the sun was coming up. During those hours of waiting, we both went back to look in on Ruth at times. It was a compulsion to still care for her body, even though she had departed from it. I didn’t like her being in the room alone. The strange, dark time afterwards was very special in a way. But there was also a tangible emptiness in the home that night, after she had passed over.

> 我和艾琳一直坐在厨房里，直到大约太阳升起的那个时候他们把鲁思抬走。在等待的那几个小时期间，我们俩几次回到房间查看鲁思。尽管她已经去世了，我们还是习惯性地关心她的躯体。我不喜欢她独自躺在房间里。事过之后的那阵奇怪和忧闷体验，在某种程度上倒显得很特别。不过，鲁思去世后的那个晚上，这个家确实有种空虚感。

Ruth’s home was offered to me the next day as a house-sitting situation. Heather said it would take months for the estate to settle and rather than leave the house empty, the family would feel more secure to have someone living in it. So I went on to live in Ruth’s home for some time afterwards, which was a blessing for my physical situation. It was also good to be somewhere that had now become familiar to me.

> 由于需要有人看房子，第二天我还是住在鲁思的家。希瑟说把房子推出市场卖掉需要几个月，与其说让这个房子空着，倒不如有人住在里面，这样他们才会感到更安全些。所以，在这之后，我在鲁思的房子里多住了一些时间，也算是给了我一个福利让我有栖身之地。同时，这也成为了现在我所熟悉的一个好地方。

I had realised the around-the-clock live-in work was going to be too exhausting. Never being able to do anything by halves, I now understood I would need to remove myself from future patients between shifts, by going home each night. Care work asked much more of me than straightforward companionship. Over the following months, I watched and assisted as Heather moved Ruth’s belongings onto new places.

> 我意识到，这种全天候的入住照顾病人工作太让人劳累，仅有的零碎时间不可能做好其他任何事情。现在我明白了，对于以后的病人客户，我需要抽出身来，每个晚上回家住。对我来说，看护工作比直截了当的陪伴工作需要做的杂事多多了。在接下来的几个月里，我一边照看房子，一边帮助希瑟把鲁思的东西搬到一个新的地方。

Her physical world was dismantled one piece at a time, as happens to everyone. I had been nomadic for so long that I still had an aversion to owning too much. As a result, I declined many things Heather kindly offered to me. They were just things after all and while they had belonged to my friend Ruth, I knew her memory would stay on in my heart, as it has.

> 如同每个死去的人那样，鲁思的物质世界一次次被拆除成一个个小块。由于太长时间的流浪生活，以至于我仍然讨厌拥有很多的东西。结果，我谢绝了希瑟好心留给我的很多东西。虽然它们是我的朋友鲁思使用过的，毕竞，那只是些物品而已。我知道，有关她的记忆会留在我的心里，的确如此。

I did fall in love with a couple of old lamps though and they are with me to this day. Ruth’s home was later demolished by the new owners and replaced with a modern concrete creation. The old frangipani tree that had thrown summer scents throughout the home for decades was crushed in the blink of an eye and replaced with a lap pool. An invitation arrived for me to the housewarming party of the new place.

> 我当时爱上了鲁思的两盏老式台灯，如今还在使用。后来，鲁思的房子在建筑结构上被新买主摧毁，由现代混凝土结构所取代。那棵几十年来使整个房子弥漫着夏季芬芳的古老赤素馨花树一眨眼的工夫被彻底粉碎，那块地方变成了一个小型健身游泳池。我被邀请参加了这座新房的乔迁聚会。

The people who had bought Ruth’s home had been uncomfortable with the spiders and their webs across the trees in the garden. Yet Ruth and I had previously sat in the sun room watching the Golden Orb spider weave a web so strong you could lift it up to walk under. It was a marvel we had both loved and shared. Standing near the lap pool, looking at all of the new, fashionable plants that had replaced an established garden of love and longevity, I was delighted to see a Golden Orb spider weaving her web high across one of the new plants.

> 买鲁思房子的人不喜欢花园里的树上有 蛛和蜘蛛网。而以前我和鲁思常坐在日光屋里观赏着金色球体 蛛在花园里编织 蛛网。那种网很结实，你可以把它举起来，然后从下面走过。这非常令人惊奇，我们喜欢这些蛛并与它们分享。站在游泳池旁，望着那些取代了充满着爱和长寿花园的新的时尚植物和盆景时，我还是很高兴地看到一个金色球体 蛛正在一株高高的植物上编织它的网。

I sent love to Ruth with a smile and knew that in her own way, she was visiting me there that day. Her home may be gone but her spirit was with me. I thanked the new owner for the invitation, had a chat and walked down to the harbour. Sitting where I had the day I first heard about Ruth being terminally ill, I felt grateful for all we had shared and for all I had learned through our association.

> 我微笑着给鲁思送去一丝爱意，我知道她也会以同样方式对我微笑，那天她准会来拜访我的。虽然她的家没了，但她的灵魂与我同在。感谢新的主人邀请了我，我们一起聊着天向港口走去。同样坐在那天刚听到鲁思病危消息后坐过的地方，我的心中充满感激之情，感谢与鲁思所分享的一切以及通过我们相处我所学到的所有东西。

On that summer’s day, I smiled realising just how much more I had been given back then, a lot more than just living rent-free. As the happy day unfolded before me, I continued to smile in gratitude. And by having directed my vision to that Golden Orb spider, Ruth had already smiled back.

> 在这个特别的夏日，我欣喜地意识到，我得到了如此多的回报，这远远比简单的免费居住要多得多。当这幸福的一天展现在我的眼前时，我继续保持着感激的微笑。望着那个金色球体蚋蛛，我感觉到了鲁思也正在向我微笑。
