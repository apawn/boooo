# Regret 4: I wish I had stayed in touch with my friends · 保持联系

Occasional shifts were done in nursing homes, in between regular clients in their private houses. They were not common shifts, to which I was grateful as I found these places absolutely awful. The clients attended to in these situations were not always palliative either. They were just people in some need of help, and sometimes I was employed simply to be an extra staff member in an existing team, rather than looking after any one particular client.

> 在定期的私家护院客户的间隙，我也在几家养老院做过临时的轮班工作。这些养老院的客户们并不都是姑息护理病人，有些只是一些需要帮助的人。我在那里的工作有时候仅仅是作为他们护理团队的额外替补成员，不特定照顾哪个客户。

If ever one wants to live in denial about the state of our society, avoid nursing homes. If ever you feel strong enough to look at life honestly, spend some time in one. There are a lot of lonely people in them – a lot. Any of us could become a patient at any time. Being exposed to the staff on these occasions was both devastating and inspiring. Some of those I worked with briefly over the years were beautiful, goodhearted people who were obviously working in the right field. Their spirits were bright, their hearts kind. Thank goodness for these people. But as most nursing homes were understaffed, they were constantly challenged to spread their good cheer around.

> 〔补译〕若想对社会的现状视而不见，就别去养老院。若是哪天有勇气正视生活，就去那里待一阵子。里面有许多孤单的人，真的很多。我们谁都可能在某一天成为其中一员。那几次接触到的工作人员，既让我心碎，也让我振奋。这些年来，有些与我短暂共事的人善良又热心，显然选对了行当。他们精神爽朗，心肠也好。幸亏有他们。可大多数养老院人手不足，要把这份好心情分给每个人，对他们始终是个难题。

At the other end of the scale were those who had either grown weary and despondent working in the field, or had never had the enthusiasm in the first place. Empathy goes a long way in life and was lacking terribly in the team I was placed with on the night I met Doris. The residents shuffled into the communal dining room on their walking sticks and frames. They were people from relatively decent means, as it was a private and so-called ‘luxurious’ nursing home. The décor was lovely, the gardens well maintained, the communal areas clean. But the meals were awful. Everything was pre-cooked outside of the home and reheated in the microwave, without taste or enticing aroma. There was nothing nutritious or fresh about any food I saw there. Residents placed their orders at the end of the previous week and usually had a plate of something slid in front of them, with no greeting or kindness from the staff.

> 〔补译〕也有人在这一行做得疲倦灰心，或者从来就没对它有过热情。待人能设身处地，日子会大不一样。可我遇见桃瑞丝的那晚，和我搭班的人恰恰缺少这一点。住客们拄着手杖，扶着助行架，慢慢走进公共餐厅。这是一家号称“豪华”的私立养老院，住客的经济条件都还不错。屋里布置得很美，花园打理得好，公共区域也整洁。饭菜却糟透了。全是在外面预先做好，再用微波炉加热的，既无滋味，也闻不到诱人的香气。我在那里没见过什么新鲜、有营养的食物。住客早在上周末就点好了菜，工作人员往往只把盘子往他们面前一推，连声招呼也没有。

Seeing a cheerful face, they would touch my hand to keep me at the table talking with them. These were regular people whose minds were clear and who loved social interaction. Their bodies were ageing and becoming frail, but that was all. A year or two earlier these same charming, delightful people were living completely independent lives of their own. As I returned to the kitchen for another tray of plates, scowls from some of the staff awaited me. I had merely chatted and laughed with some of the residents briefly on my round and this was met with disapproval. I just ignored it.

> 〔补译〕见到一张笑脸，他们便伸手碰一碰我的手，想留我在桌旁说会儿话。他们都是头脑清楚、喜欢与人交往的普通人，只是年纪大了，身体弱了，仅此而已。一两年前，这些可爱风趣的人还完全独立地过着自己的日子。我回厨房去端下一托盘饭菜时，有几个工作人员正对我皱眉头。我不过是在送餐时跟几位住客说笑了几句，他们就看不惯了。我没理会。

Returning a plate of lamb, I told the head person in a friendly manner, “Bernie ordered chicken, not lamb”. She half laughed replying, “He’ll get what he’s bloody-well given”. “Come on,” I said, not intimidated by the nonsense. “Surely we can give him a chicken dish.” “He’ll get lamb or he’ll starve,” she said bitterly. I looked at her with compassion for her obvious unhappiness, but with no respect for her manner in the role she was playing. A lovely staff member fell into step with me as I returned the lamb to Bernie. “Don’t worry about her Bronnie. She’s always like that,” Rebecca said.

> 〔补译〕我把一盘羊肉端回去，和气地对负责人说：“伯尼点的是鸡肉，不是羊肉。”她半笑着回我：“给他什么，他就得吃什么。”我没让这荒唐话吓住。“得了，”我说，“给他一份鸡肉总可以吧。”她没好气地说：“要么吃羊肉，要么就饿着。”我看得出她自己也不快乐，心里同情她，可我无法认同她这样对待住客。我把羊肉端回去给伯尼时，一位亲切的同事赶上了我。“邦妮，别往心里去。她总是这样。”丽贝卡说。

I smiled, glad for a genuine heart. “I’m not worried about her at all. It’s the residents I care about who have to live with this treatment day in and day out.” Rebecca agreed, “It used to affect me a lot when I first started here. But now I just do what I can to give them all the kindest treatment possible, within my limitations”. “Good on you,” I replied with a smile. She rubbed my back as she walked off in another direction. “There are a few of us who care, not enough, but there are a few.”

> 〔补译〕我笑了，很高兴碰到一个真心待人的人。“我一点也不在意她。我在意的是那些住在这里、天天要受这种待遇的人。”丽贝卡点点头。“我刚来的时候，心里也特别难受。现在，我只尽我所能，在能力范围内尽量对他们好一点。”“你做得真好。”我笑着说。她往另一个方向走，随手轻轻抚了抚我的背。“我们有几个人是真正在乎他们的。人不够多，可总还有几个。”

When the meals were served and somehow eaten, and the kitchen was clean, some of the staff members went outside for a smoke. A few of us stayed inside and chatted to the departing residents. It was jovial, as a dozen or so people gathered around to share a laugh with us. Their wit and cheerful spirits amazed me as I marvelled at the resilience of these people, having adapted so well to their new conditions.

> 〔补译〕饭送完了，住客们总算勉强吃了，厨房也收拾好了。有几位同事出去抽烟。我们几个人留在里面，陪正要离开餐厅的住客聊天。十来个人围在一起说笑，气氛很快活。他们的机智和兴致让我惊喜。到了这种新环境，他们竟能适应得这么好，那份韧劲让我佩服。

Each of the residents had their own room and bathroom. As I did the nightly round of helping people to get dressed for bed, each room revealed some personality of its resident. Photos of smiling families, paintings, crocheted rugs and favourite teacups dotted each room. Some balconies had pot plants on them.

> 这里的每位居民有他们自己的房间和卫生间。虽然这里有许多人，可是他们却很少得到“真正的友谊”或“真正的关心”。这里每个房间都展现了一些体现个人特质的东西，比如家庭成员微笑的照片、各式的画、用钩针编织的毯子以及自己喜爱的茶杯等等布满各个房间。有些阳台还放有盆栽植物。夜班的时候，我会巡视各个房间，并帮助需要的人换睡衣。

Doris was already dressed in her pink nightdress when I breezed in cheerfully, introducing myself. But she just smiled without saying anything then looked away. Asking if she was OK, I was then met with a flood of tears. Immediately sitting beside her on the bed, I held her in my arms. No words were spoken while she sobbed, holding onto me desperately. I prayed for strength and waited.

> 当我轻松愉快地走进桃瑞丝的房间并作自我介绍的时候，她穿着她的那件粉红色睡裙。她只是对我微笑一下，不讲话，随后把目光从我身上移开。在我关心她是否都好的时候，我看到眼泪立刻唰唰地从她眼睛里流了出来。我立刻坐在她旁边，手托着她的手臂。在她伤心时我保持沉默，我只是尽力抓住她的手臂，我祈祷给我力量和耐心去等候。

When the tears stopped, it was as quickly as they had started and she reached for her handkerchief. “Oh silly me,” she said wiping her eyes. “Forgive me, Sweetheart. I’m just being a silly old woman.” “What’s going on with you?” I asked gently.

> 她的眼泪来得快去得也快，在瞬间停止了，她伸手拿起她的手帕，边擦眼泪边说：“噢，我真傻。亲爱的，原谅我。我只是一个愚蠢的老太婆。”我温柔地问她：“你到底怎么啦？”

Doris sighed then shared how she had been there for four months and had hardly seen a cheerful face since. She said my smile had set her tears off, which almost made me want to cry. Her one daughter now lived in Japan and while she was in touch fairly often, they were no longer terribly close. “You never think as a mother nursing your beautiful little girl that anything could ever take away your closeness. But it has. Life has. Not through an argument, mind. Just life and its busyness,” she shared.

> 桃瑞丝叹了口气之后讲述了她在这里已经住了四个月，可她几乎没有见到一张笑脸。她说是我的微笑让她流泪。听到这里，几乎也让我想哭。她唯一的女儿现居住在日本，以前她经常跟她联系，现在她们再也不那么亲密了。“你永远想象不到一位母亲养育了自己漂亮的小女孩，结果任何事情都有可能剥夺你们之间的亲密关系。可的确是这样，生活就是这样。不是心智上的分歧，只是现实生活，忙碌的生活造成的。”

“She has her own life now and I learned over the years you just have to let go. I brought her into the world, but we do not own our children. We are just blessed with the role of guiding them until they can fly on their own and that’s what she is doing now.” I warmed to this dear lady instantly and promised to return in half an hour for a longer chat, if she could stay awake long enough for me to finish my shift. She said she would love that.

> 她继续给我讲述：“她现在有她自己的生活，这些年我懂得了很多，你只能放她走。我虽把她带到了这个世界，但我们不能拥有自己的孩子。我们得到的祝福只是担当养育和引导他们的角色，直到他们能够为自己自由飞翔。我女儿现在就是自由的过着自己的生活。”我立刻拥抱了这位可爱的女人，并答应她如果她可以保持不睡等我的话，过一个半小时我下班之后再回来跟她聊天。她说她很乐意跟我聊天。

Later then, Doris sat up in bed talking away. In a chair beside her, I listened. She held my hand the whole time, now and then playing with my fingers or the ring I was wearing, without realising she was doing so. “I have been dying of loneliness in here, Sweetheart. I had heard it was possible and it is. Loneliness can surely kill you. I get so starved for human touch at times,” she said sadly. My hug had been the first she had had in four months.

> 稍晚，我来陪伴她。桃瑞丝坐在床上打开话匣子。我坐在她的床边专心听她讲述。她一直握住我的手，时不时不由自主地弄弄我的手指和我手上戴的戒指。“亲爱的，我将在这里孤独地等死。即使没有躯体的疾病，但孤独必定可以杀了你。已经很长时间没有人跟我亲近了，我太渴望生活能有人情味。”她很伤感地说道。她已经在这里住了四个月，才第一次拥有了我的拥抱。

She didn’t want to burden me with it but I insisted on her continuing. I was genuinely interested in knowing her, so she went on. “I am missing my friends most of all. Some have died. Some are in situations like me. Some I have lost touch with. I wish I hadn’t lost touch with them. You imagine your friends will always be there. But life moves on and suddenly you find yourself with no one in the world who understands you or who knows anything about your history.” I suggested we try to reach some of them. She shook her head saying, “I wouldn’t know where to start”.

> 她不想让我因听到这些而给我带来任何负担，可我坚持要她继续讲下去。我真的有兴趣想了解她，于是，她又继续对我述说：“我几乎失去了我的所有朋友。有些过世了，有些跟我现在的情形一样，有些已经失去了联络。我希望我没有跟他们失去联系就好了。你以前一直想象你的朋友将永远在你的身边。可是生活总是在变化，某一天你猛然发现这个世界上再也没有人理解你或者了解你过去的所有经历。”我提议，我们可以尝试找到她的某些朋友。她摇了摇头说：“我不知道从哪里开始去找。”

“I can help,” I offered as I went onto explain about the Internet to her. It was all very foreign to Doris and she did well to understand it to a degree. At first she declined, worried about my time. Finally though, I convinced her I would love to.

> “我可以帮你找。”我自告奋勇表示愿意帮她，并给她讲述了有关网络的知识。网络对桃瑞丝来说简直是天方夜谭，听了我的解释她懂了一点点。刚开始她谢绝了我的好意，她担心耽误我的时间。最后我说服了她我乐意帮她。

Investigative skills were something I enjoyed. During my years in banking, I had worked for a short time in frauds and forgeries and had loved it. She laughed at the comparison. “Please allow me,” I requested. So she agreed with a hopeful, wistful smile.

> 对我而言，用先进技术调查东西是一种享受。我在银行工作的那些年，我曾经做过有关调查诈骗和伪造的工作，我很喜爱这种工作。她勉强地笑了笑。我请求她说：“请让我试试吧。”最后，她带着期望，微笑着答应了我的请求。

I wanted to help Doris for a few reasons. I had liked her from that first moment and I could help her. I had the skills to try to find her friends but also wanted to help because I knew how she felt. I had also known the crippling pain of long-term loneliness and the longing for understanding.

> 我愿意帮助桃瑞丝有几个原因。当我第一眼看到她我就喜欢上她，我就觉得我可以帮助她。我不仅具有能力可以尽量帮她找到朋友，而且我了解她的感受，愿意去帮她。并且我还懂得长时期的孤独所带来的那种沉重的痛苦和对被理解的渴望。

In previous times, the pain of my past had worn me down to such a place that I had withdrawn a long way into myself. It was the misled belief many people experience, that if you keep people out, you keep the pain out. You stop yourself from being hurt even more. If no one can get close, then no one can hurt you anymore either. Of course the only real way to heal is to let love flow in and through again, not to block it out, but reaching that point can take a long time.

> 过去很多时候，我以往的痛苦也曾经把我折磨到了这种地步，我不跟任何人联络，把自己封闭起来。我想很多人都体验过这种错误的信念：如果你远离人群，你就会远离痛苦，你就再也不被伤害。如果没有人跟你亲近，那么，也就没有人再会伤害你。可这样做的结果是你往往会更加痛苦。唯一真正可以疗伤的方法是让爱重新进入你的心田，不要阻挡它。然而，要了解到并且做到这一点需要很长的时间。

On the surface I was a friendly person to those I came across but the pain I was carrying from my difficult past was still weighing me down. I had definitely grown into a place of compassion by now for those who had previously thrown their negativity my way. That wasn’t the problem. It was my thoughts of myself that were still taking some time to be transformed. Decades of negative thinking were being undone and at times the pain was unbearable. Although I knew intellectually that I was worth more than what I had been conditioned to believe, emotionally the healing still had distance to go.

> 对那些与我只是泛泛之交的人来说，表面上我是一个和蔼的人，但是，我过去的种种经历所带给我的伤痛依然使我萎靡不振。对于那些以前以负面眼光看待我的人来说，我现在肯定已经成长为很有同情心的人。这些我都无所谓。关键是我自身的思想依然需要一些时间来转变。几十年的悲观思想正在被消除，很多时候痛苦仍难以忍受。虽然我很明智地知道我是有价值的，比平时习惯性自我认定的更有价值感，但是从情感上讲，彻底疗伤还有一段路要走。

‘Sunday morning coming down’ became my theme song. Having always loved Kris Kristofferson’s music and being quite influenced by him in my own writing, I found that song to be the best expression of my loneliness. Sundays were always the worst. Lucinda Williams wrote a good song about this too, singing “I can’t seem to make it through Sundays”.

> 《星期天的早上来到了》这首歌是我生活的写照。我永远热爱克里斯•克里斯托弗森的音乐，我在自己的歌曲创作中受到了他的很大影响，我发现这首歌最恰当地表达了我的孤独感。星期天永远是最坏的一天。露辛达•威廉斯也写了一首类似的歌，歌中唱到“星期天里我依然孤独”。

It wasn’t just Sundays though. Loneliness leaves emptiness in the heart that can physically kill you. The ache is unbearable and the longer it hangs around, the more despair adds to it. Miles of city streets, country roads, and everything in between were walked during those years. Loneliness isn’t a lack of people. It is a lack of understanding and acceptance. Huge amounts of people the world over have experienced loneliness in crowded rooms. In fact, being alone in crowded rooms often highlights and exacerbates loneliness.

> 这不仅是星期天，孤独造成的内心空虚感可以杀害你的身体。疼痛难以忍受，孤独越久，绝望越深。在过去那些年间，我穿过了城市里的大街小巷，走过了无数条的乡村小道，经历了种种事情。孤独和寂寞不是缺乏与人交往，而是缺乏理解和接纳。在拥挤的人群中拥抱全世界的人也会体验到孤独。事实上，即使是在人满为患的地方，独自一人仍旧会感到孤独，甚至会加重孤独体验。

It doesn’t matter how many people are around you. If there is no one available who understands you, or accepts you as who you are, loneliness can very readily present its agonising self. It is very different than being alone, as I had loved this often in the past. Being alone can mean you are lonely or you are happy. Loneliness is a longing for the company of one who understands you. Sometimes being alone and loneliness are related, but very often they are not.

> 在你的周围有多少人并非很紧要。如果那些人可以理解你或者接纳和包容你，既使一个都不在你的身边，孤独也不太容易出现来打扰你。这与真正的孤独一人有所不同。

The loneliness became so unbearable, the ache in my heart so constant, suicide became an occasional accompanying thought. I didn’t want to die at all of course. I wanted to live. But realising my own worth, not what I had been led to believe, and breaking free of the pain, sometimes took incredible strength to master. Allowing love and happiness to flow back through my life, to even accept I deserved it, was so unbearably difficult at times that the option of suicide felt more appealing.

> 孤独让人难以忍受，心痛感觉持续性地折磨着我，以至于自杀的念头偶尔会出现在我脑海里。当然，我根本不想死，我要活着。而有的时候，意识到我的自身价值和需要摆脱痛苦使我有巨大的力量去掌控一切。在大多数时间里想让爱和快乐回到我的生活是多么艰难，简直难以忍受。

As the pain and loneliness finally became too much to bear, when I had reached the most painful place so far, my prayers were answered via an act of kindness and understanding. A friend rang at the perfect time. He knew I was going through some hard stuff but he didn’t know at that very moment I was writing my exit letter through slow, heartbroken tears. I was ready to go. I just couldn’t live with the constant ache in my heart anymore.

> 当我到达了痛苦的极点，那些痛苦和孤独即将变得再也无法忍受的时候，我过去所坚持的友善和理解的行 得到了回报。我的一位朋友恰好在这个紧要关头打电话给我。他知道了我现在正经历一些最艰难的事情，可是，他并不知道此时此刻我正在伤心欲绝，边流眼泪边缓慢书写着我的遗书。我已经准备离去，我再也不能承受一次次的打击，在绵绵不断的心痛中苟活下去。

He insisted I didn’t have to say a word. I just had to listen. So via my exhaustion and tears, I reluctantly agreed. Through the phone I heard him begin to play his guitar then the words “Starry, starry night” from Don McLean’s song “Vincent” started to reach my ears as he sang to me, replacing Vincent with Bronnie.

> 他在电话中坚持强调我不必说任何话，只需听他讲。由于我已经疲惫不堪，泪流满面，我勉强地答应了他。电话中，我听到他开始奏起吉他，唱着纳唐•麦克菜恩的歌曲《文森特》“繁星点点，繁星点点的夜晚⋯⋯”，他在为我歌唱，他把“文森特”这个名字改唱成我的名字邦妮。

My tears just fell harder as I related to the song, its tragedy and pain, the gentle melody telling the story of Vincent’s Van Gogh’s own suffering. When he had finished, I continued to sob. There was nothing else I could do. He sat patiently in silence, and then I thanked him and hung up the phone still crying. I wasn’t capable of more words at the time.

> 当我联想到这首歌中所表现的那种悲剧和痛苦时，我的眼流得更加凶猛，这首歌以温柔的旋律讲述了文森特的梵高的悲惨故事。当他唱完后，我依然在哭泣。我没有什么话可以说。他在那边默默地耐心等我说话。最后，我只说了声谢谢，哭着挂了电话。那一刻，我再也不能够吐出更多的字。

Falling asleep that night I was totally drained and emotionally exhausted. I acknowledged though, that through my friend’s understanding and kind intentions, a small pilot light of hope had at least been reignited. The following evening a friend from England rang out of the blue. We talked long and honestly, and my strength slowly started to return little by little.

> 那一晚，我是在生理上筋疲力尽、在情绪上心灰意冷下睡着了。我明白了，借助朋友的理解和善意，至少一束小小的希望之光已经再度点燃。第二天晚上我的一位英国朋友也突然打电话给我。我们真诚地聊了很久，我心中的力量又开始一点一点地慢慢恢复。

On another very difficult occasion though, sometime later but during those same lonesome years, I was pleading and praying for help, trying so hard to be strong. At the time I was driving into town and I hit a bird. It had been of a decent size and the noise on the windscreen was enough to wake me up.

> 在那些寂静的年月里，稍后我又遇见另外的非常困难的、难以面对的生活状况，我恳求和祈祷有人帮我，历尽全力希望能坚强起来。那时我开车到镇上去，突然撞死了一只鸟。那只鸟的体积不大不小，刚好撞在我的挡风玻璃上，那声音把我惊醒了。

Of course being an animal lover, this made me feel even more abysmal in a way but it was a good wake-up call. Life can be over as quick as that and did I really want mine to be so? I thanked the bird for the role it had played in my evolution and drove on with more mindfulness. Just then a piece of classical music came onto the radio, lifting me into the most beautiful place.

> 这算是给我敲响了一次警钟。一个生命就那样瞬间结束了，难道我真的希望自己的生命也像这样快速完结吗？感谢这只鸟在我的思想进化中扮演了这样一个角色，我更加小心地继续驾驶我的车。正好一段古典音乐从车内收音机里播出，那音乐把我带到了最为美丽的地方。

The incredibly delicate sounds soothed me, gently wiping away my heartache. Instead, I was blessed with a beautiful and inspiring moment as the music soared. I decided then that this is what life was about: Beautiful moments of purity. That’s it, as simple as that. Beautiful moments. And I wanted to live, to experience and acknowledge more of them.

> 那不可思议的微妙而细腻的乐曲声安抚了我，温柔地抹去了我内心的伤痛。我在乐曲中那美丽且鼓舞人心的一刻获得了祝福。随后，我明白了，生活就是这个样子：由一个个美丽纯洁的瞬间所组成。是啊，就是这么简单，美妙的分分秒秒。所以，我要活着，要去体验和认识更加神奇的瞬间。

Having previously been to this level of sadness and loneliness though, I now understood that the pain Doris was experiencing was real and tangible for her. She was around people at meal times, and here and there during the day. But she was longing for understanding and acceptance, missing her friends because they were the people who truly understood her. If I could help to ease that pain, then why wouldn’t I?

> 有了曾经的悲伤和孤独体验，我非常理解桃瑞丝正在经历的这种真真切切的痛苦。虽然每天她在吃饭时间，以及在养老院各处都能见到周围的人，但是，她特别渴望被理解和接纳。她想念她的那些朋友，因他们能够真正地理解她。如果我能够帮助她缓解痛苦，为什么不去做呢？

The following week I dropped in to find a list of names waiting for me in the dear lady’s lovely handwriting. Doris told me what she could about the four friends and where they had been living when she had last made contact. We drank tea as she shared their stories with me.

> 第二个星期，我又去了养老院，可爱的桃瑞丝用漂亮的手写体写了一张名单给我。她告诉我她可能还有四个女性朋友尚在人世，以及她们住在哪里，她们最后一次联络的时间。我们边喝茶，边聊她的朋友们。

One of the women was easy to locate, but she had suffered a stroke and could no longer talk. With news of this, Doris dictated a brief message that her friend’s son would read on to her. While she was saddened to hear of her friend’s state, she was peaceful knowing a message could at least be passed on.

> 其中一位朋友很容易就找到了，可惜她患了脑出血，再也不能讲话。桃瑞丝得知了这个消息之后，决定写一个简短的纸条，这样朋友的儿子会读给她听。在听到朋友令人伤心的状况之时，她的心情格外平静，因为她知道至少这个纸条会被传送到她的那位朋友那里。

Dear Elsie. I am sorry to hear you are not well. Years have flown by. Alison is still living in Japan. I sold the house and I am in a nursing home. A young lady is writing this for me. I love you Elsie. Yours Sincerely, Doris.

> 亲爱的埃尔希：我很抱歉得知你的身体状况不是很好。光阴荏苒，岁月如梭。艾丽森一直住在日本。我卖掉了我的房子，现住在养老院。一位年轻的小姐帮我写了这张纸条。我爱你，埃尔希。

It was simple, but said everything she wanted to. I called Elsie’s son that night and passed the words on. He called me back later telling me how Elsie had smiled in delight. I passed this onto Doris, which then left her with a smile of content.

> 你诚挚的桃瑞丝纸条的内容很简单，但也说出了桃瑞丝想要说的话。当天晚上我就给埃尔希的儿子打了电话，把纸条的内容告诉了他。晚些时候，他给我回了电话，告诉我埃尔希当时有多么的愉快并难得地笑了。我又把这一切转告给了桃瑞丝，然后，她也露出了会意的笑容。

Over the next few weeks, I managed to find out about another two of her friends. Sadly they had both already passed over. Doris nodded in acceptance of this. Sighing she said, “Well, it was probably to be expected, Sweetheart”.

> 接下来的几个星期里，我积极地找出了她的另外两个朋友的下落。不幸的是她们俩都已经去世了。桃瑞丝点点头，接受了这个现实。她叹了叹气说：“嗯，亲爱的，这可能就是预料之中的结果。”

The pressure to find the last friend left me very determined. Scanning the Internet and making numerous phone calls, things were not looking good. People were lovely and helpful when I called but, “Sorry. Right name. Wrong family,” became a familiar reply. In the meantime, I still visited Doris twice a week. She would always hold one of my hands as soon as I sat down, and for the duration of our conversations.

> 寻找最后一位朋友的过程很艰难，但这让我更加坚定一定要找到。我在网络上寻找，也打了许多电话查询，看来不是那么顺利。每当我打去电话，人们都很热心地帮助我，“对不起，名字是对的，但不是这家。”成了一种熟悉的答复。与此同时，我仍然每周去看望桃瑞丝两次。只要我一坐下来，在我们谈话期间，她总是握住我的手。

Sometimes insisting I must have better things to do, she would try to rush me off or convince me not to come. When I assured her I was gaining great pleasure from our time too, which I was, I would see the relief on her face and the eagerness for each new visit. There is much to learn from older people, so much history is carried forward with them. How could I not enjoy our delightful conversations? They were fascinating.

> 有的时候，她坚持说想必我有更需要的事要去做，尽量催我离开或者说服我不要再来了。当我让她相信我也从我们的相处里获得了很多快乐的时候，我看得出她的脸上表现出轻松愉快以及对再一次探望的渴望。我们从老人那里可以学到很多的东西，他们在他们的经历中所积累的经验值得我们借鉴。我又怎么能不享受我们愉快的交谈呢？我们在相互吸引对方。

A breakthrough finally came while looking for the last friend. I received a phone call from an elderly man who said he had once been Lorraine’s neighbour. He told me what suburb the family had moved on to and I was successful in tracking her down. In fact, it was Lorraine herself who answered the phone in her elderly, but friendly voice. Explaining who I was and my intention, she gasped in joy and wholeheartedly agreed for me to give Doris her number.

> 在我寻找她的最后一位朋友的时候，一个重要的突破点出现了，我接到了自称曾经是洛琳邻居的一位老先生打来的电话。他告诉了我洛琳的家迁移到了郊区，我很成功地找到了她的电话。事实上，是洛琳亲自接的电话，虽然她的年纪很大，但声音却很温柔。我跟她说明了我是谁以及打这通电话的目的之后，她高兴得不得了，真心诚意地请我把她的电话号码给桃瑞丝。

Naturally, I took it straight in to her. Hugging Doris as I grinned, I then handed her the piece of paper with Lorraine’s name and number on it. She grabbed me again and hugged me full of excitement. It was precious. Signalling for me to bring the phone, I couldn’t get it fast enough for her. Before the number was dialled though, I said I would leave her to her phone call rather than just sit in on them both.

> 当然，得知这一喜讯后，我直奔桃瑞丝那里。我咧嘴笑着拥抱了她，把一张写有洛琳的名字和电话号码的纸条交给了她。她再次抓住我，兴奋地紧紧拥抱着我。这是多么珍贵啊。她急忙表明要我拿电话机给她。我以最快的速度把电话机交给了她。在她拨电话之前，我说我最好还是离开这里，让她单独跟朋友通电话。

She protested mildly but I could see she really didn’t mind. She was too excited. Asking me stay until the call went through though, I agreed. So we exchanged a warm and loving hug goodbye before I dialled Lorraine’s number for her. My heart was beating fast with excitement.

> 她稍微表示不必离开，可我看得出她真的不介意我离开。她那时太兴奋了。她请求我不忙离开等电话接通了再走，我同意了。在我帮她拨电话之前，我们互相温馨且热烈她再次拥抱对方。我也因兴奋而心跳加快。

As she held the receiver, Doris’s face lit up in joy at the sound of her friend’s voice. While Doris’s own voice was old and I knew Lorraine’s was too, the spirit in that phone call was instantly like two young women. They were laughing in no time and chatting non-stop. I tidied the room up a bit, pottering around, unable to extract myself from this incredible happiness. Eventually though I did leave.

> 她紧握着电话，听到她朋友的声音，桃瑞丝的脸色因高兴而立刻变得光彩照人。桃瑞丝这边是一位老妇人的声音，我知道电话那头的洛琳也是相同的音调，然而她们俩的通话气氛立刻变得像两个年轻女人一样叽叽喳喳。在电话中他们笑起来，讲个不停。我整理了一下房间，被这令人难以置信的幸福所感染，我无法控制住自己，在屋内悠然地来回走动。最后我决定离开。

At the door, I waved quietly goodbye to Doris, who has radiant. She stopped talking for a moment, asking Lorraine to wait and said to me, “Thank you, Sweetheart. Thank you”. I nodded while smiling so much my face ached. Walking down the corridor I could still hear Doris laughing until the door had closed properly. The smile didn’t leave my face all the way home.

> 在门口，我轻轻地向容光焕发的桃瑞丝挥挥手以示再见。她立刻停止了讲话，并请洛琳等她一下，然后对我说：“谢谢你，亲爱的，谢谢你。“我点点头，脸上挂满笑容。走进走廊里我依然可以听得见桃瑞丝的笑声，直到大门在我身后关闭那幸福的笑声才消失。我在回家的路上，脸上一直带着微笑。

It was a glorious day and a swim was beckoning. The high stayed with me while I enjoyed the water flowing around me, as I dipped and swam for a couple of hours. At home, just after sunset, I received a phone call from Rebecca, the lovely staff member I had met the night I worked there and had first met Doris. Dear Doris had passed away later that afternoon in her sleep.

> 这是极其令人愉快的一天。我回到家，正好太阳落山，我接到了养老院同事的电话。她告诉我下午傍晚的时候，桃瑞丝在睡梦中去世了。

Tears of sadness fell instantly, but there was joy too. After all, she had died happy, the dear lady. It is amazing how a little bit of time can change a person’s life. When I think about the lonely woman I met that first night to the person who I hugged goodbye to on her last day, no amount of money could substitute the satisfaction this gave me.

> 悲伤的眼泪不停地从我眼眶里流出，但那也是喜悦的泪水。毕竟这位可爱的女人是带着幸福离去的。真是令人惊讶，怎么可能一个瞬间就改变一个人的生命。我不停地追思着我与桃瑞丝所经历的每段时光，从我在那里上班的第一个晚上遇见孤独的她到她最后一天我给她的告别拥抱，再多的金钱都换不来桃瑞丝给予我的那种满足感。

There are thousands of beautiful, but very lonely people in nursing homes all over the world. There are also many young people whose lives are now confined to nursing homes. Young or old though, a couple of hours a week of a new friendship can make all of the difference to these people and to their final chapter. Of course, keeping people out of nursing homes in the first place is preferred, though sadly, not always possible. There are many people in homes who shouldn’t be there; people just disposed of, in a way. It is awful to witness.

> 全世界有成千上万善良且非常孤独的人住在养老院。现在许多年轻人也因种种原因住进了养老院。无论是年轻人还是老年人，对他们而言，尤其是在他们生命的最后阶段，每周几个小时的新的友情可以让他们的生活质量变得完全不同。

A bit of time given though has the potential to change these people’s lives immensely. For me, the timing of Doris’s departure was perfect. Simply, it was her time to go and she had been happy. We had played the role we were meant to in each other’s lives and for that, I will always be grateful. She was a darling woman. Lorraine and I met soon after. The phone call between them had gone on for ages, she said.

> 对我而言，桃瑞丝离开人世的时间点是完美的。很简单，她已经得到了快乐，她该离去的时间已经来临。我们来到这个世界，各自所担当的角色必须对彼此的生活产生影响，对此，我将永远充满感激之心。不久以后，我跟洛琳见了面，她是一位和蔼可亲的老人。她说，她与桃瑞丝之间的那次通话情景很长时间之后她才慢慢放下。

Both had parted with great happiness. We sat under trees at a café, talking happily about Doris and life in general, until it was time to drive Lorraine home. It was lovely to have been able to meet her friend. Certainly, it had been beautiful to know Doris too. And hopefully of course, our dear friend was able to meet up with her other friends when she reached the other side.

> 双方结束谈话时都感到拥有了莫大的幸福。理所当然，我也希望，我们亲爱的朋友桃瑞丝在到达另外一个世界里能够遇见她的其他朋友。
